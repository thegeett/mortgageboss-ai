## Design

**The server sends `waiting_on_when_sent` with each condition** (`ConditionPublic`). This is who the
condition WILL be Waiting on once its next email, or its question, is marked sent.
- It is computed by `condition_drafts.waiting_on_when_sent(next_step, items)`, next to `WAITING_ON` and
  `_wait_on_first_asked`, which is the move it predicts.
- A question to the underwriter waits on the lender.
- Otherwise the condition waits on the recipient of its first open ask, in item order. Open means
  neither dropped nor done. The recipient comes from `recipient_key`, so the LO relay (0132 waits on
  the broker, "LO" while waiting) and the appraiser-through-the-lender route are the same code the
  drafts use.
- It is `None` when nothing will be sent: her task, or an item already in the file.
- `recipient_for` and `recipient_key` now take an `AskShape` protocol (option, performer, performers),
  so the rule reads the public rows the payload already carries, without a second query. The schema
  stays pure: the value is computed in `api/conditions._condition_public`, the one place the derived
  fields are supplied.

**The client renders it.** `becomes()` words the server's owner (`waitingLabel`: the broker is "LO")
and still says WHICH email moves it, because that is wording, not the rule. It still returns nothing
for a condition past To do, a display-only step, or her task (which is "Ready to send").

**Deleted:**
- the client's `WAITING_ON` performer-to-owner map;
- its drift check: the LP-942 appraiser assertion that `becomes` said "Waiting on Lender". The
  appraiser's email grouping stays tested; who it waits on is now a server test.

## Review (of `8a9def53`)

**No defect — and I want to be exact about that, because my first reading said otherwise and I checked
before reporting. One finding, narrower than what I first had: the ticket claims one statement of the
rule, the code has two predicates that coincide, and the test is blind to the difference.**

### What I first found, and why it is not a bug

`waiting_on_when_sent` selects the first item whose **status** is in `_OPEN_ASK`.
`_wait_on_first_asked` selects the first item whose **option** is an ask **and whose draft has been sent**.
Two predicates. They give different answers — measured on 0132, whose items are `disclosure` (LO),
`attorney` (LO), `wire_instructions` (attorney):

```
prediction                                 -> broker
mark ONLY the title/attorney email sent    -> waiting_on = title
```

I nearly reported that. Then I checked what the screen actually says: `becomes()` pairs the server's value
with `askRecipients(condition)[0]`, so the sentence names a **specific** email — *"Becomes Waiting on
Broker when the LO email is marked sent."* Marking **that** email sent gives `broker`:

```
after marking the LO email sent — the one the sentence names — waiting_on = broker   AGREE
```

So the sentence is true for the email it names, and my first probe falsified a proposition it never made.
Not a defect.

### The finding — the equivalence is real, narrow, and untested

What survives is about the claim rather than the behaviour. The ticket says *"the ONE statement of the
rule"*; there are two, and they agree only on the input that matters — the named email. That coincidence
is load-bearing, and nothing records or tests it.

**And the test cannot see it.** `test_the_prediction_is_the_move` marks **every** draft sent, which is
exactly the configuration where "first open ask" and "first ask whose email has gone" always select the
same item. So the one test meant to prove the two are the same rule is blind to the only way they differ,
and would not notice them drifting apart.

Two ways to close it:

1. **Make it true**: have the prediction call the move's rule with "this draft treated as sent". Then there
   is one statement, and the test's claim is a tautology rather than a coincidence.
2. **Keep two and pin the boundary**: with two unsent drafts, mark the **named** one sent and assert the
   predicted owner. That is the sentence's own claim, and it is the only place it could fail.

### What is right

- **The direction is right, and it closes an open item I raised.** Deleting the client's `WAITING_ON` map
  removes the cross-language pair from LP-942 — two statements of one rule in two languages, which had
  already drifted once (the appraiser). The duplication is now same-language, which is precisely why I
  could measure it.
- `recipient_key` reads `condition_plan.recipient_for`, so "which email an ask goes into" stays one
  function across the prediction, the drafts and the re-ask. That part **is** unified.
- The `AskShape` protocol lets the rule run over the `ConditionItemPublic` rows already on the payload,
  so the prediction needs no second query.
- The positive control in the test (6637 borrower, 0132 broker, 6178 lender) is three different recipients,
  not one repeated — which is what makes it a control rather than a shape check.

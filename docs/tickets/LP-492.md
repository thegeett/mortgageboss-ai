## The rates, and what they actually measure

| rule | rate | cases | disagreements | verdict spread |

| PR-4 | 1.0000 | 2 | 0 | `{needs_review: 2}` |

⚠️ **These are the weakest rates in any cohort so far, and the bars say so.** Two things compound:
**n=2** — two appraisals is the entire corpus — **and every spread is single-valued.** A judgment rule
collapses any value into `needs_review`, so two *different* answers would still agree at the verdict
level (LP-491's finding). **The rate shows the pipeline is stable. It says nothing about whether the
judgment is right.**

**The three pre-rate checks all ran:**
1. **Context printed** — every field non-redacted and non-empty (`condition_rating` len 2 → "C4"/"C3").
   CR-8's `[redacted]` trap did not recur.
2. **Calls confirmed** — no `judgment_ai_failed` lines. LP-491's expired-token artifact did not recur.
3. **Spread reported** — and it is the caveat above.

## Assumptions and decisions

1. **`input_resolves` is the LP-485 development-mode meaning.**
2. **PR-2 and PR-7 fire; the judgment rules cannot.** PR-2's and PR-7's directions are argued in their
   specs — a value shortfall and a wrong address are unambiguous facts with definite consequences,
   unlike the name differences that led IH-2 and TI-1 to `needs_review`.
3. **No allow-list of eligible property types was invented** (tier U). PR-3 surfaces an unplaceable type
   instead of comparing against a fabricated list.
4. **Freddie's stricter C5 standard is not branched** — the agency axis is not a fact on any file
   (LP-501).
5. **UAD 2.6 → 3.6 (Nov 2026) is a live risk**, noted on PR-5's spec: both real appraisals are 2.6-era
   ("9/2011"), and the closed C1–C6 vocabulary abstains rather than misreading a new surface form.

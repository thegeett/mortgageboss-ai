**How far this goes:** this was one removal, of one domain at one provider. I did not measure why the

The check's own last good answer was 44 minutes old at removal, and it still saw the removal at its

The delay we got is set by the hourly job, not by DNS: up to an hour to pause, and up to an hour to
come back.

## Recommendation

**Open 1 (2-day delay): no fix before the pilot.** The measured delay was 16 minutes, and it came from
the hourly schedule. A short TTL on each customer zone's NS record was the proposed fix, and there is
no measured delay left for it to shorten. Skip it unless a real case shows otherwise.

**Open 10 (the check can't see what the customer typed): no code before the pilot, but the blind spot
is real.** With 3 of 4 names the check said `ok`. The same reasoning covers a later edit that leaves
at least one of our names, though that was not tried. Email still works in that state, with less
backup if servers go down, and nothing warns us.

The smallest fix is one line in the onboarding call (step 4 of the runbook below): ask the customer's
DNS server directly and see all 4 names before moving on. It costs no code, and with one pilot customer
on a call it covers the setup case. It does not catch a later edit. If that matters once there are
more customers, the code fix is to have the check ask the parent zone directly. Not built.

## Onboarding runbook

For the pilot customer, on the call. Run commands as in LP-1005 "Staging command".

1. Before the call: confirm who runs their DNS, and that the company exists in our system.
2. Run `set_up_mail_domain <company> mb-mail.<their domain>`. It prints one NS record with 4 names.
3. The customer adds that record at their DNS provider: name `mb-mail`, all 4 names.
4. Check what they typed: `dig +short NS <their domain>` for their DNS server, then `dig +norec +noall +authority +answer NS mb-mail.<their domain> @<their DNS server>`. It must list all 4. Don't use `dig @1.1.1.1` for this: it showed 4 when the customer had entered 1.
5. Run the command again. It moves to `verifying`.
6. Wait a few minutes (2m42s here) and run it again. It moves to `active`.
7. Send a test email from one of their files to a mailbox we own. In "Show original", check SPF, DKIM and DMARC pass, and DKIM `d=mb-mail.<their domain>`.
8. Tell them not to change that record. If it's removed, Send disappears within about an hour, with no reason shown on screen, and returns within about an hour of putting it back.

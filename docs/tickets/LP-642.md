# LP-642 — the rental-income documentation rules SEL-2026-08 introduced

- **Status:** Step 1 done (gate wording + the 1007/1025 need). Steps 2-4 need the domain expert.
- **Date:** 2026-09-05
- **Trigger:** Fannie Mae announcement **SEL-2026-08**, dated 02 September 2026
- **Found via:** LF-ZE9N, whose DTI gates for want of a document nothing on the file asks for

## What this is about

Two things, and they are the same thing from opposite ends: **where the subject's gross rent may
come from**, and **what the file must hold before that source is usable**. Neither is modelled.

The income-vs-offset defect the same announcement creates is **bug-012** and is urgent; this is the
larger, slower half.

## 1. Form 1007 / 1025 is mandatory, and we never ask for it

> When the subject property will generate rental income and it is used for qualifying purposes, one
> of the following Fannie Mae forms must be used.

**Form 1007** for one unit, **Form 1025** for two-to-four. Not preferred — required. This settles a
question the LF-ZE9N investigation left open: that file's gate is **correct**, the rent genuinely is
not on the file, and the missing thing is a comparable rent schedule.

What is wrong is everything around the gate:

- Neither form has a catalog type or an extractor. `activation_bars.yaml:677` already records this —
  *"1007 / 1025, the market-rent sources B3-3.8-01 names, have NO catalog type and NO extractor, so
  that path is unmodellable rather than…"* — written as a limitation, now a blocker.
- Nothing puts either form on the **Need List**. A processor sees a blanked DTI and no request to
  send. That is the "explains nothing" shape LP-634 was about, on the page a processor opens first.
- The unit count decides which form to ask for. We do capture it
  (`mismo.facts.property.financed_unit_count`), so the need can name the right one — but a rule must
  read it before it can, and an absent count must not silently pick 1007.

## 2. The subject's rent is looked for in exactly one place, and it is the wrong one

`rental_treatment._subject_gross_rent` requires exactly one `stated_owned_properties` row with
`is_subject IS TRUE`. MISMO exports do not repeat the subject in the owned-property schedule — our
own parser says so (`mismo/parser.py:490`): *a file where nothing is marked subject means "the
schedule lists other properties", NOT "this loan has no subject property"*. So the lookup finds
nothing on every MISMO-imported investment file.

**A caution against the obvious fix.** "Fall back to a lease" is too loose. The guide permits a
lease only in named scenarios, and the 1007/1025 is required *regardless* — a lease sits alongside
the form, not instead of it. Where the two disagree:

> Any variance between the documented rental income and the rental amount reflected in the lease
> agreement must be adequately explained by the borrower or property management company. The lender
> must retain all documentation to evidence the explanation.

B3-3.8-02 puts it beyond doubt (verified verbatim, 2026-09-05) — the conjunction is **and**, not
*or*:

> The lender must obtain the following: a *Single-Family Comparable Rent Schedule* (Form 1007) or
> *Small Residential Income Property Appraisal Report* (Form 1025), as applicable, **and** a copy of
> the fully executed lease agreement(s) when a lease is being transferred to the borrower.

Not "take the lower", not "take the form". That is a **condition on the file**, not a calculation —
which makes it a needs/findings concern, not a calculator one.

## 2b. Short-term rental is a separate topic we have not looked at

Verified 2026-09-05: **B3-3.8-03, Rental Income from the Subject Property: Short-Term Rental**
(09/02/2026) exists as its own topic alongside B3-3.8-02. Neither this ticket's first draft nor the
announcement summary mentioned it, and nothing in the engine distinguishes a short-term rental from
a conventional one — so a subject let on a nightly basis is currently run through B3-3.8-02's rules
whether or not those are the rules that apply to it.

Unscoped deliberately: someone has to read B3-3.8-03 and say what differs before it can be
estimated. Recorded here so it is not discovered later as a surprise.

## 3. Lease eligibility now has teeth

A lease is permitted where it transfers on purchase; the borrower bought during or after the last
filing; rental interruptions kept income off the return; the property was placed in service in the
current calendar year; or the lender documents another situation with justification in the file.

Exclusions and conditions, none of them modelled:

- Not where the investment property is a **departing residence**.
- Not for investment properties **purchased within 45 days** of the subject.
- Not with an **interested party or family member**.
- A **newly executed** lease (dated within two months of application) on a property not yet on the
  1040 needs a **minimum six-month term**, with the first payment due on or before the subject
  mortgage's first payment date.
- Evidence the terms took effect: two consecutive months of bank statements or electronic
  transfers; or the security deposit plus first full month's rent with proof of deposit; or a
  property-management agreement with proof of two months' rents.

Extraction status: `lease_start_date` / `lease_end_date` give the term; `landlord_or_lessor_name` and
`tenant_or_lessee_name` give the parties. Interested-party and family relationships are not
determinable from a lease alone. The 45-day rule needs a purchase date we may not hold.

## 4. Schedule E is the default for a property already owned

A lease is the exception, not the fresher alternative. And the two paths compute differently —
**75% applies to lease and 1007/1025 market rents, never to Schedule E**, which adds back
depreciation, interest, HOA dues, taxes and insurance. See LP-641; the restriction should be
written down before the lookup widens.

## 5. "Housing payment" is now a defined term and must be documented

Defined as the rental payment, PITIA or leasehold payment for mortgaged properties, or property
taxes and leasehold payments for unmortgaged ones — because the usable rental income is restricted
by whether the borrower has one. Where it is not already a credit-report liability the lender must
document it: direct verification of rent from a management company, six months of bank statements,
six months of cancelled cheques, or the latest property tax bill.

We read the borrower's own housing from the 1003's PRESENT expenses
(`borrower_present_housing`) and require nothing further. On LF-ZE9N that is a stated $1,136/month
with no documentation requirement attached.

## Questions for the domain expert

The announcement answered the first round. These are what building would raise next:

1. **Interested party / family member** — is there a practical test a processor applies, or is it a
   judgement call recorded on the file? This decides whether it is a rule or a condition.
2. **The 45-day rule** — 45 days from what, exactly: contract date, closing date, or acquisition
   date on the REO schedule?
3. **"Placed in service in the current calendar year"** — established by what document?
4. **The variance explanation** — when a 1007 and a lease disagree and the explanation is thin, is
   there a working default, or does the file simply not proceed?

## Suggested sequencing

The pieces are separable and get more useful in this order:

1. **Say the right thing, and ask for the right document.** Correct the gate wording and seed the
   1007/1025 need by unit count. No new extraction; fixes what a processor sees on LF-ZE9N today.
   (The wording half is scoped in **LP-643**.)
2. **Catalog + extractor for 1007 / 1025.** Unblocks the mandatory source.
3. **Widen `_subject_gross_rent`** across the permitted sources, in guide order, with the 75%
   restriction respected per path.
4. **Lease eligibility and the housing-payment documentation rules** — the largest piece, and the
   one most dependent on the answers above.


## Step 1 as built (2026-09-05)

**The gate names the document now, not the empty field.** It read *"the application states no GROSS
monthly rent for the subject"*, sending a processor to the 1003 for a number MISMO never puts there.
It now reads: *"no document on the file states what the subject will rent for — a comparable rent
schedule (Form 1007 for one unit, Form 1025 for two-to-four) or a lease for the subject establishes
it, and a net figure cannot substitute for gross."* Four tests moved from asserting the word `GROSS`
to asserting the message names a document, which is the property that matters.

**The forms exist as document types.** `comparable_rent_schedule` (1007) and
`small_residential_income_appraisal` (1025), both Tier 2 — classified, categorised, summarised, no
extractor. That is what a type needs to be REQUESTABLE and FILEABLE; reading the market rent off the
form is step 2 and has its own accuracy question. Cataloguing at Tier 1 would claim an extractor that
does not exist.

**A floor need seeds them, chosen by unit count.** An investment subject now gets a rent-schedule
need: 1007 at one unit, 1025 at two-to-four. **An absent unit count asks for neither** — a wrong form
is a wasted round-trip with the borrower, and the gate keeps saying why until the count is known.

### What this cost, and the coupling worth knowing

Adding two catalog types broke 24 tests. The catalog is also the source of truth for the
*classifier's* type list, and a parity test asserts every catalog type has a recognition indicator —
so a type cannot be added without describing how to recognise it. That is the design working: the
alternative is a classifier that can never return a type the system knows about.

The indicators are written around what is PRINTED on each form (the form number, the comparables
grid, the per-unit rents) rather than around "market rent", which would have pulled in leases and
rental-income letters.

### Still open in step 1's own scope

Nothing seeds the need from the DTI gate itself — the gate lives in the calculator, and needs are
seeded from the floor or from findings. The floor rule fires on **investment occupancy**, which is
broader than "the DTI actually gated": a file with a lease for the subject would still be asked for
a rent schedule. That is correct under B3-3.8-02 (the form is mandatory regardless), but it is a
coincidence rather than a design — if the mandatory-regardless reading ever softens, this rule asks
for a document the file does not need.

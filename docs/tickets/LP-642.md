# LP-642 — the rental-income documentation rules SEL-2026-08 introduced

- **Status:** Steps 1-3 done — an investment file with a rent schedule now computes. Only step 4 (lease eligibility) remains, and it needs the domain expert.
- **Date:** 2026-09-05
- **Trigger:** Fannie Mae announcement **SEL-2026-08**, dated 02 September 2026
- **Found via:** LF-ZE9N, whose DTI gates for want of a document nothing on the file asks for

## What this is about

Two things, and they are the same thing from opposite ends: **where the subject's gross rent may
come from**, and **what the file must hold before that source is usable**. Neither is modelled.

The income-vs-offset defect the same announcement creates is **bug-012** and is urgent; this is the
larger, slower half.

## 1. Form 1007 / 1025 is mandatory, and we never ask for it

> When the subject property will generate rental income and it is used for qualifying purposes, one
> of the following Fannie Mae forms must be used.

**Form 1007** for one unit, **Form 1025** for two-to-four. Not preferred — required. This settles a
question the LF-ZE9N investigation left open: that file's gate is **correct**, the rent genuinely is
not on the file, and the missing thing is a comparable rent schedule.

What is wrong is everything around the gate:

- Neither form has a catalog type or an extractor. `activation_bars.yaml:677` already records this —
  *"1007 / 1025, the market-rent sources B3-3.8-01 names, have NO catalog type and NO extractor, so
  that path is unmodellable rather than…"* — written as a limitation, now a blocker.
- Nothing puts either form on the **Need List**. A processor sees a blanked DTI and no request to
  send. That is the "explains nothing" shape LP-634 was about, on the page a processor opens first.
- The unit count decides which form to ask for. We do capture it
  (`mismo.facts.property.financed_unit_count`), so the need can name the right one — but a rule must
  read it before it can, and an absent count must not silently pick 1007.

## 2. The subject's rent is looked for in exactly one place, and it is the wrong one

`rental_treatment._subject_gross_rent` requires exactly one `stated_owned_properties` row with
`is_subject IS TRUE`. MISMO exports do not repeat the subject in the owned-property schedule — our
own parser says so (`mismo/parser.py:490`): *a file where nothing is marked subject means "the
schedule lists other properties", NOT "this loan has no subject property"*. So the lookup finds
nothing on every MISMO-imported investment file.

**A caution against the obvious fix.** "Fall back to a lease" is too loose. The guide permits a
lease only in named scenarios, and the 1007/1025 is required *regardless* — a lease sits alongside
the form, not instead of it. Where the two disagree:

> Any variance between the documented rental income and the rental amount reflected in the lease
> agreement must be adequately explained by the borrower or property management company. The lender
> must retain all documentation to evidence the explanation.

B3-3.8-02 puts it beyond doubt (verified verbatim, 2026-09-05) — the conjunction is **and**, not
*or*:

> The lender must obtain the following: a *Single-Family Comparable Rent Schedule* (Form 1007) or
> *Small Residential Income Property Appraisal Report* (Form 1025), as applicable, **and** a copy of
> the fully executed lease agreement(s) when a lease is being transferred to the borrower.

Not "take the lower", not "take the form". That is a **condition on the file**, not a calculation —
which makes it a needs/findings concern, not a calculator one.

## 2b. Short-term rental is a separate topic we have not looked at

Verified 2026-09-05: **B3-3.8-03, Rental Income from the Subject Property: Short-Term Rental**
(09/02/2026) exists as its own topic alongside B3-3.8-02. Neither this ticket's first draft nor the
announcement summary mentioned it, and nothing in the engine distinguishes a short-term rental from
a conventional one — so a subject let on a nightly basis is currently run through B3-3.8-02's rules
whether or not those are the rules that apply to it.

Unscoped deliberately: someone has to read B3-3.8-03 and say what differs before it can be
estimated. Recorded here so it is not discovered later as a surprise.

## 3. Lease eligibility now has teeth

A lease is permitted where it transfers on purchase; the borrower bought during or after the last
filing; rental interruptions kept income off the return; the property was placed in service in the
current calendar year; or the lender documents another situation with justification in the file.

Exclusions and conditions, none of them modelled:

- Not where the investment property is a **departing residence**.
- Not for investment properties **purchased within 45 days** of the subject.
- Not with an **interested party or family member**.
- A **newly executed** lease (dated within two months of application) on a property not yet on the
  1040 needs a **minimum six-month term**, with the first payment due on or before the subject
  mortgage's first payment date.
- Evidence the terms took effect: two consecutive months of bank statements or electronic
  transfers; or the security deposit plus first full month's rent with proof of deposit; or a
  property-management agreement with proof of two months' rents.

Extraction status: `lease_start_date` / `lease_end_date` give the term; `landlord_or_lessor_name` and
`tenant_or_lessee_name` give the parties. Interested-party and family relationships are not
determinable from a lease alone. The 45-day rule needs a purchase date we may not hold.

## 4. Schedule E is the default for a property already owned

A lease is the exception, not the fresher alternative. And the two paths compute differently —
**75% applies to lease and 1007/1025 market rents, never to Schedule E**, which adds back
depreciation, interest, HOA dues, taxes and insurance. See LP-641; the restriction should be
written down before the lookup widens.

## 5. "Housing payment" is now a defined term and must be documented

Defined as the rental payment, PITIA or leasehold payment for mortgaged properties, or property
taxes and leasehold payments for unmortgaged ones — because the usable rental income is restricted
by whether the borrower has one. Where it is not already a credit-report liability the lender must
document it: direct verification of rent from a management company, six months of bank statements,
six months of cancelled cheques, or the latest property tax bill.

We read the borrower's own housing from the 1003's PRESENT expenses
(`borrower_present_housing`) and require nothing further. On LF-ZE9N that is a stated $1,136/month
with no documentation requirement attached.

## Questions for the domain expert — researched 2026-09-05

Read against the live guide before asking. **One is answered outright, three are not — and the guide
being SILENT is itself the finding**, because it means these cannot be resolved by reading harder.
They are lender-policy calls, and whatever is decided has to be written down somewhere other than a
rule's comment.

### 4. A 1007 and a lease disagree → ANSWERED, and there is a working default

B3-3.8-02, verbatim (the purchase branch; the refinance branch reads the same with Schedule E added):

> If the current market rents do not reasonably support the gross rents reported on the lease
> agreement, the lender must: determine if additional documentation is necessary to support income
> stability, provide a written analysis explaining the discrepancy, and to justify their
> determination that the rental income used in qualifying the borrower is stable and reasonably
> expected to continue; **or use the lesser amount.**

So it is an **or**, not a fallback: "use the lesser amount" is a route the lender may simply take.
That answers what the earlier draft called open — the file does not stall, and there is a defensible
default.

**What this means for us.** Take the LESSER as the computed figure, and treat the written analysis as
the route to using the higher one. That is the conservative direction and it needs no judgement call
to compute. Whether we ever offer the higher path is a product question: it requires capturing a
processor's written analysis and attaching it to the file, which is a condition/audit feature, not a
calculator one.

### 1. "Interested party or family member" → NOT DEFINED for this purpose

B3-3.8-01 states the exclusion and defines neither term.

The guide does define interested party elsewhere — **B3-4.1-02, Interested Party Contributions** —
as someone with "a financial interest in, or [who] can influence terms and the sale or transfer of
the subject property", naming the seller, builder, real-estate agent and their affiliates. **Do not
import it.** That definition is scoped to who may contribute to closing costs; the rental exclusion
is about who may be a *tenant or property manager*, which is a different relationship and a different
risk. Borrowing it would be exactly the cross-context reasoning this ticket has already had to
correct once.

Still a question for the domain expert, now with the guide's silence established rather than assumed.

### 2. The 45-day rule → THE GUIDE DOES NOT SAY WHAT THE 45 DAYS RUN FROM

B3-3.8-01 says a lease may not be used "for investment properties purchased within 45 days of the
subject property" and names no reference date. Contract date, closing date, and the REO schedule's
acquisition date are all plausible and produce different answers.

Unbuildable until someone decides. A rule that picked one silently would be a threshold invented
rather than cited — the thing ADR-361 forbids.

### 3. "Placed in service in the current calendar year" → NO DOCUMENT NAMED

Neither topic says what establishes it. Same status as (2): a real gap, not a reading failure.

## Suggested sequencing

The pieces are separable and get more useful in this order:

1. **Say the right thing, and ask for the right document.** Correct the gate wording and seed the
   1007/1025 need by unit count. No new extraction; fixes what a processor sees on LF-ZE9N today.
   (The wording half is scoped in **LP-643**.)
2. **Catalog + extractor for 1007 / 1025.** Unblocks the mandatory source.
3. **Widen `_subject_gross_rent`** across the permitted sources, in guide order, with the 75%
   restriction respected per path.
4. **Lease eligibility and the housing-payment documentation rules** — the largest piece, and the
   one most dependent on the answers above.


## Step 1 as built (2026-09-05)

**The gate names the document now, not the empty field.** It read *"the application states no GROSS
monthly rent for the subject"*, sending a processor to the 1003 for a number MISMO never puts there.
It now reads: *"no document on the file states what the subject will rent for — a comparable rent
schedule (Form 1007 for one unit, Form 1025 for two-to-four) or a lease for the subject establishes
it, and a net figure cannot substitute for gross."* Four tests moved from asserting the word `GROSS`
to asserting the message names a document, which is the property that matters.

**The forms exist as document types.** `comparable_rent_schedule` (1007) and
`small_residential_income_appraisal` (1025), both Tier 2 — classified, categorised, summarised, no
extractor. That is what a type needs to be REQUESTABLE and FILEABLE; reading the market rent off the
form is step 2 and has its own accuracy question. Cataloguing at Tier 1 would claim an extractor that
does not exist.

**A floor need seeds them, chosen by unit count.** An investment subject now gets a rent-schedule
need: 1007 at one unit, 1025 at two-to-four. **An absent unit count asks for neither** — a wrong form
is a wasted round-trip with the borrower, and the gate keeps saying why until the count is known.

### What this cost, and the coupling worth knowing

Adding two catalog types broke 24 tests. The catalog is also the source of truth for the
*classifier's* type list, and a parity test asserts every catalog type has a recognition indicator —
so a type cannot be added without describing how to recognise it. That is the design working: the
alternative is a classifier that can never return a type the system knows about.

The indicators are written around what is PRINTED on each form (the form number, the comparables
grid, the per-unit rents) rather than around "market rent", which would have pulled in leases and
rental-income letters.

### Seeding on occupancy is correct, not coincidental

An earlier draft hedged that seeding on **investment occupancy** — rather than on the DTI actually
gating — was "a coincidence rather than a design", since a file with a subject lease would still be
asked for a rent schedule. The hedge is withdrawn. B3-3.8-02 requires Form 1007/1025 **and**, where
a lease transfers to the borrower, the lease — with the parenthetical *"(This must be supported by
Form 1007 or Form 1025.)"* The form supports the lease; the lease never substitutes for the form. So
a file with a subject lease genuinely does still need the form, and occupancy is the right trigger
for the reason the guide gives.

### One dependency worth knowing before someone edits copy

An absent unit count asks for **neither** form. That is deliberate — a wrong form is a wasted
round-trip with the borrower — but the reasoning holds only while the GATE keeps saying why. If that
message ever stops naming the missing unit count, the silence here stops being deliberate and becomes
an unexplained absence. Two files, one dependency, and nothing enforces it.

## Review (2026-09-05)

**(a) The mandatory-regardless reading is correct, and it is not a coincidence.** Verified against
B3-3.8-02 (Rental Income from the Subject Property, 09/02/2026). The purchase requirement is Form
1007 or 1025 **and** "a copy of the fully executed lease agreement(s) when a lease is being
transferred to the borrower", carrying the parenthetical:

> (This must be supported by Form 1007 or Form 1025.)

So a lease does not substitute for the form — it is the form that supports the lease, not the
reverse. Seeding the need on OCCUPANCY rather than on the gate is therefore right for the reason the
guide gives, and the ticket's hedge that it is "a coincidence rather than a design" can be dropped: a
file with a subject lease still needs the form.

**(c) Tier 2 satisfies a need, and it is now pinned.** Nothing in `needs_engine` reads a tier at all —
satisfaction is `needs_type == document_type` — so a Tier 2 type with no extractor clears a need like
any other. That was true by ABSENCE rather than by design, which is exactly the kind of thing that
holds until someone adds a tier filter for an unrelated reason. `test_rent_schedule_need_lp642.py`
asserts both halves: the types exist in the catalog, and uploading each one actually closes the need
seeded for it. Mutation-checked against a plausible wrong slug.

Without that, this ticket would have seeded a need whose own document could never clear it — bug-001
and bug-009, a third time.

**(d) The indicators are sound.** They key on what is printed on each form — the Fannie form number
and its Freddie equivalent (1007/1000, 1025/72), the unit count, the comparables grid, the
operating-income statement — rather than on "market rent". So a lease cannot drift in (no grid, no
appraiser's opinion) and neither can a Form 1004 URAR (different number, different scope). This is
the right axis: form identity is printed on the page, where "market rent" is a phrase several
document types use.

**(b) Silence on an absent unit count — agreed, and worth one addition.** Asking for the wrong form
costs a round-trip with the borrower and teaches a processor to distrust the list. But the reasoning
holds only while the gate keeps saying why, so that dependency is worth naming: if the gate message
ever stops naming the missing count, silence here becomes an unexplained absence rather than a
deliberate one.


## Review round 2 — the layer the check was one step away from

The review added the round-trip nobody had asserted: uploading a rent schedule **closes** the need
seeded for it. The failure shape is well-worn here — satisfaction matches `needs_type ==
document_type`, so a seeded type no document can carry sits on a processor's list forever,
unclearable by the exact upload it asks for (bug-001 and bug-009, twice over). It works today
because `needs_engine` reads no tier **anywhere**, so a Tier 2 type with no extractor satisfies like
any other — true by absence, not by design, and now asserted rather than assumed.

**But both of those tests construct the need directly** (`need.needs_type = slug`) or check a
hardcoded pair against the catalog. Neither runs `seed_floor_needs`, so neither sees what the
**seeder actually emits**. Verified by mutation: renaming the seeder's slug to
`comparable_rent_schedule_typo` leaves both green — which is the exact defect the file exists to
prevent, travelling through the one path it did not cover.

A third test now drives seed → upload → closed, reading the type off the seeded row rather than from
a constant, because a constant is the thing that agrees with itself. It fails on that mutation and
passes on restore.

## Review round 3 — and the discipline is narrower than "test the seeder"

Round 2's fix stopped where round 1's did, one subject over. It selected the seeded rows with
`if n.needs_type in _FORMS`, so the constant re-entered through the filter and the CATALOG assertion
below it could never fail. The mutation still failed the test — via `len(seeded) == 1` on an empty
list — but reported *"an investment subject seeds exactly one rent-schedule need"* when the truth was
*"the seeder emitted a slug no upload can clear"*. Right verdict, wrong story, and the story is what
the next person acts on. Now selected by CATEGORY, and the mutation reports the real fault.

The round also added the assertion that removes the constant entirely: **every** need the floor emits
is reachable, selected by nothing — no filter, no list to remember to extend. `canonical_need_type`
is the repo's own answer to "can a document reach this" and is deliberately wider than `CATALOG`
(it resolves umbrella types, alternative heads, and the bug-001 aliases), returning `None` for a slug
nothing can reach.

**And the most useful finding was a reviewer catching their own green-that-meant-nothing.** That
test's first version claimed in its docstring to catch a `payoff_statement` typo. Run, it did not:
the payoff need is seeded only on the refinance branch and the fixture was an investment purchase.
**A reachability test covers exactly the branches its fixtures fire — so the fixtures are the
coverage**, and a confident claim had been written about coverage that did not exist. It now runs
both loan purposes and asserts the branch-specific need appeared before asserting anything about it,
so a branch that stops firing fails loudly rather than quietly narrowing the test.

Mutation-verified in both directions across three slugs: `payoff_statement`,
`comparable_rent_schedule`, `existing_mortgage_statement`.

## The pattern, stated properly

Four findings in this ticket sat one layer from where the check was pointed: the `how_to_fix` copy,
the seeded-need round trip, the seeder's own literal, and the guard against that literal. The last
one matters most, because the check that was pointed one layer short was itself pointed one layer
short — and only a mutation said so.

So the rule is not "test the seeder". It is **run the mutation you claim to catch**. Every step here
was written by someone who believed the previous step's claim, including the two who wrote the claims
without running them.

## Step 2 as built (2026-09-05) — the forms are readable

One extractor, both forms. A 1007 (one unit) and a 1025 (two-to-four) answer the same question in the
same shape — an appraiser's opinion of monthly market rent supported by a comparables grid — so they
share `comparable_rent_schedule.py` and are registered against both document types. `form_type`
records which was read; `unit_rents` carries the per-unit breakdown a 1025 has and a 1007 does not.

**The field the DTI consumes is `opinion_of_monthly_market_rent`**, and the prompt spends most of its
length defending it. An opinion is a judgement the appraiser signed, so the model is told never to
average the comparables into it, never to copy a comparable's rent, and never to substitute the
subject's CURRENT rent — a tenanted subject shows both, and the actual rent is what a tenant pays
while the opinion is what the market supports. A blank opinion line must read null: *a qualifying
ratio is computed from this figure, so a plausible guess is worse than a null — null shows a
processor what to check, and a wrong number is invisible.*

The comparables grid and unit rents are kept **verbatim as strings**. A rent written "$1,850/mo" and
an adjustment "-100" are what the appraiser wrote, and the DTI consumes the opinion rather than the
grid, so normalising would lose fidelity for nothing.

### The tier moved with the fact

Step 1 catalogued both at **Tier 2** because no extractor existed and Tier 1 would have claimed one
that did not. They are **Tier 1** now, which is a contract this repo enforces in three places, each
of which caught a half-finished version of this change:

- `test_every_registered_extractor_is_a_tier_1_type` — registered but still Tier 2.
- `test_tier_1_iff_the_type_has_a_schema_spec` — Tier 1 with no schema spec.
- the spec validator — `decimal` is not a coercer; the type is `Decimal`.

Two schema specs written (122, 123) and two pinned spec counts updated from 121 to 123. The counts
are deliberate guards against silent additions, so updating them with the reason is their
maintenance rather than an obstacle.

### What is starter-grade

No real 1007 or 1025 was available. The field list comes from the form layout and the guide, and the
accuracy needs validating against real forms — the same caveat every extractor here carries, recorded
in both specs' notes.


## Step 3 as built (2026-09-05) — the lookup reads the mandatory source

`_subject_gross_rent` read exactly one place: the MISMO owned-property schedule's subject row. MISMO
does not repeat the subject there — `mismo/parser.py` says a file where nothing is marked subject
means *"the schedule lists other properties"*, not that the loan has no subject — so on every
MISMO-imported investment file it found nothing and the DTI gated for want of a rent no export was
ever going to put in that field.

It now reads **both** permitted sources: the Form 1007 / 1025 opinion of market rent (the mandatory
one) and the stated schedule row.

**Where they disagree, the lesser wins**, which is the guide's own answer rather than a convention:

> If the current market rents do not reasonably support the gross rents reported on the lease
> agreement, the lender must: determine if additional documentation is necessary … provide a written
> analysis explaining the discrepancy … **or use the lesser amount.**

An `or`, so taking the lesser is a route the lender may simply take — no judgement, no stalled file.
The written-analysis branch needs a processor's document attached to the file, which a calculator
cannot supply, so this implements the conservative half of a rule the guide states in full.

Only `opinion_of_monthly_market_rent` is read. A 1025's `total_gross_monthly_income` is an all-units
total and is not interchangeable with it; the extractor keeps them apart and so does the lookup. A
non-positive opinion is treated as absent, not as a rent of zero — zeroing would compute a net of
−PITIA and carry the whole payment as an obligation, which is a different wrong answer rather than an
answer.

### LF-ZE9N now computes

It is an investment **purchase**: the borrower does not own the subject yet, so the owned-property
schedule has no row for it and never will. With the rent schedule on the file, the ratio computes.

### What is deliberately still missing

**The lease path.** B3-3.8-02 requires the form AND, where a lease transfers to the borrower, the
lease. Lease *eligibility* — the interested-party test, the 45-day rule, evidence the terms took
effect — is step 4, and three of those are silences in the guide. Reading a lease's rent before they
are settled would use a source we cannot establish is permitted.

Verified by mutation: `min` → `max` fails two tests, and dropping the rent-schedule source fails
three.


## Review round 2 — the real forms, and what the correction did not reach

The review obtained the actual blank forms (Freddie 1000 / Fannie 1007 (8/88); Fannie 1025 / Freddie
72 (03/2005)) and found the prompt asked for a label **neither form prints**. The field list survived;
the instruction for finding the one figure that matters did not.

**The 1025 has no property-level opinion at all.** "Opinion Of Market Rent" is a per-UNIT column
heading over rows 1-4, and the property-level figure is the printed "Total Gross Monthly Rent". Under
the old instruction the likeliest reading was one unit's row — so a four-unit subject would have
qualified on **a quarter of its rent**, computing cleanly the whole way. The 1007 states its figure in
a foot-of-page sentence, and the "Final Reconciliation of Market Rent" line above it is a comments
line that usually holds prose.

Four more printed neighbours were added to the prohibitions, each of which passed every rule the
first version wrote: `Indicated Monthly Market Rent` (per comparable), `Adjusted Monthly Rent`,
`Total Estimated Monthly Income` (rent **plus** parking/laundry), `Total Actual Monthly Rent`. Plus
the joint branding, since a model reading "Freddie Mac Form 1000" cannot know it holds a 1007.

### What the correction had not reached

**The schema specs still carried the old definitions**, and had become self-contradictory:
`total_gross_monthly_income` was described as "total across all units", which is precisely what
`opinion_of_monthly_market_rent` now holds on a 1025 — two fields documented as the same number while
the prompt treats them as different. Both specs now match the forms, and record that the forms read
were **blank**: this is the printed field vocabulary, not what an appraiser writes in the boxes.

### A guard that would have survived the removal of what it guards

The prompt is the only defence for a figure a qualifying ratio rests on, and no test can measure
whether a model obeys it — so the guard asserts each confusable neighbour is still NAMED. The first
version asserted the labels appeared *anywhere in the file*, and several also appear in the
descriptive half. Verified by mutation: deleting the per-unit prohibition entirely left it green. It
now parses the `NEVER` clauses and asserts within those, and fails on that deletion.

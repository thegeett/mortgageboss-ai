## §1 — The documents are already displayed. AS-8 and AS-10 have none to display.

**This is not a UI gap, and that matters because the obvious fix would change nothing.**

`SourceDocuments` is a real, rendered component — `components/file/verification/rule-finding-row.tsx:140`,
and `finding-card.tsx` renders it too. It prints "Document:" / "Documents:" and links each filename.
A finding that carries provenance already shows it today.

Measured on staging, across all live rule-engine findings:

| provenance | findings | distinct rules |
|---|---|---|
| populated | 1,014 | 37 |
| **EMPTY** | **449** | **57** |

AS-8 and AS-10 are in the empty set. On both LF-ZE9N and LF-JR4T, all four findings carry
`source_document_ids = null`.

### Why they are empty — the subject is the loan, and the loan is not a document

`_attach_document_provenance` (`verification_run.py:879`) has exactly two sources, and its own
docstring names the case that fails:

> 1. What the RULE already carried… Never overwritten.
> 2. The SUBJECT's own document. A `per_document` rule's subject IS the document; a deposit's and a
>    tradeline's subject is nested inside one…
>
> "A subject with no document in the map — **a borrower, the loan, a MISMO-stated liability** — gets
> NOTHING, and the finding says nothing."

AS-8 and AS-10 are `subject_enumeration: loan`. AS-1 and AS-12 are `per_deposit`, nested inside a
statement, and their provenance works — which is why the file shows documents on some asset findings
and not these.

### And the tag producer has already discarded the answer

This is the part that decides where the fix goes. The finding's own `load_bearing_tags` on LF-JR4T:

```json
AS-8:  {"tag_id": "stmt.continuity", "value": "broken",
        "reasoning": "the bank of america na ****4679 account's statements do not chain — an ending
                      balance of 11338.28 does not carry into the next statement's opening balance
                      of 3041.19",
        "source_facts": ["loan"]}

AS-10: {"tag_id": "stmt.min_account_months", "value": "1",
        "reasoning": "the account with the fewest statements has 1 distinct month(s)",
        "source_facts": ["loan"]}
```

`source_facts: ["loan"]`. The recipe compared two specific statements — it knows the account, both
balances and which side of the break each is on, and says so in prose — and records the loan as its
source. **The document identity is lost in the producer, one layer below the finding.** Nothing
downstream can reconstruct it, which is exactly what `_attach_document_provenance` says about path 1:
"nothing here can reconstruct that".

So the work is in the `stmt.*` derived recipes: carry the content_ids of the statements each tag was
computed from, and let path 1 pick them up. `_attach_document_provenance` already prefers what the
rule carried and never overwrites it, so no change is needed there.

### The user's read of AS-8 vs AS-10 is right, and they are still two rules

Both declare `requires_documents: [bank_statement]`, so at the request level they ask for the same
thing. They are different questions:

- **AS-8** — *statement chaining*: each statement's ending balance must carry into the next one's
  opening balance. Fires on statements that ARE present and disagree.
- **AS-10** — *statement recency completeness*: the account with the fewest months is compared to the
  requirement.

On LF-JR4T both fired on the same account, which is why they read as duplicates: one month of
statements produces both "not enough months" and "nothing to chain to". **Deciding whether to
consolidate them is out of scope here** — it is a rule-design question for the domain expert, and
§1's provenance fix makes the overlap legible either way, because both findings would then name the
same statement.

### The general rule the user asked for

> *"we should always display documents in each finding if the finding derived from such documents."*

The display side already honours this. The gap is the 449 findings across 57 rules with nothing to
show. AS-8 and AS-10 are the two the user hit; **the survey of the other 55 is part of this ticket**,
because fixing two rules and leaving the class is the shape this repo keeps repeating
(`an-asymmetry-is-a-class-not-an-instance`).

A finding with genuinely no document must keep showing nothing — the docstring's reason stands:

> "Attributing it to a document it did not come from would send a processor to the wrong page with
> the system's confidence behind it."

### BUILT: a derived recipe may now name what it read

`Recipe` accepts an optional third element — the content_ids the tag was computed from — and
`produce_derived_tags` prefers it over the subject. 77 of 79 recipes are untouched and keep their
subject, which is honest for them: a tag over a computed figure has no document to point at.

- **`stmt.continuity`** carries the PAIR that disagrees, in order, and only the first break — the
  message names one break, so naming more would point at statements it says nothing about.
- **`stmt.min_account_months`** carries the SHORTEST account's statements, not every statement on the
  file. The tag reports one number about one account.

Asserted at `produce_derived_tags`, not at the recipe. The recipe returning the ids proves nothing:
`produce_derived_tags` is the function that was overwriting them with `(subject_id,)`, and a test
stopping at the recipe would have passed against the shipped defect.

### SURVEY: the class, and why it is bigger than two rules

Provenance on staging: **1,014 findings across 37 rules have it; 449 across 57 do not.** By subject
enumeration, across the 78 active rules:

| subject_enumeration | rules | can the subject name a document? |
|---|---|---|
| `loan` | **28** | **no** — the subject is the string "loan" |
| `per_document` | 22 | yes, the subject IS the document |
| `per_borrower` | 19 | no — a borrower is not a document |
| `per_liability` | 5 | sometimes — a stable list row maps to the document declaring it |
| `per_deposit` | 4 | yes, nested inside a statement |

**The 28 loan-subject rules are the class.** `_attach_document_provenance`'s two paths are "what the
rule carried" and "the subject's own document", and a loan subject has neither — so every one of
them renders with no documents unless its producer names them, which is what this section built for
two of the 28.

`AS-10, AS-4, AS-8, CL-1, CO-1, CO-3, CO-4, CR-13, DT-7, ID-6, IH-3, IH-7, IH-9, IN-2, …`

**Not built for the other 26, deliberately.** A wrong answer is worse than none — a finding pointing
at a document it did not come from sends a processor to the wrong page with the system's confidence
behind it.

### What answer each of the 26 needs — FOUR shapes, not 26 questions

Measured, by inspecting each producer for whether it holds a document content_id at all:

**A. MECHANICAL — the recipe already iterates `documents.entries` (9 rules).**
`CO-1, CO-3, CO-4, IH-3, IH-7, IH-9, IN-3, IN-4, PC-3`

Identical to AS-8: the ids are in hand, the only work is deciding WHICH of the documents it looked at
the finding is about. Cheapest group, and the one to do first.

**B. ONE STEP AWAY — the producer selects a document's tag but does not say which (3–4 rules).**
`PC-2, PC-7, CR-13, PR-6` (the last two through `_age_in_months_at_closing`'s `pick` policy)

These read `by_subject` tags — whose keys ARE content_ids — or run an explicit date-selection policy
that already identifies one document. The work is making the selector return the subject it chose
rather than only the value.

**C. GENUINELY NO DOCUMENT — computed or MISMO-stated (about 11 rules).**
`AS-4, CL-1, ID-6, IN-2, MI-1, MI-4, PE-1, PE-3, PR-2` and `occupancy.stated` for `OC-1, OC-2`

Reserves against PITIA, an LTV, a UFMIP percentage, a conforming-limit test, a MISMO-stated
occupancy. **The correct answer here is to stay empty**, and LP-617's rule already says why:
attributing a finding to a document it did not come from is worse than saying nothing. This group
needs a DECISION recorded, not code.

**D. AI-PRODUCED — NOT a quick shared fix. Corrected after measuring (DT-7, OC-3, OC-1 / OC-2).**

`produce_ai_group_tags` does set `source_facts=(subject_id,)`, the same line `produce_derived_tags`
carried, and an AI group does build its context from documents — so the mechanism looked identical
to §1's and was first written up here as one change covering four rules.

**Measuring it says otherwise.** All three loan-level AI groups (`occupancy`, `atr_documentation`,
`occupancy_rental`) declare `applies_to: all` and take the default `document_cap` of **60**. So the
model is sent every document on the file — 44 of them on LF-ZE9N — and "the documents this judgement
was made from" is honestly *all of them*.

A finding listing 44 documents is not provenance; it is the same failure as naming the wrong one,
reached by naming everything. The `SourceDocuments` component renders a list a processor scans.

So group D has two real options and neither is small:

* **Record it as group C.** A whole-file AI judgement has no single source, and empty stays the
  honest answer. Costs nothing, closes the question.
* **Ask the model which documents drove the judgement.** This is the only route to a true answer,
  and it is a PROMPT CHANGE — a quality risk needing the calibration harness, the same constraint
  LP-644 §5 carries. Not a mechanical fix.

`parsed.py:48` has the same line, and the same question applies to it separately.

So the 26 are: **9 mechanical (A), 3–4 small (B), ~11 that should stay empty by design (C), and 4
that need a decision or a prompt change (D)** — with A and B the only groups that are
straightforward code.

## §2 — Nothing stops a verification and a document from racing

Confirmed in both directions, and neither has messaging.

| guard | today |
|---|---|
| Run button while documents upload/extract | `disabled={running}` only — `running` is the VERIFICATION's own state. Document processing is not consulted. |
| Upload while a verification runs | `disabled: upload.isPending` only — the dropzone's own upload. The verification's state is not consulted. |

`verification-panel.tsx:247` and `document-dropzone.tsx:78`.

**Why this is worth building rather than tolerating.** A verification builds a frozen snapshot from
the file as it stands. Started while an extraction is in flight, the snapshot is built from a document
whose fields are not yet written — the run then reports `couldnt_check` for facts that are seconds
away from existing, and those findings persist under the reconcile identity until the next run.

The repo already has a NEIGHBOURING mechanism that is not this one: `loan_files.verification_stale`
marks "inputs changed since the last run" and is surfaced to the UI (`verification.py:464`). Staleness
is about the past; this is about what is in flight right now. **Do not extend `verification_stale` to
carry it** — a flag meaning two things is how the gate reasons in LP-643 ended up joined.

### ANSWERED: the backend refused neither, and the two directions are NOT the same defect

The API already refuses a second concurrent VERIFICATION (LP-629, the findings-index collision). It
had no view of document processing at all, in either direction.

**Direction A — Run while a document is being read — is harmful.** `build_documents_section` selects
the file's current documents with **no status filter**, so a document mid-extraction is frozen into
the snapshot with an empty `fields` map and, before classification lands, no `document_type`. Every
rule needing a typed field from it abstains on the document-type predicate, and those findings
PERSIST under the reconcile identity until a later run retires them. LP-640 measured that shape
costing 22 queue rows from one unidentified document.

**Direction B — upload while a run is in flight — is not.** `_document_count`'s own comment records
the design: "a document arriving mid-run does not extend the window it is already inside; it is
picked up by the NEXT run." The snapshot froze at enqueue and the upload calls
`mark_verification_stale`, so nothing is corrupted. What remains is a confusion: a run completes
minutes later without the document just added, and nothing on the upload screen said it would not be
included.

Built as asked in both directions. **The cost of B is stated rather than hidden:** a processor who
receives a document mid-run waits for the run rather than parking it immediately. That is a real
cost for a confusion rather than a corruption, and it is the one part of §2 worth revisiting if it
proves annoying in use.

### Which statuses count as in-flight

`PENDING`, `CLASSIFYING`, `CLASSIFIED`, `EXTRACTING`. Measured on staging rather than read off the
enum: only `completed` (199) and `needs_review` (16) persist across 215 documents, so the rest are
transient.

**`FAILED` is deliberately NOT in-flight.** A failed document is finished — it will never gain
fields, so treating it as in-flight would block that file's verification forever. It is a reason to
re-process (LP-637's button), not a reason to refuse a run.

**AND THE WINDOW IS BOUNDED BY AGE, for the same reason (review).** That argument is not about the
FAILED label — it is about any document that will never reach a terminal status, and one WEDGED in
`extracting` is exactly that without saying so.

It happens. Three documents on staging have sat in `extracting` since **2026-08-23** — a fortnight.
They are harmless only because they were soft-deleted, which `only_active` already excludes; the
review's conclusion that they would block LF-AWBB was wrong for that reason, and its argument was
right anyway. Measured: `would_block = 0` under the guard's real predicate, `extracting_any = 3`.

`document_processing`'s docstring says a document is "never left stuck in CLASSIFYING / EXTRACTING",
and the load-bearing word is "every **HANDLED** path". A SIGKILL at the hard limit, or an OOM taking
the worker (the risk LP-629 raised worker memory for), is not handled. There is no sweep.

The bound is `DOCUMENT_HARD_LIMIT_SECONDS x 5`, read against `updated_at` — which moves on every
status transition and is therefore a progress heartbeat. **Not a percentile:** `updated_at -
created_at` across completed documents has a median of 108s and a p99 of 3.4 DAYS, because that
column is bumped by later edits. The statistic measures "time until last touched", not time to
process, and a window built on it would have been three days wide.

### Built (`<this commit>`)

- `_documents_in_flight()` — ONE helper, read by the run guard AND by the status response, so a
  refused run and a disabled button cannot disagree.
- `POST /verification/run` returns **409** with the count in the message. Not gated on `force`:
  `force` bypasses the input-fingerprint cache, which is an answer to "the inputs look unchanged",
  not permission to read a half-written file.
- `documents_processing` on the status response — deliberately a separate field from `stale`, which
  means the inputs changed in the PAST. A flag meaning both is how LP-643's joined gate reason
  happened.
- The Run button disables and names the count, with singular grammar. It does NOT stack the message
  onto an already-running verification: one disabled control, one reason.
- The dropzone holds uploads while a run is in flight and says when it comes back.

### Messaging

The user asked for "proper messaging" explicitly. A disabled control with no reason reads as broken —
the same standard LP-643 set for the ungate dialog. Each direction needs to say which thing is
running and that the control returns when it finishes.

## §3 — An edit abandoned without Enter is discarded silently

The first version of this section read "already works", from the report's own wording. The user
clarified: *"if user edit value and without enter or clicking correct sign if they move then value
does not get updated."* That is a silent data-loss path, not a missing feature.

Enter DOES save (`dti-calculator.tsx:412`, `calculator-card.tsx:250`). The defect is what happens
when the processor does anything else.

### Measured behaviour, both editors

Neither has an `onBlur` handler at all, so the loss is narrower than it feels — and where it happens,
it is total and silent:

| action while editing | result |
|---|---|
| click elsewhere on the page | editor STAYS OPEN, draft intact |
| click another row's edit icon | `editingKey` moves → **draft discarded, no signal** |
| leave the page / unmount | **draft discarded, no signal** |
| return to that row later | `setDraft(item.amount)` — even a surviving draft would be clobbered |

Both editors are the same shape: a single `editingKey` at the parent, `draft` local to the row, reset
on entry. `dti-calculator.tsx:90/360/443` and `calculator-card.tsx:98/224/281`. **Fix both**; fixing
one is the asymmetry-as-instance failure this repo keeps repeating.

### Why it goes unnoticed — the part that decides the fix

An unsaved edit and a never-started edit RENDER IDENTICALLY. Nothing distinguishes "I typed this and
lost it" from "I have not got to this yet", so the processor's own memory is the only record that a
number was entered.

Worked example, from a finding really on LF-JR4T — IH-3, *"Confirm which homeowners insurance policy
is in force, as the file contains two binders with conflicting effective dates"*:

1. Processor decides which binder is live, clicks the pencil on Homeowners insurance, types the premium.
2. Without pressing Enter, clicks the pencil on Property taxes — natural, both came from the same
   document set.
3. The insurance draft is gone.
4. Taxes save on Enter and render in override styling; insurance renders in ordinary auto styling,
   indistinguishable from untouched.
5. If insurance was a fail-closed unknown, the DTI stays GATED — the processor sees a gated ratio
   immediately after believing they supplied the missing input, with nothing connecting the two.

### DECIDED: do not auto-save on blur

The user offered both options ("either user know that this is not saved, by default we save the
change"). Auto-save is rejected, for reasons specific to this field rather than general caution:

1. **An override is an audited claim, not a draft.** It writes a `dti_overridden` activity row with a
   note field. Auto-save logs a deliberate act the processor did not take, under their name.
2. **Blur fires mid-typing.** Clicking away while `1` is typed toward `150` persists `$1.00`.
3. **An override CLEARS A FAIL-CLOSED GATE.** `dti.py`: "an override on the line clears the gate (a
   processor-supplied figure is trusted)". So a stray auto-save turns a deliberately-refused
   calculation into a confident ratio resting on a typo — the LP-375 failure, through a new door.
4. **It makes Cancel ambiguous.** If blur saves, the X stops meaning "discard" and starts meaning
   "undo what was just written", which is a different and worse interaction.

Auto-save would be defensible on a field that only affects display. This one feeds a qualifying ratio
and an audit trail.

### Built (`d3d688a`, `b564252`)

1. Drafts moved to the parent, keyed by field — a row switch is a PAUSE, not a discard.
2. A dirty row renders `unsaved — press Enter or ✓ to apply $X`, FIRST in the caption chain.
3. Escape and X stay the only discards.
4. `beforeunload` warns — PARTIAL, and labelled so in the code: it catches a tab close or reload, not
   Next's client-side navigation. The caption is the primary signal.

### THREE components, not two — and the miss is the lesson

`dti-calculator`, `calculator-card` AND `ltv-calculator` all held the same shape. The first pass
fixed two, and the second was only found because writing its test exposed a half-fix in the first.
Peer review then found the third.

The enumeration that finds all three is one command — `grep -rl "editingKey" components/` — run
BEFORE fixing anything, not after fixing two. The first pass enumerated the files already open. Two
is not evidence a class has two members; it is evidence you looked at two. Recorded here because the
commit that fixed the second one NAMED this pattern while committing an instance of it.

### The caption order is guarded in all three

`unsaved` must beat `overridden`, or a second edit to an already-overridden row reads as saved while
holding an unapplied figure. Only a comment defended that, in every component. The fixture that
distinguishes the orderings is an already-overridden row carrying a pending edit.

### A note on verifying this kind of fix

The first attempt at the caption-order mutation reordered the JSX, produced a syntax error, and
vitest printed `Tests  no tests`. **The edit landed, so "confirm the edit landed" passes — the run
simply never happened, and `no tests` reads almost exactly like a clean run.** The tell is the count:
`1 failed | 13 passed` proves a mutation landed and was caught; `no tests` proves nothing while
looking like success. A mutation that breaks the file it mutates is worth less than no mutation,
because it comes with a false positive attached. Prefer a one-token semantic change over
restructuring.

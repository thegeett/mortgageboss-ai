## ⚠️ Where the repo diverged from the ticket

The ticket said the workbook is the generator's source and the repo wins. Four divergences:

1. **AS-2 does not share PC-5's tags.** The ticket says `contract.emd_amount` and `contract.emd_sourced`
   are "used by PC-5, **AS-2**". **AS-2 reads neither** — it gates on `txn.has_identified_source` and
   `txn.apparent_category` on the *transaction* subject. They answer the same underwriting question from
   opposite ends. (This makes the "AS-2 verdict unmoved" check trivially true rather than meaningful —
   AS-2 shares no input with anything built here.)
2. **`contract.emd_amount` was already declared** (parsed, `purchase_agreement`), from an earlier ticket.
3. **`contract.personal_property`'s vocabulary row is malformed**: `allowed_values` reads
   `["yes", "no + list"]`. `"no + list"` is not a value — the workbook meant "yes/no, plus a list".
4. **Two fields the ticket did not mention exist and matter**: `parties_relationship_disclosed` (the
   arm's-length signal) and `personal_property_value`.

## ⚠️ PC-1 — dropped, for two independent reasons

**The ticket asked whether `title.parties_match` exists.** It does — in `fact_tags.csv`, entity `doc`,
`yes|no|unknown`, AI-produced, described *"Title parties match borrowers/seller (fuzzy)"*. It is **not
declared**, so it produces nothing today. But existence was not the blocker:

**1. It duplicates a live matcher.** `title.parties_match` asks the same question **TI-1 already answers**
with `title.vested_owner_matches` — built at LP-491, deterministic, live, and proven against the four real
commitments. Declaring it would put a **second matcher on one comparison**, which is exactly what LP-483
forbade for CR-1/CR-4 and the reason those two share one producer.

**2. Its other input has no data.** `contract.arms_length`'s only schema field,
`parties_relationship_disclosed`, is **0 of 5** on the real contracts. That is TI-3/4/5's shape and
ADR-354's point: a field existing is not a field populating.

Either alone would block it. **Both tags stay undeclared, and a test pins that** — if someone declares one
against an invented source, it fails. ⚠️ The ticket asked that `contract.arms_length` be built once for
FR-2's definition rather than shaped to PC-1. **It was not built at all**: building an AI tag whose only
source field never populates is the ADR-354 trap, and FR-2 would inherit it. FR-2 should decide what
evidence it actually has.

## ⚠️ PC-5 — built, and held on the ticket's own rule

PC-5 is the **rare testable cross-source rule**: LP-480 found most two-source combinations exist on no
file at all, and LF-6T3N carries both a purchase agreement and bank statements. The derivation ran there:

```
contract.emd_sourced   cases=2   disagreements=0   rate=1.0000   spread={unknown: 2}
```

**Calls succeeded** (no `ai_call_failed`), **context non-redacted** — and **every derivation returned the
same abstain value.** Pre-rate check 2 says: *"refuse to record a rate when every derivation returned the
same failure or abstain value."* This is the CR-8 shape — perfectly consistent and carrying no
information — so **no rate was recorded and PC-5 stays held.**

⚠️ **Why the model abstained is the next question, not a defect.** `unknown` is what the prompt asks for
when the statements do not cover the account the deposit left, and LF-6T3N's may genuinely not.
Distinguishing "correctly abstained" from "could not see the input" needs a file where the trace is
**known** to exist — which the corpus may not contain.

⚠️ **A second deposit is lost, and that is an input gap.** Doc 183 understated a **$204k additional**
earnest money distinct from the primary figure. `earnest_money_amount` is **singular** and **no
`additional_earnest_money_amount` field exists** in the schema, so a contract with an initial and an
additional deposit contributes only the first. The prompt asks the model to name a second in its
reasoning; the tag cannot carry it. → LP-499.

⚠️ **No "customary for the area" threshold was invented.** B3-4.3-09 says large deposits and those
exceeding what is customary "should be closely evaluated" and gives **no number**. A fabricated percentage
would fire on ordinary files.

## PC-8 — activated, and the strongest evidence in any cohort so far

```
contract.personal_property   cases=5   disagreements=0   rate=1.0000   spread={unknown: 2, yes: 3}
```

⚠️ **A genuinely two-valued spread** — unlike LP-492's three rules, which were single-valued and therefore
showed only that the pipeline was stable. Here the model agreed with itself on **different answers**,
which is what a consistency rate is supposed to measure. **It is still 5 cases**, and still not evidence of
correctness (a systematically wrong tag scores 1.0).

**All three pre-rate checks passed:** context printed and non-redacted, calls confirmed, spread reported.

⚠️ **PC-8 has no firing path**, pinned by test. The catalog rationale is *"Surface included personal
property — judgment"*, and a judgmental rule has exactly two exits — `needs_review` and `couldnt_check`.
It tells a processor what the contract includes; a human decides whether it is material.

⚠️ **It computes no deduction.** Non-realty items are deducted from the sales price for LTV under the
sales-concessions topic (tier S — the IPC page, whose per-cell table was not fetched). PC-8 carries no
number and applies none.

⚠️ **The field is free text, not the `yes|no` the workbook declares**: "None", "Gas Logs in fireplace…",
"Refrigerator, Washer/Dryer". The fixture/personal-property line is the whole judgment, and it is
genuinely hard — a refrigerator is usually personal property, a built-in oven is a fixture, gas logs
normally are. `personal_property_value` fills **1 of 5**, and that one is `"0"`.

## Proven vs assumed

| rule | proven on real data | assumed |

⚠️ **n=5, and free-text contracts are the least reliable document class in the corpus** — of ~8
purchase-agreement claims in the bench, one was real; the free reader projected Texas TREC fields onto a
North Carolina form. **These rates establish that the rules run and abstain correctly. They establish
nothing about accuracy**, and contract wording varies by state form more than any bureau field does.

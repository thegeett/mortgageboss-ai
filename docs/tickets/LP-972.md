# LP-972 — Call log and reminders

- **Ticket:** LP-972 (the owner, 2026-10-08). Series LP-967 to LP-973; the overview is in `LP-967.md`.
- **Status:** built (the owner, 2026-10-09: "start LP-972"). The full backend suite is still open; see "Open".

## What it adds

- **"Call or talk to someone":** who, when, and what was agreed, recorded on the condition.
- **"Wait for something":** from whom and by when, with a reminder if it has not arrived. It builds on
  the reminders that exist (`reminder_snoozes`).

## Done when

- Both are steps she can add.
- The reminder fires on the date.
- Peer review.

## What was built

### Call or talk to someone: a record, not a task

**Why not a task.** A task she marks done counts toward the condition being ready (`ready_because`), and
then the condition goes in the package. A call that ended "he'll send it next week" finishes nothing.

**So** `condition_plan.log_call` writes a `CONDITION_CALL_LOGGED` event to the condition's history: who
(a performer), the day, and what was agreed, in her words, up to 300 characters. The plan's tasks are
untouched. Tested: no task is added and the condition does not move.

**Her words are the second free-text field the history projects** (`ConditionEventPublic.call_agreed`),
with its own trace in the schema, next to `withdrawal_reason`:
- **who writes it:** she does, about her own call;
- **where it travels:** the same two company-scoped event routes;
- **where it is stored:** in `detail`, which the readonly view drops;
- **its guard:** the kind. The same keys on any other event do not travel, and a test checks that.

**On screen:**
- the history line reads "Talked to Title / escrow on 10/09: “They will send the final CD on Monday”";
- the step in "Add a step" asks who, when (today unless she changes it), and what was agreed;
- a toast says where it went, because the plan itself does not change.

**The door:** `POST /conditions/{id}/calls`. It refuses "herself", and refuses empty words.

### Wait for something: a task that waits, with a reminder on its date

**The task.** `add_item(waiting=True, due_date=…)`, through the existing `POST /conditions/{id}/items`.
- It is her task (`i_will_do_it`), so no email goes: she asked some other way. The flag is
  `condition_items.waiting`.
- It needs a date and someone to wait on.
- It stays open, so it holds the condition until the thing comes.

**On the path:**
- "Title / escrow sends the payoff letter", with "If it hasn't come by 10/15, it shows on your Reminders
  page.";
- then upload or link it, then our check.

**Elsewhere on screen:** "Route:" says "Wait for Title / escrow". "Your first moves" lists the person under
"Then we wait on", and does not count the wait as a task she does now.

**The reminder** (`reminders._waits_due`, `ReminderKind.CONDITION_WAIT_DUE`):
- **When it shows:** on the date, in Eastern time, on the Reminders page, while the wait is still open on
  an open condition.
- **The evening before:** already the date in UTC, and correctly too early. Tested.
- **When it goes away:** once the thing has come (RECEIVED) or she marked it done.
- **Snoozes:** a snooze is about this date. Moving the step's date brings the reminder back for the new
  one, because `since` is the date.
- **An ordinary ask** with a due date raises no wait reminder (the control).

**"Fires on the date", honestly:** the product has no notification channel (`reminders.py` says so; there
is no beat). So "fires" means it appears on the Reminders page from that date. It does not email her or
push to her. That would be a ticket of its own.

**Migration `169e48fff378`:** `condition_items.waiting` (not null, default false), and the
`condition_events.kind` and `reminder_snoozes.kind` CHECK swaps (raw SQL, ADR-037). The downgrade removes
rows that use the new values before narrowing. Up, down and up again, clean, locally.

**Neither new block is offered to "Describe it".** `PlanBlock` stays LP-969's seven: a call and a wait each
need a date she picks, and neither should come from a sentence without her choosing the day.

## Review (peer session, 2026-10-10)

Reviewed at `16b01eb3`. A call as a record rather than a task is the right call, and the reminder's
Eastern day and `since`-keyed snooze are right. Five findings, all fixed; the six questions are answered
below, four of them with measurements.

### 1. A wait counted as one of her tasks, in two counts (fixed)

The ticket's own reasoning is that a wait is "not a task now" — which is why its first moves name who is
waited on. The same reading was left in both places that COUNT her tasks, because a wait is stored as
`i_will_do_it`:

- `conditions.has_open_step((I_WILL_DO_IT,))`, which is the dashboard's "Your tasks N" and, by its own
  docstring, the filter behind its click: "ONE PREDICATE FOR THE FILTER AND THE COUNT".
- `condition_plan.round_plan_summary`'s `your_tasks` pill on the plan panel.

Measured: 7086 carries only borrower asks, so it is in neither count; one wait on the title company took
the file's figure from 2 to 3 and the panel's from 2 to 3. She is told she has a third thing to do, and
the thing is "wait for the title company". Both now exclude a wait, each mutated separately.

### 2. The row called a wait her task while the path called it someone else's (fixed)

`next-step.ts`'s action line — the one-liner on every row — read "Your task · the payoff letter" for a
wait, while the plan path for the same condition said "Title / escrow sends the payoff letter". Two
statements about one condition on one screen. The line now reads "Waiting on Title / escrow · the payoff
letter" with the quiet `waits` icon, using the path's own `askedWho` so the wording is shared rather than
restated.

### 3. "Add N tasks to your list" promised her someone else's work (fixed)

The confirm summary counted a wait among the tasks Confirm would add. Excluded rather than re-worded,
because what that line says is what Confirm will do.

### 4. One click turned a wait into an ask and left it a wait (fixed)

`ItemLinkActions` offers "Ask someone" on every `i_will_do_it` item, and a wait is one. Clicking it moved
the item to `ask_borrower` while `waiting` stayed set: an item in a draft AND carrying a wait reminder,
which is neither of the two things it can be. `update_item` now clears the flag when the option leaves
`i_will_do_it`, and the test checks the reminder goes with it. The invariant is on the server rather than
in the menu because deciding to email after all is a real thing to do, and the API reaches it either way.

### 5. The reminder spelled "an open condition" differently from everywhere else (fixed)

The builder's third question, and the answer is that it mattered. `conditions.is_open` is `not info_only
and lender_status in OPEN_LENDER_STATUSES`, and its docstring says why it is one function: "a dozen
`count(*)` queries would each be a chance to disagree with the row about what open means". The wait
reminder's SQL spelled the second half only, so a wait on an INFORMATION LINE — a row that asks for
nothing — nudged her on its date while every count on the screen called that condition closed.

Fixed by writing the rule once: `conditions.is_open_sql()` sits beside `is_open` and the reminder uses
it. Dropping the `info_only` half again fails the new test.

### 6. A rollback would have deleted her logged calls (fixed)

The builder's fifth question. Narrowing `condition_events.kind` means no row may hold the new value, so
the rows have to go before the CHECK does — and a `condition_call_logged` event is the record of a
conversation: who, when, and up to 300 characters of her own words about what was agreed, held nowhere
else. The downgrade deleted them.

It now counts them and refuses, naming the SQL that accepts the loss. A database where no call has been
logged still rolls back on its own, which is most rollbacks since they follow a deploy. The SNOOZES still
go: a snooze is derived state about a date and its reminder returns by itself. Tested by loading the
migration module and swapping its `op`, both branches, as LP-975's backfill test does.

### The questions

1. **Should a call make a condition "worked" for LP-973's Read again?** No, and the measurement says why
   rather than the reasoning: `clear_for_reading` replaces the reading, the items and the step and never
   touches events, so the call survives a re-read with its day and her words. If a call made a condition
   ineligible, one phone call would take "Read again" away from a condition whose reading has no type and
   one generic task. Pinned by `test_read_again_leaves_a_logged_call_alone`.
2. **Readers keyed on "i_will_do_it means hers".** Findings 1-4. Of the five you listed I confirmed three
   (the panel pill, the confirm summary, the Ask-someone menu) and found two more you did not: the
   dashboard count and filter, and the row's own action line. `handle_own_way` and `revert` drop or
   replay an item wholesale and do not read the option as ownership, so both are fine.
3. **The open-condition rule.** Finding 5.
4. **`reminders` importing `condition_plan._RECIPIENT`.** Fixed: `recipient_label(performer)` is public
   now, with the fallback in one place instead of at the call site.
5. **The downgrade.** Finding 6.
6. **The call's date in her time zone against the reminder's Eastern.** Measured, and there is nothing to
   fix: only the CALL block defaults its date (`today: true`, documented as "in her own time zone"), and
   her clock is the right one for a conversation she was in. The WAIT's date is never defaulted
   (`today: false`), so the Eastern reminder never inherits a browser clock. It is the same principle as
   `VerdictRequest.source_date`, which is deliberately never defaulted because a verdict is the LENDER's
   date — a call is hers, so hers is the clock that should fill it in.

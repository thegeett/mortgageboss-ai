# LP-977 — A wait moves the condition to "Waiting on …"

- **Ticket:** LP-977 (the owner, 2026-10-10: "write the ticket and build it", after approving the mock).
- **Source:** the LP-972 review's last note: a wait does not move `prep_status`, so "Waiting on others"
  leaves the condition out while its own plan says it waits on someone. The agreed design is the "Plan
  screen explained" artifact, board "Wait for something — the status follows".
- **Status:** built. One owner question is open; see "Open".

## What it does

Adding a "Wait for something" step (LP-972) moves the condition from **To do** to **Waiting on {who}**, the
way marking an email sent already does (`condition_drafts._wait_on_first_asked`).

- **Forward only, from To do.** A condition at any other status is left alone, so a status she picked is
  never overridden.
- **Who it names: the same rule as a sent email.** The wait's performer maps to its email recipient
  (`condition_plan._RECIPIENT`), then to `WAITING_ON`:
  - Borrower is Borrower;
  - Title and the attorney are Title, since they share one email;
  - the LO is Broker;
  - Insurance is Insurance;
  - the lender and the appraiser are Lender;
  - the HOA, an employer or another party is "someone".

  One mapping, not a second copy. The mock first said the attorney reads "someone", and is corrected to
  match.
- **Two waits:** the first moves it, and a later one does not move it again (it is no longer at To do).
- **Not needed after all:** the status stays where it is, and she moves it herself, as today.
- **When it arrives:** unchanged. Linking the document, with the checks passing and every task done, moves
  Waiting to Ready (`condition_evidence`). That already works today.
- **The history** reads "Moved to Waiting on Title (a wait step)". The move's event carries
  `by: "wait"`, and the history projects that as a boolean for `CONDITION_PREP_MOVED` only.

## Done when

- **Adding a wait** at To do moves the condition to Waiting with the mapped owner, and writes one
  `CONDITION_PREP_MOVED` event with `by: "wait"`.
- **Not moved:** a condition at any other status, and a second wait.
- **The mapping** is the email's: a test per class (Title, attorney to Title, LO to Broker, HOA to
  someone).
- **The history line** names the wait.
- **The dashboard:** "Waiting on others" counts the condition.
- Mutation pass; peer review.

## What was built

- **`condition_plan._wait_moves_status`,** called from `add_item` when `waiting=True`:
  - only from To do, otherwise nothing;
  - the owner comes from `_RECIPIENT`, then `condition_drafts.WAITING_ON`, the sent email's rule;
  - one `CONDITION_PREP_MOVED` with `by: "wait"`.
- **`ConditionEventPublic.moved_by_wait`** is a boolean, for that kind and `by` only; it is on the events
  allow-list. The history line: "Moved to Waiting on Title (a wait step)", or "… (a wait step)" for
  someone the status cannot name.
- **The mock's rule is corrected:** the attorney reads Title, the email's rule.

### A regression found before release, and fixed

The peer asked for the four ways a wait stops being one. The first, **marked done by hand**, was broken.
`apply_step_status` moved a condition to Ready only FROM TO_DO. So once the wait put it at Waiting, marking
the wait done left it at Waiting with nothing to wait on. Before this ticket it went To do → Ready.

**The fix** (`_waiting_by_wait`): the plan may also advance a Waiting whose latest `PREP_MOVED` says
`by: "wait"`.

**Why a branch and not a set of statuses.** "Waiting may become Ready" would also advance a Waiting she
picked, and one a sent email made: someone else genuinely being waited on. The property is "the wait
made this Waiting", and `by` is what records it.

**Pinned from three sides:**
- the wait's Waiting advances: `test_marking_the_wait_done_…`, which fails without the fix;
- a Waiting she set does not: `…_she_set_herself_…`;
- a Waiting an email made does not: `…_an_email_made_…`. This one was the peer's ask; it fails if the
  check accepts `email`.

**What the rule rests on** (the peer's census of every writer of `prep_status`): no writer may set WAITING
without writing a `PREP_MOVED` that carries its `by`.
- Today the two writers that set WAITING (the email send and the wait) both do.
- The three that write status without a `PREP_MOVED` (verdict, reopen, came back) all set TO_DO, which
  advances anyway.
- **Ties:** the latest-move lookup orders by `(occurred_at desc, id desc)`, as `_last_move_was_a_send`
  does. Two moves in one transaction tie on time and the id breaks the tie arbitrarily, but no writer
  makes two moves of one condition in one transaction.

### The other three exits

- **Its document received, checks passing:** `condition_evidence` already moves Waiting → Ready.
- **Removed or not needed:** the status stays, as approved in the mock.
- **Turned into an ask** ("Ask someone ▾"): the LP-972 review's `update_item` clears `waiting`. The
  condition stays at Waiting on that person, with an unsent email to them. That is the owner question
  below.

## Review (peer session, 2026-10-10)

Reviewed at `17077e36`. The regression fix is the right shape — `by: "wait"` is the property, not the
status — and reusing the send's own `WAITING_ON` chain rather than restating it is the right instinct.
Two findings, both fixed; the three questions are answered below, all three measured.

### 1. After a wait, no later send could say who we are waiting on (fixed)

A send renames `waiting_on` when the condition is already Waiting on someone ELSE, guarded by
`condition_drafts._last_move_was_a_send` — which asked whether the latest `PREP_MOVED` carried
`by: "email"`. That was the same question as "is this ours to rename?" only while a send and her own
hand were the two things that could leave a condition at Waiting. This ticket makes a third.

Reproduced: a wait on Title moves the condition to Waiting on Title (`by: "wait"`), an ask to the
borrower on the same condition is drafted, confirmed and sent — and `waiting_on` stays TITLE. The row
and the dashboard say we are waiting on the title company while the newest email sits in the borrower's
inbox, and it never corrects itself, because the event history does not change.

The guard now asks whether the latest move carries a `by` AT ALL. Every code-made move has one —
`email`, `wait`, `plan`, `evidence` — and the move `condition_status` writes when she picks a status by
hand has none, so her own choice is still protected. A future `by` then behaves like the others instead
of being silently excluded, which is the trap the enumerated version had just fallen into.

Pinned from both sides, and one side was already there: `test_a_later_send_still_names_who_we_wait_on`
fails with the old `by == "email"`, and the existing `test_a_send_never_overrides_her_own_move` fails if
the guard is widened to always allow — so "a send may rename a machine's move, never hers" is now held
by one test each way.

### 2. The wait reached into a private name in another module (fixed)

`_wait_moves_status` composed the owner itself — `condition_plan._RECIPIENT`, then
`condition_drafts._RECIPIENT_KEY`, then `WAITING_ON` — which meant importing a private name across
modules, the thing the LP-972 review had just taken out of `reminders`. The chain now lives as
`condition_drafts.waiting_owner_for(performer)`, beside the `WAITING_ON` map it ends in. No behaviour
change; the ticket's own 17 tests cover it, and `OwnerHint` dropped out of `condition_plan`'s imports
with it.

### The questions

1. **Does any reader of `prep_status_from` assume `to_do` for `by: "plan"`?** No, and the stronger
   version of the answer is that no reader reads it for this kind at all. The frontend's
   `condition_prep_moved` line uses `prep_status_to`, `draft_recipient`, `moved_by_wait`, `waiting_on`
   and `plan_option`, never `prep_status_from`; `history.ts`'s `ourTrackMoved`, which does read it, is
   called only from the verdict and came-back lines. `condition_compare` reads it only on
   `CONDITION_CAME_BACK`. So `prep_status_from = before` is correct provenance with no visible
   consequence — worth knowing if the intent was for the history to SAY "from Waiting", because it does
   not.
2. **Can two `PREP_MOVED` be written for one condition in one transaction?** No, and for a better reason
   than the one I gave when I endorsed the premise. It is not that nothing happens to do it: it is that
   every mover refuses the status another mover leaves. `apply_step_status` takes To do or a
   wait-made Waiting; `condition_evidence`'s takes To do or Waiting and never calls the plan's;
   `_wait_moves_status` and the send's first branch take To do only; the send's second branch only
   renames an existing Waiting. So whichever runs first, the next one finds a status outside its own
   guard. The ticket's tests already assert exactly one move on both paths (`(moved,) = await _moves(...)`),
   which is the property pinned rather than argued.
3. **`_wait_moves_status` before `_resync_drafts`.** No ordering issue, measured rather than reasoned:
   `condition_drafts` reads `prep_status` in exactly one place, inside the send's own mover, which runs
   from `mark_sent`. `sync_round_drafts` never reads it, so the order of those two calls cannot matter.

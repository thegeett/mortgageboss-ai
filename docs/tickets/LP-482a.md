# LP-482a — cohort re-check: regroup the remaining rules against the catalog

## What this ticket is

LP-481 planned seven credit rules and shipped **zero**. Not because the work was hard, but because the cohort
was assembled from *summaries* rather than from `rule_kinds.csv`: four of the seven rule descriptions were
wrong, only two were `deterministic_only`, and no activation bar existed for any of them.

Every remaining cohort was grouped the same way. This ticket re-derives the grouping for **all 83 non-active
in-scope rules** directly from the catalog, joins the LP-476 census and the activation bars onto it, and sorts
each rule into a buildability bucket **by its hardest blocker**. Read-only, no model calls, **$0**.

**The answer to the question the plan depends on: zero rules are buildable today with no Priya and no new
documents.** The detail below is what to do about that.

2. **The hardness ordering C > D > B > E > A is mine**, stated above; the ticket does not define one. A

3. **Bucket C's 13 lineage-only members are conservatively placed.** Lineage is an upper bound (LP-476); with

5. **Fill rates are LP-476's**, measured within the document type. The n≤2 caveat applies to every credit,

## ⚠️ The LP-481 lesson, recorded once

**Cohorts must be built from `rule_kinds.csv`, never from a summary.** LP-451's verdicts, the census roll-up,
and the ticket backlog all describe what a rule *needs next*; none of them carries the rule's `kind`. Four of
LP-481's seven descriptions were wrong and four of the seven were AI-gated by a `kind` nobody had read.

**And the census's blocker is the FIRST blocker, not the only one.** LP-476 recorded each rule's first broken
hop by design — it says so — and 51 of its rules carry an uncalibrated AI tag *behind* that first hop. Reading
"blocked-on-tag-production" as "needs only a tag" is exactly how a seven-rule cohort became zero. **Bucket a
rule by its hardest blocker, and read the catalog before planning around it.**

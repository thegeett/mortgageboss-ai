  own draft; she gets a task only if 'you' is one of the performers. The item shows where each part

- **Closes:** the product question "an item edited to [processor, borrower] routes as her task and
  nothing says the borrower was not emailed" (LP-942).

## Design

**An item is split into PARTS, one per destination.** Each part is a whole item: its own option, its own
draft or task, and its own evidence. It links to the item it came from through the new
`condition_items.part_of_item_id` (migration `c4a8e2f1b637`; the downgrade refuses while a part exists).
- `destinations(item)` groups an ask's performers by where each is asked: one group per email, and one
  for her.
- `split_item(item)` keeps the first group on the item and returns one part per other group. Each part
  asks its lead (the borrower, or a third party); `route_ask` then makes her own part her task, the one
  rule LP-942 set.
- It is called wherever an item is made or edited: the plan's build, "Add an item", and `update_item`.
  A re-edit re-splits: the open parts from the last split go (soft), and a part already done is kept as
  the record of what arrived.
- The round's draft sync then puts each asked part in its own recipient's draft, with no change to the
  sync itself.

**The LO relays to the borrower (STOP AND ASK 3, reading taken).** Plan §6 and the reviewed Stage 3A
acceptance put 0132's `[borrower, lo]` disclosure in the LO email only, because the LO has the borrower
re-sign it. So `[borrower, lo]` stays one group, and every other combination splits. If the owner wants
the borrower emailed directly too, it is the `relayed` line in `destinations`.

**Where each part went:** `ConditionItemPublic.part_of_item_id`. The sheet shows a part on its parent's
row ("Also: You — your task", "Also: Title / escrow — In title email · draft") rather than as a row of its
own, and "Items · N" counts the parent.

## Review (of `1233372a`)

**The split is right, and I verified its invariants exhaustively rather than on the five cases. One
finding, reproduced end to end: a part whose ask was already SENT is soft-deleted by a re-edit, so the
file stops showing a request that went out.**

### The split invariants hold — every combination, not a sample

I built every 1-, 2- and 3-performer combination against both ask options, ran `split_item` then
`route_ask`, and checked three properties. **Zero violations.**

- no resulting item is a stranded ask (LP-942's rule survives the split);
- she gets a task **iff** the processor is one of the performers — the owner's test, generalised;
- exactly one item per distinct destination, no duplicates.

The merges are right too: `[lender, appraiser]` → one ask (both go to the lender's draft),
`[title, attorney]` → one (they share an email), `[processor, appraiser]` → her task **and** a lender ask.

**The split-before-route ordering is load-bearing, and all three call sites have it.** `build_plan:498`,
`update_item:841` and `add_item:906` all call `split_item` first. Reverse either and the owner's own case
breaks: `route_ask` would convert `[processor, borrower]` to her task, and `destinations` returns a single
group for a non-ask — so the borrower would never be asked. Worth a line in the docstring, because the
order looks incidental and is not.

### Finding — a re-edit deletes an ask that was already sent

`update_item` re-splits by soft-deleting every part that is not DONE:

```python
ConditionItem.part_of_item_id == item.id,
ConditionItem.deleted_at.is_(None),
ConditionItem.status != ConditionItemStatus.DONE,
```

A part whose email has been **sent** is `REQUESTED`, not `DONE`, so it is deleted. Measured:

```
item 'source' [borrower, employer] -> 'source' (borrower) + 'source.employer' (employer)
employer's email marked sent       -> part status=requested, draft_id set
she re-edits to [borrower]         -> part deleted_at set, status still requested
live items now: [('source', ['borrower'])]
-> the employer was emailed, and the file no longer shows that ask
```

**This contradicts the doctrine written in this repo for exactly this case.** `_leave_unsent_drafts`
(LP-940) unlinks items only from **unsent** drafts, because *"A SENT draft is history and keeps its
items."* And `update_item`'s own comment keeps a DONE part *"as the record of what arrived"* — the same
argument applies to a part that was **asked**: it is the record of what was requested. A document arriving
from the employer afterwards cannot link either, since `_takes` refuses a deleted item.

If she genuinely means to take the ask back, that is the withdraw path with a reason (LP-940), not a
silent soft-delete. The guard wants to be "not DONE **and not already sent**" — status not in
(DONE, REQUESTED), or the draft's communication still being a draft.

**No test covers this.** `test_a_re_edit_re_splits` never marks anything sent, so the five new tests are
green on the case that works.

### The `[borrower, lo]` reading (STOP AND ASK 3) — right, and correctly flagged

The owner's sentence says *every* outside performer gets their own draft; keeping `[borrower, lo]` as one
group is a departure from that read literally. Taking it anyway is the right call: plan §6 and the Stage 3A
acceptance decided that specific pair, and a general new sentence should not silently overturn a specific
prior decision. Recording it as a STOP AND ASK rather than deciding quietly is the mechanism working.

### Verified

- **The migration, in the database:** `part_of_item_id` is a nullable `uuid` with
  `FOREIGN KEY … REFERENCES condition_items(id) ON DELETE SET NULL`, and the downgrade refuses while any
  part exists rather than dropping which item each came from.
- **Parts compose with the counts correctly.** `_settle` requires every live item done, so a condition with
  an employer part stays un-Ready until the employer delivers; `has_open_step` is a condition-level EXISTS,

  constraint on `(condition_id, key)` to violate regardless.

## Follow-up (the review's finding)

**A re-edit no longer deletes an ask that went out.** `update_item` removes only the parts never asked:
an OPEN part is still in an unsent draft, or is her untouched task. A part that is REQUESTED (its email
was sent), RECEIVED or DONE is kept, as the record of what was requested or arrived. This is the LP-940
doctrine that a sent draft keeps its items. A kept part's key is not split out again, so asking the same
performer again does not create a second part beside the one that went out.

**Test:** `test_a_part_whose_ask_went_out_survives_a_re_edit`.
- It first asserts the premise: the part is REQUESTED, its draft is the title draft, and that draft's
  message is SENT.
- Re-editing to [borrower] keeps the part, still linked to the sent draft.
- Re-editing back to [borrower, title] leaves the same two items.

**Mutations,** each restored from a copy and killed (1 test fails each time):
- the old guard (`status is not DONE`);
- no kept-key filter;
- delete every part;
- keep every part.

**A reading taken (STOP AND ASK 4):** a kept part still blocks the automatic move to Ready (`_settle`
counts every live item). Someone was asked and has not answered. She is not stranded: a manual move to
Ready has no item gate. Whether the sheet should dim the part or mark it not needed stays the owner's
question in the progress file.

## Review of the follow-up (`247ddd14`)

**Your two claims, verified independently:**

- *"`move_prep_status` has no item gate, so a manual move to Ready works."* True. Its refusals are
  trackable, not-replaced, target-offered, backward-needs-a-reason and waiting-needs-an-owner — none reads

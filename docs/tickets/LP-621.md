# LP-621 — the DTI ignores rental income entirely

## The report

LF-ABRS is a **rate-and-term refinance** ($487,500 at 6.875%) of an **investment property the
borrower does not live in** ($650,000 stated value, so the 75% LTV MI-1 reports), stating **$3,000/month of
net rental income** on the owned-property schedule. The DTI counts the subject's full new PITI of
**$5,067.13** as the borrower's housing expense and credits **nothing** for the rent.

`app/services/dti.py` never mentions rental income. `_auto_income_lines` reads `StatedIncomeItem` only;
`_auto_housing_lines` builds the subject's PITI + MI + HOA with no reference to occupancy. Nothing in
the calculator reads `stated_owned_properties.rental_income_gross` / `rental_income_net`, which the
MISMO import populates (`mismo/import_service.py:260`) and the snapshot publishes
(`snapshot/mismo_section.py:275`).

The file's stated income is two items, confirmed against staging — Base $16,000.00 and ContractBasis
$8,333.33, total $24,333.33 — with no rental line. So the $3,000 enters the ratio **nowhere, on either
side**.

## Why this is not simply "the DTI is too high"

The calculator's treatment is wrong in **two directions at once**, and the net is not obvious.

Fannie Mae B3-3.1-08 / B3-6-06 compute a subject investment property as
`(gross monthly rent × 75%) − full PITIA`; a positive result is added to gross monthly income, a
negative one is carried as a monthly **liability**. The borrower's **own** housing expense — the rent
or primary mortgage they actually pay — is what belongs on the housing side, because they do not live
in the subject.

Against that:

* **Too high** — the full $5,067.13 subject PITI sits in the numerator with no rent offset at all.
* **Too low** — the borrower lives somewhere, and that housing cost appears nowhere in the file.

So the current 44.8% is not a conservative figure that a lease would simply improve. It is a number
computed by a method the guideline does not describe, and the direction of its error depends on facts
the file does not yet carry.

## What is missing before it can be computed at all

1. **Gross rent.** The application states `rental_income_net = 3000.00` with `rental_income_gross`
   NULL. The 75% factor applies to GROSS (IN-14's cited primary, verbatim: "multiplying the gross
   monthly rent(s) by 75%"). A net figure cannot be run through it, and treating the stated net AS
   gross would silently apply the haircut twice.
2. **Support for the rent.** OC-3 is open on this file precisely because no lease, Schedule E or
   appraisal rent schedule is present. Unsupported rent must not enter the ratio — which is the
   correct behaviour today, reached by accident rather than by rule.
3. **The borrower's own housing expense.** Not modelled anywhere for a non-occupant borrower.

## Proposed

**A. The calculator reads rental income, gated on support.** A new auto-line derived from
`stated_owned_properties`, computed per Fannie: `(gross × 75%) − PITIA`, positive to income, negative
to debts. Gated on `occupancy.rental_support` — the loan-level tag OC-3 and IN-14 already read — so the
number moves when the evidence arrives, not when someone clicks. An unsupported or unknown support
verdict contributes nothing and says so on the line, in the same fail-closed spirit as the housing
inputs (LP-375): absent ≠ zero, and a rent we cannot support is not a rent of $0.

**B. Fail closed on a missing gross.** Where only net is stated, the line is not derivable and must
report itself as such rather than substituting the net. Mirrors `_unverified_housing_inputs`, which
already carries structured "the file states this but it is not verification" reasons (LP-620 era work).

**C. The non-occupant housing expense.** Scoped as a follow-on — it is a data-model gap, not a
calculator one, and the file has no field for it today.

## Explicitly NOT an Apply button on OC-3

Considered and rejected. Apply exists for a finding that knows the correct value and can write it
(FR-5 reads a payment and its counterparty off a bank statement; CR-1, DT-6, DT-8, PC-2 likewise).
OC-3 found the ABSENCE of a document — that is not a value. The only change it could plausibly write
is the rent offset, which asserts the very fact OC-3 exists to say is unproven; a one-click path from
"this rent is undocumented" to a materially better ratio is the wrong affordance. The rent must move
the number when it becomes SUPPORTED, which is what (A) does.

## Scope note

Neither Form 1007 nor Form 1025 exists as a document type in this system (IN-14 records this), so the
market-rent path cannot be represented and the lease path is the only available one.
`lease_agreement`, `rental_income_schedule` and `verification_of_rent` are cataloged;
`schedule_e` is NOT — worth adding, since it is often the fastest proof for a borrower who has owned
the property through a tax year.

## Built (LP-621, this ticket)

`services/rental_treatment.py` — `subject_rental_treatment()` returns Fannie's net figure for an
INVESTMENT subject, or the reason it cannot be computed. Wired into `build_dti_calculation` after the
housing lines, because the treatment nets against the PITIA they sum to — POST-override, so a
processor who corrected the tax figure has corrected what it nets against.

`(gross x 75%) - full PITIA`; positive becomes a named income line, negative a named obligation. The
75% is B3-3.8-01 verbatim, cited not chosen (ADR-361). `derivation` carries the arithmetic.

**On LF-ABRS it GATES rather than reporting 44.8%**, and that is the deliberate part. Neither input
exists: the application states net rent ($3,000) and no gross, and states no PRESENT housing expense,
so the borrower's own cost is unknown. The gate names both. Reporting 44.8% meant charging a
non-occupant borrower the full subject PITI while omitting what they actually pay to live — a
confident number produced by a method that does not apply to the loan, on the figure the decision
rests on. The same fail-closed discipline LP-375 settled for the housing inputs.

Three refusals, each pinned by a test:

* **Net is never substituted for gross.** The factor applies to GROSS; running the net figure through
  it haircuts an already-haircut number and UNDERSTATES the obligation — the direction that ships a
  bad loan.
* **Absent own-housing is None, not zero.** A borrower living with family genuinely has no housing
  cost, but that is a fact someone states, not a default to assume.
* **A primary residence and a second home are untouched.** The borrower occupies them and their PITIA
  IS the housing expense, which is what the calculator already does — this cannot reach an ordinary
  file.

Enabled by LP-627: `borrower_present_housing` reads MISMO's PRESENT housing expenses, which is why
`timing` was kept rather than filtered to "Proposed" at the parse.

## Still not built

**The `occupancy.rental_support` gate.** The treatment currently computes from stated figures without
asking whether the rent is DOCUMENTED. OC-3 and IN-14 answer that, and the tag is right there. Until
it is wired, a documented lease and an undocumented claim would be treated alike once gross rent is
stated — so this must land before the gate can be considered complete.

## Confidence

The mechanism is verified: the calculator's fields, the two income items, the owned-property row
(`is_subject = true`, usage Investment, disposition Retain, net $3,000, value $650,000, no lien UPB
stated), the loan itself (refinance / rate_term / $487,500 / 6.875%), and the absence of any rental
reference in `dti.py` were all read directly rather than recalled.

**The guideline mechanics should be confirmed with the domain expert before implementation** — whether
a subject-property negative net rental is carried as a liability or netted against housing, and how a
file that states only net rent should be treated, are the two decisions the whole calculation hangs on.
An earlier version of this analysis (delivered in-session) put the corrected DTI at 32.4% using a
simple "subtract rent from PITI" method; that is NOT the guideline's method and the figure should not
be relied on.

## A1 — ⚠️ "Retained" is an INFERENCE. No document, field or MISMO fact states it.

**Four independent searches, all negative:**

1. **No extractor field.** `grep` across all 121 extraction specs for `retain|reo|for_sale|listed_for|
   disposition|intent_to_sell|pending_sale` returns **one** hit — `retained_earnings_or_capital` on a
   business tax return, unrelated.
2. **No REO schedule document.** ⚠️ Searched the **raw source tree**, not one classifier query (policy 11,
   LP-494's mistake). 15 filename matches for `reo|real.?estate.?owned|schedule|rental|lease` — **all of
   them lease agreements and mortgage notes.** No REO schedule, no 1003 property section.
3. **No MISMO fact.** `mismo_section.py` emits nothing for REO, owned properties, or dispositions.
4. ⚠️ **The tags themselves are VOCABULARY ORPHANS.** `property.is_retained_reo` and
   `property.retained_pitia` are in `fact_tags.csv` and `rule_tags.csv` with a producer named, and have
   **zero entries in `tag_production.yaml` and no producer in `app/`** — the exact ADR-289 shape, and
   `property.is_warrantable_condo`'s situation exactly.

**So DT-6 is dropped, and the reason is RE-2's, which is stronger than "no fields exist":** owning a
property is not retaining it. A borrower may be selling. **Inferring the trigger makes the rule wrong at
its root** — it would put a PITIA into the DTI for a property that is under contract, inflating the ratio
and failing a qualified borrower.

### ⚠️ RE-1 considered SEPARATELY, as the ticket asked — and it also fails, for a different reason

Reconciling *owned* properties across sources needs no retention inference, so it deserved its own look.
**It fails on the other side: there is nothing to reconcile AGAINST.**

The *signals* exist and are strong — **71 mortgage statements, 21 property tax bills, 22 lease agreements,
16 payoff statements**, many for non-subject properties. But the **stated** side does not: no REO schedule

which is the AI-inference half the catalog names, with no stated list to test it against.

**Recommend: drop both.** If Priya says a 1003 REO section reaches processors, RE-1 becomes buildable and
worth reopening — that is question one for her.

## How both rules survive WITHOUT the retention inference

⚠️ **The trigger only breaks if a rule ASSERTS retention. Neither needs to.** Both SURFACE a discrepancy

**And they are split so ONE MATCHER serves both (ADR-375, the CR-1/CR-4 precedent):**

| rule | question | outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **RE-1** | is each mortgage statement's obligation **DISCLOSED** among the stated liabilities? | unmatched → `needs_review` — *possible undisclosed obligation* |
| **DT-6** | for a MATCHED obligation, does the stated monthly payment equal the statement's **PITIA** (payment + escrow)? | short → `needs_review` — *confirm retained vs. selling; the DTI may understate* |

**Neither ever fires.** An unmatched statement can be a paid-off loan, a duplicate, or a co-signed debt;
an understated payment can be a property under contract. Both are questions, not defects.

**RE-2 stays dropped and that is still right** — it asserted a *consequence* from the inference. These
surface a *discrepancy*. Different acts.

# ⚠️ THE LESSON, and it belongs to the review as much as to the build

**A search that finds no source for ONE side of a comparison has not established the comparison is
impossible. Check BOTH sides before dropping a cross-source rule.**

**Second occurrence in two tickets:**

| | what was checked | what was not |
|---|---|---|
| **LP-494 / CO-3** | IH-7 covers master insurance → "duplicate" | IH-7's own header **excludes fidelity** — the leg CO-3 needed |
| **LP-495a / DT-6 + RE-1** | no source for "retained" | **the stated side** — 135 `StatedLiability` rows, 61 `MortgageLoan`, never queried |

⚠️ **The failure mode is RHETORICAL, not technical, and that is why it survived review.** A1 opened with
*"Four independent searches, all negative"* — and **not one of the four queried the stated side.** All four
probed extractor schemas and document filenames; none touched the database. Enumerating searches reads as
thoroughness and conceals that they all examined the same half of the comparison.

⚠️ **THE APPROVER MADE THE SAME ERROR, and asked for it to be recorded.** Geet approved the drop, then:
*"I read 'four independent searches' as thorough without noticing none of them queried the stated side."*
**A confident count of negative searches passed review precisely because it looked like diligence.** Both
author and reviewer were reading the count instead of the coverage. Recorded so the next reader distrusts
the shape of the argument, not just its conclusion.

**The checks, before any future drop:**
1. Name **both** sides of the comparison, then show a query result for **each**.
2. Query the **database / MISMO** side, not only extractor schemas — stated facts live in
   `StatedLiability` and `mismo_section.py`, not `app/ai/extraction/`.
3. When a trigger is an inference, ask whether the rule can **surface** rather than **assert**. A
   `needs_review` that hands the question to the processor needs no inference (CO-3 fidelity, CO-5
   litigation).
4. One matcher can serve two rules asking different questions (ADR-375) — overlap is not automatically a
   drop.

## Design

**Drafts are Phase 4's `communications` rows, as they are.** A condition draft is a `Communication`
(`status=draft`, `direction=outbound`, `channel=email`, `body_format=html`,
`template_key=condition_request` or `condition_question`, `template_version=v1`). Phase 4's "Mark as sent"
(`email_send.send_draft`) records the send, moves the linked needs to Requested (the reminder clock),
writes `communication_sent` and the evidence row, and transmits nothing (no transport is connected).
Phase 4's delete (`email_reply.delete_draft`) removes a draft. **Nothing is sent by the app.**

**Kept apart from Phase 4's party drafts.** Phase 4 finds "the open draft for a party" by
`communications.party`. A condition draft leaves `party` empty, so Phase 4's compose can never append
needs to it, and the link to conditions lives in a new table.

**Migration.**
- `condition_drafts`: `communication_id` (unique, CASCADE), `loan_file_id`, `company_id`, `round_id`,
  `recipient` (VARCHAR + CHECK: `borrower`, `title_attorney`, `lo`, `insurance`, `hoa`, `employer`,
  `other_party`, `underwriter`), and `condition_id` (for a question; null for an ask).
- `condition_items.draft_id` (SET NULL).
- Event kind `condition_drafted` (25 listed).
- The table is excluded whole from the readonly layer, like `condition_items`.

**When drafts are made.** At **plan confirm** ("Confirm plan and draft 3 emails"):
- one ask draft per recipient, holding every open ask item of the round (`recipient_for`, LP-920);
- one question per push-back or ask-the-underwriter condition.

Each accumulates: an unsent draft for the same recipient on the file takes the new items rather than a
second draft being made (round 2 adds to an unsent title email). After confirm, a plan edit that adds
or removes an ask re-renders the open drafts. Each condition in a draft gets a `condition_drafted` event.

**The words come from the library, filled by code.** New optional library fields, each checked by the
loader:
- a type's `short` (S3-04's left column: "More assets for closing") and `why` (the grey Why: line);
- an item's `label` (S3-05's "Earnest money receipt") and `email` (the numbered line, bold part marked
  `**…**`).

Placeholders are only `{amount}`, `{amount_short}` and `{loan_number}`, filled by code from the reading's
specifics and the letter. An item with no `email` reads "**{name}** — {acceptable}."

**The borrower email is one line per need, not per item.** The statements need shared by 7086, 6132 and
6637 is one line. It reads "Your Capital One statements ending 9912 for July and August 2026 — all pages,
downloaded as PDFs from Capital One's website (not screenshots)." Its Why joins each condition's `why`.
The due date is the items' due date ("Thursday, September 3"). The body ends with the upload link
(below) and the signature: the confirming processor's name and the company's.

**Privacy.** The last four only, and a test asserts no nine-digit run and no full account number in any
body. Documents come back through the **upload link**, minted once per borrower draft with Phase 4's
`mint_upload_link` and revoked if the draft is deleted. Phase 4's links carry no needs scoping. The
upload page lists the file's open borrower needs, which after confirm are this round's items.

**Letter facts, read by code.**
- The surname and lender loan number come from the letter's title line (`LOAN APPROVAL CONDITIONS -
  RIVERA - 1226500417`).
- The property comes from the loan facts; the mortgagee clause from the header.
- The underwriter is the letter's `UW II` (else `Senior UW`). The address comes from the lender's
  underwriter contact with that name, else the file's assigned underwriter contact, else the lender's
  contact email.
- The insurance email carries the mortgagee clause verbatim.

**A missing address is asked once and remembered.** A draft with no address shows an address field. The
answer is saved as a file participant (Phase 4's `add_participant`, or the file's LO email for the LO)
and every draft to that recipient picks it up.

**Mark as sent moves our status (§4a 10, 11).** After `send_draft`:
- each linked item goes to `requested`;
- each linked condition moves to Waiting on whoever holds its **first asked item** whose email has gone.
  S3-12's 0132 is Waiting on LO (its disclosure comes first), whichever email she marked first.

The owner is Borrower; Title for title, attorney or the title email; LO (`broker`); Lender for a question;
Insurance; otherwise "someone". A condition at To do moves; one the sends already put at Waiting follows
to the right owner; one she moved herself is never overridden. Each move is `condition_prep_moved` with
`by: "email"` and the recipient. Phase 4's activity line is renamed to "Borrower email marked sent" or
"Question on 6178 marked sent" (it said "Sent a document request for 0 item(s)" for a question). The
lender's track is untouched.

**Reminders** are Phase 4's: the send sets each need's `requested_at`, which the existing reminder rules
read. Nothing new is scheduled, and nothing is sent automatically.

**The dialog (M6: reused, with a side column).** `ConditionDraftDialog` on the Conditions tab:
- title ("Email to the borrower · round 1") and the subtitle "Draft — nothing is sent from the app. Copy
  it into your mail, send it, then mark it sent.";
- left column: **In this email**, **Asked once**, **Privacy**, **Other drafts this round**, **Insurance
  emails**, **Why we think so**;
- To, Subject and the body;
- **Copy & open Gmail** (Phase 4's `composeUrl` and the user's mail-client preference), **Copy message**,
  **Mark as sent**, **Delete draft** (confirmed inline).

A due-date field on an ask draft (pre-made decision: "editable in the draft") sets the draft's items'
due date and re-renders. It is opened from the Next step token, the item's "In borrower email · draft", and a "Drafts for round
1" line under the round strip while any is unsent.

**Tails.** Items and question conditions carry their draft's state, so S3-12 reads "Borrower + title
emails · sent 08/28" and S3-01 reads "In borrower email · draft".

## Decisions

1. **Drafts are made at plan confirm.** That is S3-02's button: "Confirm plan and draft 3 emails".
2. **The owner follows the first asked item** (ADR-412). The first version used "first send wins", and
   my own test encoded Title for 0132. S3-12 says LO, so the rule and the test were corrected together.
3. **"(prior to funding)" only on an item that collects something.** 1947 carries it; 6378's instruction
   does not (S3-05).
4. **The dialog opens near the top**, as drawn, and scrolls when the email is long.
5. **The processor's mail client.** The harness's processor has chosen Gmail, as S3-04's "Copy & open
   Gmail" implies. Without a choice, Phase 4's picker asks once.
6. **"Drafts for round 1" line under the round strip.** It is shown while any draft is unsent. This is a
   way back to the drafts once the plan panel has gone. It is not drawn: S3-12 is after the sends, so it
   shows no such line.
7. **The due-date field** on an ask draft. This comes from the pre-made decision ("editable in the
   draft"). It is an addition, not on S3-04.
8. **No AI polish pass.** This is **STOP AND ASK 1** in the progress file: every sentence is a library
   template or a code-filled fact, and Phase 4 keeps its own AI prose switched off
   (`email_draft_enabled`).

## Review (of `29c74a7d`)

**One finding, and it is not this ticket's: a pre-existing test fails about once in a thousand runs.**
Everything this ticket claims held up, including the safety properties, which I checked against the
stored data and the repository rather than the code comments.

### The finding

**F1 — `test_inbox_token_is_independent_of_display_id` is randomised and flaky.** It surfaced as a
second failure in my suite run (8611 passed, 2 failed, against this ticket's 8612 / 1) and passes
alone. It draws 1000 display ids and asserts each 4-character code is not a SUBSTRING of that
iteration's inbox token. Both are random, so chance alone trips it: `RAKSTDzq1DNBKVL5PyKWmQ` contains
`AKST`. **Measured rather than estimated: 2 trips in 2000 simulated runs, 0.100%** — about one full
suite in a thousand.

It has nothing to do with LP-922, and nothing to do with what the test is for (the token must not be
DERIVED from the display id). Recorded in the progress file's "tests that already failed before Stage
3" list, with the rate and the mechanism, so the next ticket is not blamed for it. **Not fixed here**,
deliberately: the file belongs to no Stage 3 ticket and the brief keeps fixes inside the blast radius.
The fix is a one-line change of approach — generate two tokens for ONE display id and assert they
differ — and belongs in its own change.

### Safety, checked against the data rather than the code

- **Nothing is sent by the app.** No transport exists anywhere in `app/`: no `smtplib`, `aiosmtplib`,
  SendGrid, Mailgun, Postmark, Resend or SES call. `send_draft` records a send — status, suppression
  and rate-limit checks — and transmits nothing.
- **Last four only, no SSN — verified on the stored bodies.** Across every `communications` row the
  drafts produced: **zero** SSN-shaped strings, the account appears only as `ending 9912`, and the
  single 9-or-more digit run anywhere is `1226500417`, the lender's own loan number, which S3-05 and
  S3-06's Must-match lines require.
- **The HTML path is sanitised on every route in.** Built bodies pass `sanitise_html` before storage
  (both branches), and the processor's own edit passes it on send (`email_send.py:371`,
  `sanitise_html(outbound.body) if authored`). No new write path for a body was added, so permitting
  `draft.body_html` through `dangerouslySetInnerHTML` is defensible.
- **The migration** is raw SQL (12 `op.execute`, no helpers), and `condition_drafts` is in the readonly
  layer's excluded tables with its reason written.

## Review of the follow-up (of `35a558f8`)

**One finding, fixed: the fact check could not see a swap.** Everything else holds, including the
properties that carry the weight now that a changed fact is shown rather than refused.

### The finding

**F2 — every comparison was by SET, so re-attribution was invisible.** `email_facts` asked "is this
figure still here?" and never "does it still say the same thing". I attacked it with six adversarial
polishes; five behaved, and one passed with **no warning at all**:

| Polish | Warned? |
|---|---|
| the two amounts SWAPPED (required ↔ verified) | **NO — silent** |
| account ending 9912 → 9921 | yes (dropped / added) |
| an amount changed outright | yes (dropped / added) |
| a deadline invented ("by Friday") | yes (added Friday) |
| "all pages" dropped | no — wording, see the boundary below |
| "not screenshots" negated | no — wording, see the boundary below |

The swap is not hypothetical. 7086's email says "closing needs **$38,210.40** and **$11,062.18** is
verified so far". Reversed, it holds exactly the same two amounts, passes every check, and tells the
borrower the opposite of the truth about their own shortfall — with nothing shown to her. Under the
owner's rule ("a changed fact is shown with a warning") that is a changed fact.

**Fixed** by comparing the ORDER the amounts appear in, and only when the sets match, so a genuine
change still reports dropped/added once rather than twice. The warning names both orders, because
"something moved" is not actionable. Deliberately cheap: reading which amount plays which role would
need the model this module exists to second-guess.
`tests/conditions/test_email_facts_reattribution.py` pins it — the swap, a positive control that an
untouched body warns about nothing, a reworded-but-same-order body staying silent, and a changed
amount not being reported twice. Mutation-checked: disabling the check fails exactly one of the four.

**The boundary, stated rather than discovered later.** The two "no" rows above are wording, not facts,
and this design cannot police them: dropping "all pages" or negating "not screenshots" changes what
the borrower does while every figure survives. That is the cost of "polish the wording", and it is why
the proposal is shown for her to read rather than applied.

### The safety properties, checked

- **Only the body goes to the model** — `messages=[{"role": "user", "content": body_html}]` and the
  prompt as system. No snapshot, no file summary.
- **The reply is sanitised before anything else touches it**, and — the ordering matters — the fact
  diff runs on the SANITISED text, so the warnings describe what would actually be stored.
- **Nothing is stored until "Use this."** `propose_polish` reads the message and returns; it writes
  nothing. `use_polish` re-sanitises the submitted body, so `PUT …/body` cannot store raw HTML either,
  which is what makes permitting `proposal.polished_html` through the innerHTML allow-list defensible.
- **Logs carry counts only** — `condition_polish_proposed, warnings=len(warnings)`.
- **The migration** is raw SQL (5 `op.execute`, no helpers).

### Design

**Polish with AI** (violet, labelled as AI per README rule 2) sits in the dialog's button row on an
unsent draft. `POST /loan-files/{id}/condition-drafts/{draft_id}/polish` sends the draft's body to the
model and returns a proposal. Nothing is stored.

**What goes to the model, and what it may do.**
- Only the email body goes: HTML, the allow-listed tags. The loan snapshot never goes.
- The prompt `ai/prompts/conditions/polish_v1.txt` lets it change tone, grammar, sentence order, the
  greeting and the sign-off. It must keep every amount, date, account ending, loan number, link and
  numbered item, add none, and return the same tags.
- The reply is passed through `sanitise_html` before anyone sees it.

**The facts are checked by code** (`app/conditions/email_facts.py`, pure). Each warning names the fact:
- a money amount (compared by value, so $2,850 and $2,850.00 are one fact);
- a numeric date or a spoken date ("Thursday, September 3");
- a digit run of four or more (account endings, the loan number);
- a link;
- day or month words it introduced;
- a numbered item count that changed.

Three kinds of warning: "Dropped: …", "Added: …", and "The list had 2 items; the AI's has 3". A model
or transport failure returns a refusal sentence, never a silent unchanged body.

**Use this.** `PUT …/body`:
- stores the sanitised proposal;
- sets `condition_drafts.polished_at`;
- writes `condition_draft_polished` on each condition in the draft (detail: the number of warnings she
  accepted, never the text).

The dialog then shows a violet "Polished by AI" mark. If a plan edit later re-renders the draft, the
body is rebuilt from the library and `polished_at` clears, so she can polish again. A sent draft cannot
be polished or changed.

**Migration.**
- `condition_drafts.polished_at`;
- event kind `condition_draft_polished` (26 listed).

### Done (follow-up), and the test for each

`backend/tests/conditions/test_condition_draft_polish.py` has 15 tests. The model is mocked in each; the
test states what the model "returned", and code decides what changed.

| Owner's answer | Test |
|---|---|
| a proposal; nothing stored until Use this | `test_polish_proposes_then_she_uses_it`: only the body goes to the model; the stored body is unchanged after the proposal; Use this stores it, sets `polished_at` and writes `condition_draft_polished` on 7086, 6132 and 6637; the model's code fence is stripped |
| a changed or dropped fact is shown with a warning, not refused | `test_a_dropped_fact_is_shown_with_a_warning_not_refused` (the proposal still comes back, with "Dropped: $38,210.40"). The fact check: `test_each_changed_fact_is_named` (amount, account ending, spoken date, deadline, upload link), `test_a_new_deadline_with_no_digits_is_named`, `test_a_merged_item_is_named`, `test_a_rewording_that_keeps_every_fact_has_no_warning`, `test_the_same_amount_written_two_ways_is_one_fact` |
| every draft | `test_every_draft_can_be_polished_and_a_failure_is_said` (borrower, title/attorney, LO and the question; a model failure is the refusal sentence) |
| always on | nothing reads `email_draft_enabled`; the same test runs with the setting at its default (off) |
| safety | `test_a_sent_email_is_neither_polished_nor_changed`, `test_the_used_body_is_sanitised` |
| her polish is kept | `test_her_polish_survives_an_unrelated_edit_but_not_a_change_to_its_items` |

**Frontend:**
- `condition-draft-dialog.test.tsx`: the proposal shows with the dropped fact and stores nothing; the send
  buttons wait while it is on screen; Use this sends `warnings_accepted: 1`; Keep mine restores the
  draft; a refusal is said; the "Polished by AI" mark.
- `history.test.ts`: the new event kind, and 26 kinds listed.
- `email-body-templates.test.ts`: `proposal.polished_html ?? ""` is permitted on the sanitised argument
  (the reply passes `sanitise_html` before the route returns it).

**Mutations.** Each of these fails a test:
- keep-polish-on-unrelated-edit;
- clear-on-rebuild;
- sanitise-on-use;
- `polished_at` on use;
- the dropped-link check;
- the item-count check.

The code-fence strip survived until the test asserted no fence reaches her. The "strip amounts and dates
before counting digit runs" step survived because the pattern's lookarounds already exclude those runs,
so the step was removed as dead code rather than tested.

**Visual.** There is no reference screen for the polish. S3-04 and S3-06 were re-shot: the violet "Polish
with AI" sits between Copy message and Mark as sent, and every earlier Must-match line still holds. The
proposal panel is checked by the render tests. The harness cannot show it, because its API would call
the real model.

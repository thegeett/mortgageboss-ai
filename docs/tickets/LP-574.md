# LP-574 — a recurring payment is not a debt payment just because it recurs

**Status:** prompt change shipped; unverified against a live model until the next run

## What happened

On LF-WCHG, FR-5 (recurring undisclosed debit) raised a finding on a **$116.43 monthly payment to
GEICO** — an insurance premium, not a debt.

**FR-5 was correct.** Its applicability is three conditions, and all three held:

```yaml
- {tag: txn.is_money_in, op: eq, value: out}
- {tag: txn.apparent_category, op: eq, value: debt_payment}
- {tag: txn.is_recurring, op: eq, value: "yes"}
```

It recurs (2 months), it matches no stated liability (4 compared), so `exempt_when` did not clear it,
and per ADR-336 a judgment rule surfaces for ratification. The finding existed **only** because an
insurance premium had been tagged `debt_payment`.

## The prompt, not just the model

The two candidate definitions were:

- `debt_payment` — *"a payment to an apparent CREDITOR ... identified from a lender-like payee"*
- `vendor` — *"an ordinary purchase / merchant"*

An insurance premium is neither. It is not a creditor, and a monthly premium does not read as an
ordinary purchase. **Insurance had no home**, so the model reached for the nearest bucket and said so
in its own reasoning:

> "While insurance is not a traditional debt, premium payments to insurers can be considered
> recurring obligations similar to debt payments."

It returned **0.70** confidence — the model telling us it was uncomfortable. The same gap swallows
utilities, subscriptions and memberships: every recurring NON-CREDIT obligation.

## The fix

A definition change, not a vocabulary change. `debt_payment` now leads with **BORROWED MONEY BEING
REPAID** and names the exclusions outright; `vendor` now says *"a purchase or a payment for goods or
services — INCLUDING a recurring one: insurance premiums, utilities, phone/internet, subscriptions,
memberships, tuition"*.

Adding an `insurance` enum value was the tempting alternative and the wrong one: every rule reading
the enum would have to learn it, and `vendor` already means "paid for goods or services".

**The lender-like-payee guard is deliberately kept.** The risk in the other direction is a real auto
or personal loan to an unfamiliar lender landing in `vendor`; that phrase is what prevents it.

## The finding also contradicted itself on screen

The tags panel showed `txn.apparent_category = debt_payment` while the composed message read
*"represents an insurance premium, not a debt obligation."* Both were displayed to the processor.
Not a second bug — the same one surfacing twice, because the composer reasoned from the payee while
the rule scoped on the category. It resolves with the category.

## Blast radius

`txn.apparent_category` also feeds **AS-1, AS-2, AS-5, AS-12 and IN-1**. Narrowing `debt_payment` to
borrowed money is the right direction for all of them — a debt payment should mean debt — but this
is a change to five live rules' inputs, not only FR-5's.

## Two copies, one text

The prompt exists twice by design: `STAGE_A_TRANSACTION_SYSTEM_PROMPT` (what live AS-1 runs) and the
`txn_stage_a` group in `tag_production.yaml` (what calibration measures). A convergence guard asserts
they are byte-identical so drift cannot be merged. I edited one and the guard caught it — the YAML
copy is now generated from the Python constant with the block-scalar reindent applied.

## What is NOT proven

That the model now classifies GEICO as `vendor`. The prompt names the distinction; whether the model
follows it needs a real run. **Re-measure the `txn.apparent_category` distribution on LF-WCHG after
the next verification** rather than assuming the wording worked — and expect FR-5 to stop firing on
that transaction, not merely to change its reasoning.

# LP-626 — a calculative rule shows the arithmetic that decided it

## What this ticket was going to be, and why it isn't

Reading LF-ABRS's income findings I flagged two as defects:

* **IN-11 satisfied (green)** — "Two consecutive W-2s from FINRA cover tax years 2024 and 2025,
  establishing the required two-year employment and income history", on a file where the borrower
  **left FINRA in April 2025**. It looked like a false green, the most dangerous kind, because a green
  is never asked again.
* **IN-10 open (red)** — "the income is declining year-over-year" with no figures, on a file whose
  total income had risen.

**Both were wrong, and checking before changing anything is the only reason this ticket did not ship a
regression.**

**IN-11 is an expert ruling, not a defect.** From `activation_bars.yaml`:

> "LP-393-6: Priya SETTLED B14 — a TERMINATED job's two years DOES count as HISTORY;
> `has_2yr_history` is about HISTORY only, and the W-2/1099/offer-letter documentation standard is a
> SEPARATE check."

FINRA is a terminated job; its two years count. "Fixing" the prompt would have reverted a signed-off
ruling and knocked a tag measured 13/13 off 100%.

**IN-10 is correctly reasoned.** The tag's own provenance:

> "2024 full-year wages were $155,443.80 from FINRA; 2025 wages were $49,674.77 (**partial year, ~4
> months based on end date 2025-04-27**). **Annualizing 2025 (~$149,024 if extrapolated for 12
> months) still shows decline**, but more critically the borrower changed employers mid-2025…"

It detected the partial year, annualised it, and found a genuine ~4% decline. My "income actually
rose" objection conflated total current household income with the documented W-2 wage trend — a
different question from the one the tag answers. And the bar records Priya accepting false positives
here deliberately: *"FN (uses declining income at face value → a bad loan ships) >> FP (a false decline
→ a human glances)."*

That reasoning also cites `borrower_mismo` end dates — data that only exists because of LP-624's
backfill, which is separate evidence that LP-624 worked.

## What was actually wrong

The arithmetic above is **in the provenance panel and nowhere else**. IN-10's finding message is its
spec's static template: "the income is declining year-over-year — stability/continuance must be
reviewed". A calculative rule reads one tag and prints a fixed sentence, so everything the model
worked out is one panel-open away.

That matters *because* of how the rule is calibrated. The accepted remedy for a false positive is "a
human glances" — and the glance currently costs opening provenance to find out whether the decline is
real.

## The fix

`_gated_tag_derivation` on the deterministic path: when a rule gates on **exactly one** AI tag, that
tag's reasoning becomes the finding's `derivation`.

`derivation` rather than `reasoning`, deliberately — LP-535 made this the field the composer cannot
paraphrase away, for exactly this auditability purpose ("a processor who can see the threshold's
derivation can judge the threshold itself").

Scoped tightly, and each restriction is tested:

* **One gated tag only.** A rule comparing several has no single "the reason", and picking one would
  assert that it decided the outcome. AS-1 gates on three.
* **AI-produced only.** A derived or parsed tag's provenance is the arithmetic the rule's own template
  already states; repeating it is noise dressed as auditability.
* **Non-empty only.** A derivation that says nothing is worse than none, because it occupies the place
  a reader expects the arithmetic to be.

## What this leaves alone

No rule semantics change. No prompt changes. No tag values move. `income_stability`'s prompt was the
tempting target and is exactly what the two rulings above say not to touch.

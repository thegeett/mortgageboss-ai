### The one disagreement — a definitional divergence, NOT a prompt bug

`child_support`: Priya `yes`, the AI `unknown` (confidence 0.7). The AI's reasoning was **honest, not wrong**:

> *"The 1003 states the youngest child is age 9. Child support typically continues until age 18, ~9 years
> remaining, but without court orders or other documentation evidencing the specific termination date, the exact
> continuation horizon cannot be determined from income documents alone."*

Priya's `yes` carried the note *"Still child reach 16, this is counted yes"* — she KNOWS her jurisdiction's
termination age and computed 9 → 16 = 7 years > 3. The model correctly declined to invent a termination date it
had no court order for. This is the same shape as ADR-330 (`continuance_3yr=unknown` on a W-2): the model being
right about the limit of its own evidence. **A "sharper prompt" would make it worse** — it would guess `yes` on a
17-year-old too. The right fix is a deterministic child-support rule, recorded below (ADR-337).

## Her notes verbatim + the two candidate rules (recorded, NOT built — LP-393-6)

The continuance TAG judges the substance; a documentation standard is a SEPARATE rule (LP-393-6). Priya's notes
spin off exactly two:

**① Pension / Social Security ⇒ require an AWARD LETTER** — a documentation JUDGMENT (needs calibration).
> pension (…101) & social_security (…105): *"Need award letter, could be social security award letter, school
> award letter"*

- What it needs: a **producer** for "award letter present" — a document PERCEPTION tag (the `voe_present` /
  `offer_letter_present` shape), across the variants she names (SS award letter, school award letter, …), plus a
  labeling round. It is **not** arithmetic → not deterministic; it needs the LP-420→LP-426 worksheet path.

**② Child support ⇒ child's AGE vs a termination age** — POSSIBLY DETERMINISTIC (the ADR-334 escape hatch).
> child_support (…103): *"Still child reach 16, this is counted yes"*

- The rule is arithmetic: `termination_age − child_age >= 3` → continues. Given the child's age and a termination
  age, no judgment (and no labeling round) is needed — ADR-334's deterministic escape hatch from ADR-332's
  synthetic-calibration limit.
- What it needs, FLAGGED (not resolved here): (a) the **youngest child's age as an extracted field** — today it
  lives only in the free-text `other_income_description` (the ADR-333 lossy-boundary gap); (b) the **termination
  age pinned with Priya** — her note says **16**, common defaults are **18**, and it is plausibly
  **state-dependent** (a jurisdiction table, not a scalar). (b) is a domain question for the sister, not a coding
  decision — the rule cannot be built until it is a confirmed constant.

## The verdict + why NO bar is proposed

**Verdict: the tag measures 5/6; the single miss is a definitional case, not a calibration failure.** No bar is
proposed for `income.continuance_3yr` / IN-13 — four independent reasons, any one sufficient:

1. **83.3% clears no reasonable threshold** (the structural bars sit at 0.90–0.95).
2. **The miss is definitional** — a bar cannot fix `child_support`; a deterministic rule (②) can. Raising the bar
   would paper over a computation the model should not be doing by judgment.
3. **Thin + skewed n** (5 yes / 1 no, the negative on one row) — too little to trust a height against.
4. **A bar would activate nothing anyway.** IN-13 has OTHER blockers (below), so even a passing bar is moot.

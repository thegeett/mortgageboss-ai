## D1 — IN-13's real scope (the STOP, quoted)

`rule_kinds.csv`: **`IN-13,Other income continuance,Income,judgmental,ai_judgment … Continuance (3yr) + receipt —
interpret award/order.`** Spec `criteria`: *"'Other' income (e.g. **child support, alimony, a pension, an award**)
may be used only if it is likely to CONTINUE at least 3 years…"*; scope: *"every borrower with 'other' income."*

**IN-13 covers ALL non-employment income — rental is ONE of six** (LP-420's continuance worksheet built pension,
alimony, child support, disability, Social Security, notes). Gating it on `income.has_rental_income` (LP-422)
would **silently narrow it to rental**, dropping the other five — a coverage regression disguised as a fix.
**STOP: IN-13 is NOT re-scoped.** The right gate would be a broader borrower-level "has other income" signal,
which does not exist — a separate, larger ticket. (See **ADR-335**: the available gate narrower than the rule's
intent.)

## D2 — IN-13's verdict tag is uncalibrated (held regardless)

IN-13's verdict is `income.continuance_3yr` (per_borrower, AI). LP-420 generated its worksheet (6 rows) but
**Priya has not labeled it** — the committed CSV is a blank template (rows end `,,`). So even a correct gate
leaves IN-13 held on its verdict tag. **Both D1 and D2 hold IN-13 independently.**

## D3 — the activation decision (argued per rule; recommended; IN-13 not silently activated)

Both scope gates rest on the tax-return extractor — a STARTER (ADR-333), never validated, no golden files. The
**failure modes differ**, and that is the crux:

| rule | miss → | failure mode | decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| **IN-12** | missed Schedule C → is_self_employed no/unknown → **not_applicable** | wrong SCOPE, **visible** as absence; **IN-11 (live) still surfaces the 2-year gap** — no finding lost | **ACTIVATE** |
| **IN-13** | (if gated) missed Schedule E → not_applicable | **silent** under-coverage — a borrower with that income never checked | **HOLD** |

**Recommendation (implemented): activate IN-12, hold IN-13.** IN-12's verdict tag `has_2yr_history` is
Priya-validated at 0.9 / measured 100% (inherited from IN-11 — the IN-6 same-tag pattern), and its gate is a
DETERMINISTIC Schedule-C fact (ADR-334), so there is no uncalibrated judgment; the only risk is the unvalidated
extractor, accepted because the failure mode is a backstopped wrong-scope. IN-13's silent under-coverage +
uncalibrated verdict forbid activating it. **Priya's view on the real-world Schedule-C miss-rate is the follow-up
refinement; golden-file validation of the extractor is its own ticket.**

## 1. Retry a reading from the screen

After an AI failure (an expired AWS login, a stalled task), every condition falls back. The round is
recorded as read, so no condition is "unread" and the page offers no Read again. LP-966's review made such
a row say "We haven't read this one yet", with no control, because the reading action is a product
decision. That decision is to add **Read again** for those conditions.

## 2. The round-2 plan

On round 2 and later, conditions seen again are listed in the plan with the plan they already had.
Decide: list them, or show only the new conditions in the plan.

## What was built

### 1. Read again for conditions the AI did not read

**The real cause.** A failed model call does not leave conditions unread. `read_round` reads every one
anyway, from the library alone:
- `reading_source` is LIBRARY;
- the status is NEEDS_CONFIRMATION;
- since LP-965 there is no type, so at most one generic task from the sheet's owner hint.

The run ends DONE and nothing is unread, so LP-952's Read again had nothing to offer.

**`condition_reading_state.read_without_ai`** finds the conditions a fresh reading can safely replace. Each
must be:
- read from the library, and not confirmed;
- with every task OPEN and from the reading;
- with no `condition_plan_changed` of hers. An earlier Read again's own `read_again` change does not count.

Anything she confirmed, changed, marked, added to or sent is excluded and keeps its plan. Each rule has
a test that only that rule catches. A sent ask is REQUESTED with no plan change, so only the
every-task-OPEN rule catches it.

**`reading_state`** reports `without_ai` (a count, and a state when the reading is otherwise done).
**`POST /condition-rounds/{id}/read`**, with nothing unread, now:
- clears those conditions (`clear_for_reading`): reading and plan reset, the guessed task soft-deleted, the
  drafts followed, and a `read_again` event recorded;
- queues the reading. The task reads them with the AI and plans them as usual (`_run_read`).

If the AI fails again, they are offered again.

**The screen** (`ReadingStatus`) says: "6 conditions were read without the AI. The AI's reading failed,
so their plans are guesses from the sheet. Read again to have the AI read them; anything you have already
worked on is kept." It then offers **Read again**. "Failed" rather than "couldn't be reached", because the
fallback also covers an answer that could not be parsed (`_ask_model`).

### 2. Round 2's plan shows only what is new

The plan lists conditions whose `first_round_id` is this round. Conditions seen again keep their plan
(`build_plan`'s carry-over) and are counted in one line: "N conditions from earlier rounds keep their plan.
You'll see them in the list after you confirm."

**A carried condition that blocks Confirm stays in the plan.** "Confirm 0132's reading first" must name a
row she can click (LP-966). With nothing new, the heading says "Nothing new to plan on this sheet" and
Confirm stays. Round 1 is unchanged.

**Left as the server says:** the drafts named in "When you confirm" and in first moves are the round's
own drafts, which can include a carried condition's code. Those emails are real and unsent, so the
sentence stays true.

## Review (of `dc22c109`)

**Three findings: two fixed here, one reported because its fix needs a server field rather than a
review's edit. The eligibility rules are careful and the disclosure is honest, including an unpinned
clause — which turns out to be pinnable, and now is. The finding that matters is the one not on the
list: an unlink is work, and nothing here could see it.**

### Finding 1 — Read again strands a document she refused

`read_without_ai`'s four rules are chosen so that "nothing of hers is lost". A document she **unlinked**
is hers, and not one of them sees it:

- `unlink_document` DELETES the evidence row rather than flagging it (LP-953's deliberate design, so no
  query over evidence has to remember to skip it);
- it then recomputes the task, and with nothing else answering it and no ask sent the status goes back
  to **OPEN** (`condition_links.py:266-275`);
- and it records `condition_evidence_unlinked` — **never** `condition_plan_changed`.

So a condition she linked a document to and then took it off again is LIBRARY, NEEDS_CONFIRMATION, every
task OPEN and from the reading, with no plan change: eligible. `clear_for_reading` then soft-deletes the
items, and `condition_item_unlinks` is keyed `(item_id, document_id)` — so her refusal is left on an id
nothing reads while the fresh reading mints new ones, after which the arrival door
(`condition_matching.document_answers`, which consults `unlinked_pairs`) re-links the document she
removed. That is the LP-953 finding reached through this ticket's door, and the `copy_unlinks` helper
that review added cannot help: at clear time the replacement items do not exist yet.

**Fixed** as a fifth rule, in the same shape as the others: any condition with a `ConditionItemUnlink`
on one of its items is "worked" and keeps its plan. Joined through the items because the row is keyed on
the item, and soft-deleted items count — a refusal stranded by an earlier clear is still hers.
`test_a_document_she_unlinked_is_never_read_again` builds the real sequence (link, then unlink), asserts
the task is back to OPEN so the other rules genuinely do say "offer it", and keeps a second condition as
its control. Removing the rule fails that test and nothing else.

### Finding 2 — the confirm summary names a code with no row on the screen

Part 2 filters the rows to `first_round_id === round.id` plus any blocking carried condition, and then
passes those rows AND `data.drafts` to `ConfirmSummary`. The drafts are the server's, so "Unlock the 2
drafted emails: to Title/attorney (1947)" can name a carried condition while no 1947 row exists above
it. One box, two scopes.

The ticket judged this true and it is — the email is real and unsent, which is a claim about the server.
It is the screen that is the problem, and this ticket already accepts the principle elsewhere: a
**blocking** carried condition is kept in the plan precisely so "Confirm 0132's reading first" names a
row she can click (LP-966 review). The drafts case was left out of that reasoning.

**Fixed** without hiding anything or widening the rows: the panel already computes which conditions are
on the round but not shown, so the line now ends "1947 is from an earlier round, so it is not listed
above." Two tests — one that the sentence appears, one that nothing extra appears when every drafted
code has a row — and removing the clause fails the first.

### Finding 3 — reported, not fixed: confirming round 2 can put an unreviewed plan in force

A condition seen again keeps its plan and is counted in one line. If its earlier round's plan was never
confirmed, that line says "keeps their plan" about a plan she never approved — and `_plan_in_force`
reads `condition.last_seen_round_id`, which for a condition seen again is **this** round. So confirming
round 2 puts the carried plan in force (drafts resync, `apply_step_status` runs) for a condition she was
never shown, because `blocking_codes` is about readings that need her, not about plans she has not
confirmed.

This is the builder's own focus area 5, and I think the answer to "is that a hole?" is yes. I am
reporting rather than fixing it for two reasons, and I would rather say them than quietly choose:

- **the honest limit of my evidence.** I traced the mechanism statically — the row filter at
  `round-plan-panel.tsx:119-120`, `_plan_in_force` at `condition_plan.py:942-947` — and did **not** build
  a two-round fixture with round 1 unconfirmed to watch it happen. So I am confident about the code path
  and have not seen the behaviour;
- **the fix needs data the screen does not have.** Whether a condition's plan is in force is not on
  `ConditionPublic`, so showing "carried, and you never confirmed this one" needs a server field. That
  is a ticket, not a review's edit, and guessing at it in the panel would be the wrong kind of fix.

The cheap interim, if the owner wants one before that: `round_plan_blockers` could treat a carried
condition with an unconfirmed plan as a blocker, which keeps the row on screen by the mechanism this
ticket already uses.

### Checked and not findings

- **The `origin is READING` clause IS pinnable, and now is** (focus area 2). The ticket records it as
  unpinned "because a task she adds also writes a plan change", which is true of `add_item`. But
  `carry_plan` sets `origin=CARRIED` (`condition_plan.py:742`) and writes **no**
  `condition_plan_changed` — so for a carried task that clause is the only lock, not a redundant one.
  `test_a_carried_task_keeps_its_plan` pins it, asserts no qualifying plan change exists so the test
  cannot pass by the other rule, and keeps a control; removing the clause fails exactly it. The origin
  the ticket asks about — "carried items from an earlier round" — is the one that makes the clause
  matter.
- **A sent email cannot lose its items** (focus area 1). The eligibility rule is every task OPEN, and a
  sent ask is REQUESTED (`_asked` is what put it there), so such a condition is excluded before
  `clear_for_reading` ever sees it. For an unsent draft left holding nothing, `sync_round_drafts` is
  explicit — "An unsent draft left with nothing in it is deleted" — so `follow_drafts` empties or
  removes it rather than leaving a draft that asks for nothing.
- **Queueing only the unread on the first press is defensible** (focus area 3). With both unread and
  without-AI conditions, the route reads the unread and leaves the rest, and the screen offers Read
  again once more — so it is self-correcting rather than stuck, and the second press does the second
  half. The alternative (clear and queue everything at once) would re-read conditions she may be in the
  middle of looking at. Worth a sentence in the message if the owner finds the double press odd, but
  nothing here is wrong.
- **The message's wording is accurate about the cause.** "The AI's reading failed" rather than "could
  not be reached" covers an answer that could not be parsed as well as a dead login, which is what
  `_ask_model`'s fallback actually catches.

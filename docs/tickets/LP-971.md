# LP-971 — Describe it: a sentence becomes steps and drafts

- **Ticket:** LP-971 (the owner, 2026-10-08). Series LP-967 to LP-973; the overview is in `LP-967.md`.
- **Source:** the agreed design ("Or describe what you want to do · Turn it into steps").
- **Status:** built (the owner, 2026-10-09: "start LP-971"). Not yet tried against the real model; see "Open".

## What it adds

She types what she wants, for example "Ask the underwriter to move 1228 to closing, it's new
construction". The AI proposes steps from LP-969's building blocks, with the drafts written. She checks
them before anything is sent.

## Done when

- Steps come only from the real building blocks.
- Every draft passes the same checks as the reading: amounts must be the lender's, and nothing invented.
- A refusal or failure says so.
- Peer review.

## What was built

**The model proposes; code checks; she adds.** Nothing is stored until she presses "Add these steps".

- **`app/ai/condition_describe.py`** (`describe_steps`, `checked_steps`):
  - **The call:** one model call with prompt `conditions/describe_v1.txt`, sending her words, the lender's
    words and the reading's summary (what the reading already sends). Logs carry counts only.
  - **It never raises.** No answer, an unreadable one, or no steps each come back as a sentence. When the
    model says why there are no steps, that sentence reaches her, so it is held to the same fact check.
  - **Only the real building blocks:** LP-969's seven (`BLOCKS`).
    - An email goes only to someone the screen can email: `EMAIL_TO`, derived from the performers minus
      herself, the appraiser and the attorney, as the screen's `EMAIL_WHO`. The attorney is mapped to
      Title, since they share one email.
    - At most one question to the underwriter, because a condition carries one step.
    - At most six steps, and none with no words.
    - Each step left out comes with its reason.
  - **Nothing invented, checked by code:** each step is compared with her words and the lender's by
    `email_facts.fact_warnings`, the polish's checker. A step that adds an amount, a date, a day word, or
    an account or loan number found in neither is left out, naming the fact.
- **`POST /conditions/{id}/plan/describe`:** returns `DescribePublic` (steps, left out, refusal). It stores
  nothing.
- **The screen** (`plan-describe.tsx`): inside the open "Add a step" menu, "Or describe what you want to
  do" and "Turn it into steps".
  - It shows the proposed steps, each with its block and recipient, the steps left out with their
    reasons, or the refusal.
  - "Add this step" / "Add these N steps" adds each through `addedStepFor`, the function the menu itself
    now uses. An AI step is therefore drafted, checked and recorded exactly as the same block chosen by
    hand.
- **A question's words are not in its note,** and the screen says so: "We draft the note from the
  condition. Put this in it before you send." The underwriter note is written by code
  (`render_question`), and her words cannot be stored on it from here.
- **The drafts** are the ones LP-969's doors already write: an email step joins that person's email for
  the round.

## Review (peer session, 2026-10-10)

Reviewed at `1e5ef925`. The checking is the right shape — the model proposes, code compares every step
with her words and the lender's, and she adds through LP-969's own doors — and the four mirror tests make
the block list and the recipient list one rule each. Three findings, all fixed; the four questions are
answered below, two of them with measurements rather than opinions.

### 1. "Add these steps" opened the link dialog on the wrong task (fixed)

`addAll` called the add handler once per step, in a loop, so every add was fired at once. The panel opens
the link dialog by finding the item that was not there before — `updated.items.find((each) =>
!before.has(each.id))` — and `before` was captured from the prop, so every add in the batch compared
against the SAME set. The first new item therefore won, whichever step had asked for the dialog.

Reproduced: a proposal of `todo` then `check`, one press of "Add these 2 steps". The dialog opens on
"Call the LO about the fee" and offers to link her document to it; the step that asked for the dialog was
"Check the HOA certificate". She would link the HOA certificate to the LO phone call.

Fixed by handing the parent the WHOLE proposal (`onAddAll`) and chaining it: the next add starts from the
previous response, and the ids known after it are threaded in as the next comparison, so exactly one item
in each response is new and it is the one that step added. Two mutations, each failing the new test: not
threading the updated set, and firing them together instead of chaining.

**AND MY FIRST FIX WAS WRONG IN A WAY THE TEST CAUGHT.** I started with a queue and a `known` ref,
resetting the ref when the queue emptied. With a synchronous response the queue empties between the first
step being queued and the second being pushed, so the ref reset mid-batch and the bug came back. The
chain has no batch boundary to get wrong: the list is one argument.

Chaining also removes the concurrency half of the builder's question. Firing N adds at once was N
concurrent `resync_file` calls on one file, and nothing serialises those: `condition_drafts._open_draft`
looks an open draft up by file and recipient, two requests can each see none, and `condition_drafts`
carries no uniqueness on `(round_id, recipient, condition_id)` — only on `communication_id`. The reading
doors take `_lock_file_reading` for exactly this reason; the add-item door takes no lock. The door is now
sequential from the only screen that pressed it in a batch, but the underlying race is unguarded for any
other concurrent caller, and that is worth its own ticket rather than a line here.

### 2. Her words were cleared off the screen by the press that told her to keep them (fixed)

A question block sets the condition's own step and keeps no text — `render_question` writes the note from
the condition — so the step says "We draft the note from the condition. Put this in it before you send."
`addAll` then cleared the textarea and closed the menu, so the words she had just been told to put in the
note were gone from the screen, and nothing else holds them. Her words now stay when a question took
them, and clear for every other block, whose task name carries them into the plan. The control is the
second test: a `todo` still clears.

### 3. The reason the attorney is excluded is now a test (fixed, the builder's offer taken)

The mirror tests pin that the server's `NOT_EMAILED` and the screen's agree. Both could drop `attorney`
together and stay green, which is the LP-969 review's defect returning: `condition_plan._RECIPIENT` maps
`TITLE` and `ATTORNEY` to the same `title_attorney` email, so offering both puts two labels on one draft
and either choice writes the same email. The rule is now stated where it can be checked — when two
performers share a recipient key, at most one of them may be emailed — with a premise assertion that some
key really is shared, so it is not a test over an empty loop.

### The questions

1. **A day word in lower case.** Measured, and it is fine: `fact_warnings` searches both the before and
   the after text with `re.I`, so "get the monday statement" accepts "Ask the borrower for the Monday
   statement" and the step is kept. The control holds too — with no day word in her words or the
   lender's, the same step is left out naming Monday. Both are now pinned, because the flags that make it
   true are in another module and nothing here would notice if one were dropped.
2. **Several adds at once.** Finding 1, in both halves.
3. **The prompt's NPI scope.** Narrower than the reading on the condition's side: the reading sends the
   lender's text AND the underwriter notes (`condition_reading`), while this sends the lender's text and
   the reading's `summary`, which is itself derived from that text. The genuinely new channel is HER 500
   characters, which can hold anything she types — the same shape as "Polish with AI", and not something
   the field list can constrain. Logs carry counts only, verified: `condition_describe_proposed` logs two
   integers, and the two failure paths log a bare event name.
4. **The question block dropping her text.** Finding 2 — the sentence was right and the screen took the
   words away as it said it. What it still does not do is carry them into the note, which needs
   `render_question` to accept her words and is a design question for the owner rather than a defect.

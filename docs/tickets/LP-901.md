## What was asked

Make the UI palette flexible enough that recolouring the app is a matter of changing the palette, and
the colour combination (which colour is used where) is just as easy to change.

## Where it stood

The components were already clean: after LP-UI-004 nothing names a colour except through a role
(`bg-primary`, `text-warning`) — zero palette-scale classes, zero hex, zero inline style colours.
What stood in the way was underneath them. Every role in `globals.css` held a literal HSL triple, and
a `.dark` block restated all thirty:

| Value (light) | Restated as |
| --- | --- |
| `187.9 67.9% 22.0%` (petrol) | `--primary`, `--info`, `--ring` |
| `188.6 17.9% 7.6%` (near-black) | `--foreground`, `--card-`, `--popover-`, `--secondary-`, `--accent-foreground` |
| `0 0% 100%` (white) | eight roles |

There was no "palette" to swap. There were sixty values, some of which had to move together.

## The split

**Palette** — `app/palettes/petrol.css`. Raw colours only, for light (`:root`) and dark (`.dark`):

```
--neutral-1 … --neutral-12   a ramp numbered by JOB, not lightness
--brand, --brand-contrast    the one accent, and what sits on it
--red / --green / --amber / --violet (+ -contrast each)
```

**Roles** — `app/globals.css`, written **once**, no dark block:

```css
--primary: var(--brand);
--muted-foreground: var(--neutral-10);
--warning: var(--amber);
```

So the two things you might want to change live in two places:

- **Recolour the app** → edit a palette (a second palette and a switcher follow in LP-902).
- **Change which colour plays which part** (headings in brand, cards the same as the page) → edit a role.

### Why the neutral ramp is numbered by job

| Step | Job | Light | Dark |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | app background | 98.6% | 5.7% |
| 2 | raised surface (card) | 100% | 8.2% |
| 3 | overlay (popover) | 100% | 9.6% |
| 4 | subtle fill (muted, secondary) | 95.7% | 11.0% |
| 5 | hover fill (shadcn `accent`) | 93.1% | 13.7% |
| 6 | hairline (`border`) | 89.8% | 15.1% |
| 7 | loading placeholder | 89.6% | 17.8% |
| 8 | strong hairline | 81.6% | 20.0% |
| 9 | control border (≥3:1) | 54.5% | 39.8% |
| 10 | quiet text (≥4.5:1) | 41.0% | 56.5% |
| 11 | secondary text | 29.8% | 72.5% |
| 12 | primary text | 7.6% | 92.4% |

(Lightness shown; each step also has its own hue and saturation.)

Step 10 *is* quiet text in both themes, so `--muted-foreground: var(--neutral-10)` is right in both,
and the role layer needs no `.dark` block. A lightness scale (`gray-500`) means the opposite thing in
dark and would have forced a second role block — the duplication this ticket exists to remove.

Twelve steps, like Radix Colors' scales, but not the same contract. A Radix scale is ordered by
lightness. This one is ordered only by job: in dark, lightness climbs from 1 to 12, but in light the
page (step 1, 98.6%) is darker than the card and popover above it (steps 2 and 3, both white), and
steps 2 and 3 are the same colour. Nothing may assume step *n* is lighter or darker than step *n + 1*.

## Verified identical

Nothing on screen may change, so this was checked numerically rather than by eye. A script parsed the
**old** `globals.css` (at `54cb476f`, this commit's parent) and resolved every **new** role through
`petrol.css`:

```
60 colour roles compared across both themes, 0 differ
```

Then in a real browser (headless Chromium against `next dev`), `getComputedStyle` on `<html>`: petrol
light `--primary` = `187.9 67.9% 22%`, body `rgb(251, 252, 252)` = `hsl(180 14.3% 98.6%)`. And in the
production build the palette blocks are all present — the palettes sit outside any `@layer`, where
Tailwind tree-shakes nothing (the failure AMENDMENTS A4 recorded for the old `.dark` block).

## Contrast is computed now, not claimed

`globals.css` used to say *"every text tone clears 4.5:1 against its own ground in BOTH themes …
Verified, not assumed."* It was true — and it was a sentence, true of one set of values, that would
have stayed on screen after the next palette arrived.

`app/palettes/palettes.test.ts` resolves every role through every palette, in both themes, and
computes WCAG 2.x ratios for the pairs the UI actually draws:

| Pair | Floor | Petrol, tightest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `foreground` / `foreground-2` / `muted-foreground` and every status/accent text, on each of background, card, popover, muted, secondary, accent | 4.5:1 | 4.60 (light `muted-foreground` on `accent`) |
| each `X-foreground` on `bg-X`, for 11 fills | 4.5:1 | 4.86 (light, on `muted`) |
| text on a translucent fill, over background and card: `text-X` on its `bg-X/10` chip, `text-primary` on the drop zone's `bg-primary/15`, and the `/90` and `/80` hovers (review) | 4.5:1 | 4.77 (light `warning` chip, over background) |
| `input` and `ring` on background and card | 3:1 | 3.20 (light `input` on background) |

`/10` because it is the chip tint (`status-token.tsx` `CHIP`) and the heaviest resting tint a status
hue takes: 44 status fills at `/10` and 21 at `/5`, which is lighter and so reads better
(`bg-{destructive,danger,success,warning,info,ai}/N` under `app/`, `components/`, `lib/`).

It also fails a palette that is **missing a colour** (which would otherwise render that colour from
the default and look like it worked), a block under any other selector, a non-HSL value, `border` equal
to `input` *after* resolution, and any colour role holding a literal instead of `var(--…)`.

### Mutations — each one fails the test

| Mutation | Fails |
| --- | --- |
| delete `--amber` from petrol's dark block | vocabulary (dark) and contrast (dark) |
| lighten light `--neutral-10` to 60% | contrast (light) |
| `--warning: 37.8 89.4% 29.6%` in globals.css | role-is-a-reference, contrast, and the control |

## What moved out of `tailwind.config.test.ts`

Its "`%s` is defined in `.dark`" and "`border` ≠ `input`" checks compared raw strings in
`globals.css`. There is no `.dark` block there now, and comparing `var(--neutral-6)` with
`var(--neutral-9)` can never fail even if a palette gives them the same value. Both now live in
`palettes.test.ts`, against resolved colours, per palette, per theme. The "every referenced var is
defined in `:root`" check stays where it was.

## The ledger assets

`docs/design/ledger/assets/globals.css` and `tailwind.config.ts` must match the shipped files
(`ledger-assets.test.ts`). They were byte-identical before the change, so they are copied, and
`assets/palettes/petrol.css` is added with its own pair: the asset `globals.css` is roles only now,
and not a drop-in without the colours it points at. Whether those reference copies should exist at
all now that the Ledger redesign has shipped is a separate question, not answered here.

## Assumptions and limits

- **Palette and theme apply to `<html>` only.** Roles are declared on `:root` and inherited as
  resolved values, so a `.dark` on an inner element would not re-theme that subtree. Nothing does
  that today; it is written into `globals.css` and ADR-402.
- **`MEASURED_SAFE` in `a11y-contrast.test.ts`** (`text-primary/80` "5.10:1 on bg-card") is still a
  hand-measured Petrol number. Faded text is a different question from palette contrast and is left
  as it was.
- **Shadows** still use Tailwind's built-in black; out of scope (research step 5).
- Values were rewritten without trailing zeros (`22.0%` → `22%`) — the same number.

## Review — REVIEWED

**The invariant holds.** I re-checked it independently, from the files rather than from the build's
script, and at three levels:

1. **Declarations.** I parsed `globals.css` at `54cb476f` and resolved each new role through
   `petrol.css`: **60 of 60 identical**, compared as numbers. No role was added or dropped, every
   non-colour `:root` variable (radius, density, layout, confidence) is unchanged, and the old `.dark`
   block held colours only.
2. **What reaches the page.** `app/layout.tsx` is the only root layout and the only `<html>`: there is
   no `global-error.tsx` or second root that imports `globals.css` without the palette. Nothing in
   the tree sets `dark` on an inner element, where the new resolution would differ from the old. No
   JS reads the variables back (`getPropertyValue`), where `22%` for `22.0%` could matter.
3. **The build.** I ran an independent `next build` (exit 0, 212s on the Pi). The shipped stylesheet
   has all 22 palette colours under both `:root` and `.dark`, and `--primary: var(--brand)` among the
   roles.

I also recomputed the contrast minima in "Contrast is computed now" with a separate HSL→RGB conversion
(Python's `colorsys`, not the test's formula). All four figures match: 4.60, 4.86, 4.77, 3.20.

### 1. Nothing checked the rule in the other direction

ADR-402, `globals.css` and `CLAUDE.md` all say components use roles and never a palette colour. The
test checked that every role points into the palette, but not that **only** roles do. Palette colours
are ordinary custom properties on `<html>`, so a component writing `bg-[hsl(var(--brand))]`
renders, follows palette swaps and looks correct. Meanwhile it has quietly opted out of "change
which colour plays which part by editing a role". This is the shape of every guard this codebase
has learned it needs: a rule with nothing checking it holds until the first time it doesn't.

**Fix:** `palettes.test.ts` › "only the role layer names a palette colour". It scans the tree
(derived from the directory, like `design-tokens.test.ts`, not from a list), skipping `globals.css`
and `app/palettes/`, and matches the palette vocabulary itself, so a colour added to a palette is
covered the day it lands. Comments are blanked rather than removed, so reported line numbers stay
true. Controls: the scan includes `status-token.tsx` and excludes the palette directory, and the
pattern matches `--brand` / `--neutral-10` but not `--primary`, `--brand-new` or `--reduce`.
**Mutation:** a probe component with `bg-[hsl(var(--brand))]` fails as
`components/zz-mutation-probe.tsx:1 --brand`. The tree is clean today.

This also closes half of the arbitrary-value blind spot the LP-900 review recorded, the half that
names a palette colour. Hex and `hsl()` literals in arbitrary values are still unguarded.

### 2. "The pairs the UI actually draws" left out the translucent ones

The chip (`bg-x/10 text-x`) was the only translucent fill checked, but the tree draws more text on
translucent fills of the same hue:

| pair | where | Petrol, tightest |
|---|---|---|
| `text-primary` on `bg-primary/15` | `mismo-upload.tsx`, `document-dropzone.tsx` (drop zones) | 5.69 (dark, card) |
| `text-primary-foreground` on `bg-primary/90` | `Button` default hover | 6.12 (dark, background) |
| `text-destructive-foreground` on `bg-destructive/90` | `Button` destructive hover | 4.96 (light, card) |
| `text-primary-foreground` on `bg-primary/80` | `Badge` default hover | 5.03 (dark, background) |

All pass on Petrol, so nothing changes on screen. But a second palette with a slightly lighter red
would pass every solid and chip check and fail the destructive button's hover. The chip list is now a
`TINTED` list, and all four pairs are added over background and card. **Mutation:** raising light
`--red` to 47.5% fails `text-destructive-foreground on bg-destructive/90` (4.35–4.37:1).

**Recorded, not fixed:** `Badge`'s `destructive` variant hovers at `/80`, which is **4.13:1** in Petrol
light. It has no caller today, so it is not in the list. Its first caller should fix the variant.

### 3. Claims corrected

- **"62 of the app's 108 status-fill opacities"** (test comment and this ticket) does not reproduce
  under any pattern I tried. The count under the status hues is 44 at `/10` and 21 at `/5`, and the
  comment now names the pattern and makes the claim that matters: `/10` is the heaviest resting tint,
  so it bounds `/5`.
- **"the same shape as Radix Colors' 12-step scales … a consistent order in both themes"** is not
  true in light: step 1 (98.6%) is darker than steps 2 and 3 (white), and 2 equals 3. Rewritten to say
  the ramp is ordered by job only, and that nothing may assume the lightness order.
- **`petrol.css`** said "every palette" defines its names under `:root` and `.dark`. Only the default
  does; any other palette uses `data-palette` selectors, which the test enforces. LP-902 is the next
  person to read that comment with a second palette in hand. It also claimed "every text/ground pair"
  is computed, and now says "the pairs the UI draws". The asset copy is updated to match.
- **ADR-402** lists the translucent pairs and the reverse guard.
- **`docs/design/ledger/README.md`** still listed `assets/globals.css` as a "drop-in replacement"
  and did not list `assets/palettes/petrol.css`. Both are fixed. This ticket's own ledger-assets
  section already said the asset is no longer a drop-in.
- The scope line omitted the docs the commit changed, and "from `HEAD`" now names the commit.

### Not changed

- `CLAUDE.md`'s colour bullet (this ticket's) is accurate, and its "never a palette name" is now
  enforced rather than stated. I did not edit it. The stale "biome/tsc/build" CI line is left for the
  user, as asked.

## What this is

Five decisions written down **before** LP-904 writes a migration, so the schema is designed once
rather than discovered. Everything here is a decision that would be expensive to reverse after tables
exist: whether a condition is its own row, what may close one, which columns are NPI, whether a
condition can become a finding, and how a lender's codes are keyed.

## Why it is its own ticket

Each of these is load-bearing on a column in LP-904, and three of them are only visible as a
*constraint* rather than a feature:

| ADR | The column it decides |
|---|---|
| 403 — a condition is its own entity | that `conditions` exists at all, rather than `needs_items` gaining an origin |
| 404 — only the lender clears | `prep_status` **and** `lender_status`, and `completeness` on the round |
| 405 — condition text is NPI | which columns the `readonly.*` views drop |
| 406 — a condition never becomes a finding | the absence of a column, and a Stage 3 link in the other direction |
| 407 — codes are lender-scoped | `(lender_id, code)` unique, `String(16)` keeping leading zeros, and `canonical_lender_key` |

## Decisions

**ADR-403 — a condition is its own entity.** A condition has a lender, a round, verbatim text owned by
the lender, a template code, a bucket and eventually a verdict; a need has none of those. They answer
different questions and come apart constantly — one condition asks for three documents, another asks
for no document at all. Conditions create needs (Stage 3); nothing creates a condition but a sheet or
a processor typing one.

**ADR-404 — only the lender clears, and a partial source never does.** Two tracks: our preparation and
the lender's answer. Absence is evidence only when the thing absent was in a list claiming to be
complete — which is the whole content of `completeness`, and why the paste dialog asks before it
accepts and defaults to *"just some"*.

**ADR-405 — condition text, headers and event details are NPI.** Ordinary queryable columns (they are
diffed, searched and fingerprinted — `EncryptedString` is non-deterministic and cannot be indexed or
compared, so encrypting them would make the feature's central operation impossible), protected at rest
and in transit, and **dropped from the `readonly.*` views** exactly as `documents.full_text` is. No
real condition sheet enters the repository.

**ADR-406 — a condition never becomes a finding.** Findings are rule-derived and the reconciler owns
their lifecycle; a row it must not retire has no business in the table it sweeps. A finding raised by a
condition's document links *back* to the condition.

**ADR-407 — lender codes are lender-scoped.** Always `(lender, code)`; leading zeros kept, because the
code is an identifier printed on a document and not a number.

### The one decision that came from a question, not the spec

The spec anticipated a STOP AND ASK at LP-910: *"match by lender name/slug; STOP AND ASK if there is no
reliable way to identify UWM and Champions."* The survey found there is none — `lenders` is
company-scoped with a slug unique only per company (ADR-045), chosen by each processing company, so a
seed keyed on a slug matches nothing on some tenants and the wrong row on others.

**Answered by the product owner, 2026-09-23: option 1.** `lenders` gains a nullable
**`canonical_lender_key`** (`"uwm"`, `"champions"`), admin-set, and the LP-910 seed matches on it. It
is recorded in ADR-407's consequences rather than as a sixth ADR, because it is the mechanism that
makes `(lender, code)` seedable across tenants rather than a separate decision.

⚠️ **Consequence for the build order:** `canonical_lender_key` is a `lenders` column, so it is added in
**LP-904's** migration beside `mortgagee_clause`, `condition_upload_cutoff` and
`condition_handling_notes` — not in LP-910, which only reads it. A lender with the key unset is not an
error: its codes arrive as `OBSERVED_UNMAPPED`, which is the same path any unknown code takes.

## Vocabulary

`docs/glossary.md` **already had** a `### Conditions` section defining *Condition*, *PTD*, *PTF*,
*Routine condition*, *Should-have-caught condition* and *UW Round*. The seven Phase 4.5 terms were
**appended to it** rather than written as a second section, and each new term that touches an existing
one says so rather than competing with it — most importantly **Round**, which is the *record* of a
**UW Round**: the cycle is the event, the round is the sheet it produced.

Added: *Condition sheet*, *Round*, *Lender code*, *Bucket*, *Underwriter note*, *Source*,
*Completeness*.

## Boundary diagram

`docs/phases/phase4.5-boundaries.md` — the spec's mermaid diagram, plus two things it does not carry:
a table giving the question each of condition / need / action / finding answers and who ends it, and an
explicit list of **the arrows that do not exist** (need → condition, condition → finding, finding →
condition, global code → condition). The absent edges are what a later ticket is tempted to add.

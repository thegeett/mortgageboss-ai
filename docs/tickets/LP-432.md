A READ-ONLY scoping pass over the two rules still reachable without new document capability: the **pay-stub-only

**nothing else was changed** (no spec, producer, table, fixture, activation, or test). Each was traced against

five rules abandoned) and a rule whose **operand doesn't exist** (PC-7 / AS-8 / IN-6 / PC-3, four Phase-0 stops).

**Verdicts (per the taxonomy):**

| rule | verdict | one line |

## PART A — the pay-stub-only documentation rule → `write-now`

**Priya's exact answer:** *"W-2 or 1099 is required to show 2 year history."*

### A1 — what she actually ruled (her wording ≠ the question asked)

The question put to her was a **recency** one — *"how recent must a pay stub be before you'd require a W-2 or
1099?"* She answered something **different and stronger**: pay stubs alone **cannot establish a 2-year history**;
only a W-2 or 1099 can. Two readings:
- **The recency reading** (the question asked): a pay stub past some age triggers a W-2/1099 requirement. **Her
  wording does not support this** — she named no age.
- **The documentation-sufficiency reading** (her wording): **if a borrower's 2-year history rests only on pay
  stubs, require a W-2 or 1099.** ✅ This is what she wrote.

Taking her wording literally (the LP-427 discipline — her labels meant what she wrote, not what was asked), it is
the documentation-sufficiency rule. It is exactly the **B12 separate documentation standard** her LP-393-6 ruling
carved out (*"B12 — pay-stub-only; history exists, the W-2/1099 ask is a separate check"* →
*"pay-stub-only → require a W-2/1099 before using the income"*), the **sibling of IN-15** (the **B14**
terminated-employment check, LP-430).

### A2 — the redundancy trace (the likely stop — does NOT trigger)

| live rule | what it actually does (from its spec) | delivers her ruling? |
|---|---|---|
| **IN-6** (pay-stub ↔ W-2 employer coverage) | reads `income.employer_coverage`; fires `needs_review` on `uncovered` (an employer on one document type but not the other). **Its applicability is `income.employer_coverage ne one_sided`** — and `one_sided` = *"the borrower lacks pay stubs OR W-2s"* → a pay-stub-only borrower is `one_sided` → IN-6 is **`not_applicable`**. | **NO.** IN-6's "vice versa" leg is about employer-NAME coverage across two PRESENT document types; it explicitly **scopes OUT** the case where one type is ABSENT. A borrower with pay stubs and no W-2 makes IN-6 not_applicable — it never fires. This is the opposite of a duplicate. |
| **IN-11** (has 2-year history) | judges whether 2 years of history EXIST (`income.has_2yr_history`) | **NO.** Priya's B12/B14 ruling: the documentation standard is a **SEPARATE** check (that is why IN-15 exists). IN-11 judges *existence*; this judges the *documents that establish it*. |
| **IN-15** (terminated-employment documentation) | fires when a terminated job (past VOE end date) has no subsequent pay stub | **NO.** Same FAMILY (documentation checks off `has_2yr_history`), DIFFERENT trigger (a termination) and DIFFERENT documents. A sibling, not a duplicate. |
| **IN-8 / IN-9** (VOE / offer-letter presence) | per-subject document-presence checks | **NO** — the closest SHAPE precedent, different documents. |

**Conclusion: NOT a duplicate.** IN-6 in particular *excludes exactly* the case this rule targets. The
redundancy stop does not trigger.

### A3 — buildability (deterministic, no calibration)

- **What it reads:** does this borrower have a **W-2 or a 1099** among their attributed income documents? Both
  are extracted document TYPES — `w2` and `1099` are both Tier-1 income/employment types
  (`app/documents/catalog.py`). ⚠️ The 1099 EXTRACTOR is unvalidated (`form_1099.py`: built with no sample
  documents), but the rule reads document-type **presence**, not extracted 1099 fields (the IN-8/IN-9
  discipline) — so it works on the classifier's type label alone. Her ruling is **not** narrowed to W-2-only.
- **The relation class:** per-borrower document-type presence → the established `_borrower_attributed_documents`
  + `entry.document_type` shape (the `income.employer_coverage` recipe already filters a borrower's docs to
  `("pay_stub", "w2")`, `derived.py` ~line 1187). **Native to the derived-producer machinery — no new
  mechanism.**
- **Deterministic → no calibration** (ADR-334): document-type presence is a fact → a derived per-borrower tag +
  a `no-ai-dependency` rule that could **activate immediately** (the IN-15 shape).
- **The boundary:** its reason (*"require a W-2 or 1099 to establish the 2-year history"*) is distinct from
  IN-6's (employer-name coverage) and IN-11's (whether 2 years exist) — no two-findings-one-gap noise.

### A3 — the ONE open design question (flagged for Priya, not a stop)

The exact **trigger** is a scoping decision, because her ruling ties the requirement to *establishing the 2-year
history*:
- **Option 1 (fully deterministic, no-calibration):** fire when a borrower has pay-stub income but **no W-2 and
  no 1099**. Pure document-type presence. Risk: may **over-fire** on a borrower otherwise documented (e.g. a
  VOE) who is not relying on pay stubs for history.
- **Option 2 (gated on `income.has_2yr_history`):** fire only when the 2-year history is actually being claimed
  on pay stubs. Matches her intent precisely, but reads the AI `has_2yr_history` tag → **not** no-ai-dependency
  (it inherits IN-11's calibration), and is a two-tag rule.

**Recommendation:** write it as **Option 1** (deterministic, immediate activation, the IN-15 shape) and flag the
over-fire scope to Priya — narrowing to Option 2 later is a one-line applicability change. Same "the
documentation-sufficiency qualifier is Priya's, flagged not silently chosen" pattern as ADR-339.

### PART A verdict: **`write-now`**

The ticket would: add a derived per-borrower tag `income.two_year_history_documented` (or similar — enum:
`documented` [a W-2 or 1099 is attributed] / `pay_stub_only` [income docs but no W-2/1099] / `not_applicable`
[no income docs] / `unknown`), the IN-15 recipe shape; a per-borrower `no-ai-dependency` rule firing on
`pay_stub_only`; a standalone fixture (fire / satisfy / no-income n/a); it activates (ACTIVE 35 → 36). One
Priya-facing question: the trigger scope (Option 1 vs 2).

## PART B — PE-1 (jumbo / conforming) → `needs-input` + `needs-mechanism`

**Priya's exact rule:**
```
applicable_limit = FHFA_limit(year, property_county_fips, number_of_units)
if   original_loan_amount > applicable_limit: jumbo_non_conforming
elif original_loan_amount > baseline_limit:   high_balance_conforming
else:                                          standard_conforming
```

### B1 — the three operands (one is missing)

| operand | exists as a typed-core tag today? | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **`original_loan_amount`** | ✅ **YES** — `loan.amount` | MISMO `put("loan.amount", loan_file.loan_amount)` (`mismo_section.py`); already read by the LTV/MI calcs |
| **`number_of_units`** | ✅ **YES** — `property.financed_unit_count` | MISMO `put("property.financed_unit_count", property_.financed_unit_count)` (`mismo_section.py`) |
| **`property_county_fips`** | ❌ **NO** — the doubtful operand, confirmed absent | the property MISMO fields are address_line(2) / city / **state** / **postal_code** / type / occupancy / values / attachment_type / construction_method / financed_unit_count — **no county, no FIPS.** There is city+state+ZIP but no county name and no county FIPS code anywhere (MISMO or any extractor). |

So **two of three operands exist**; **`property_county_fips` is the stop.** A FIPS code is a specific federal
county identifier — there is neither a county NAME nor a FIPS code. Getting it needs one of: (a) a county
extracted from the appraisal (an extractor extension — the appraisal is a known but **unextracted** Tier-2 type,
`catalog.py`; appraisals carry the county), or (b) a `state + postal_code → county → FIPS` mapping (another
maintained dataset — ZIP-to-county is many-to-many at edges, so not exact). **Either way, a missing input, not a
rule.**

### B2 — the FHFA table is a MAINTAINED DATASET, not a threshold → `needs-mechanism`

`FHFA_limit(year, county_fips, units)` is a **3-dimensional lookup table**. The spec's `reference_values.values`
is a flat **`dict[str, str]`** of scalars (`specs.py`), read one key at a time by a `reference` operand — it
**cannot hold a year × county × units table.** So the table is a **new mechanism**, not a spec reference value.

And — decisively — it is **not a threshold Priya signs off once; it is data with an update cadence.** FHFA
**republishes the limits every January.** That is a different kind of obligation than a Priya sign-off:
- **Who updates it each year?** A stale table **silently misclassifies** — a loan conforming under this year's
  limit reads as jumbo under last year's, or vice versa. There is no precedent in this codebase for a
  maintained, versioned reference dataset (the existing `reference_values` are static scalars validated once;
  `max_loan.py` carries only an unset `loan_limit: Decimal | None` starter — *"validate with Priya — the limits
  change annually + are county-specific"* — never populated).
- **What happens to a file evaluated under last year's table?** A versioning/as-of question the mechanism must
  answer (the limit that applied at application vs at evaluation).

**This is the real weight of PE-1: not a rule, but a maintained-reference-data subsystem + its update process.**

### B3 — three outcomes, only one is a finding

| classification | condition | maps to |
|---|---|---|
| `standard_conforming` | loan ≤ baseline_limit | **satisfied** — a normal conforming loan |
| `high_balance_conforming` | baseline < loan ≤ applicable_limit | **satisfied (distinct)** — a LEGITIMATE conforming loan (high-cost county), **NOT a finding**; it may carry program overlays but is in-program |
| `jumbo_non_conforming` | loan > applicable_limit | **fired** — exceeds the conforming limit → out of the Conv+FHA program scope (PE-5 marks Jumbo `out_of_scope`) |

PE-1 fires **only** on jumbo. The middle case must NOT be flagged (the LP-430 D4 discipline — never accuse a
legitimate file). PE-1's own catalog description is *"Eligibility matrix incl. CONFORMING LIMIT check (the jumbo
catch)"* — the jumbo catch is the finding; the high-balance case is informational at most.

### B4 — scope + siblings + `baseline_limit`

- PE-1 catalog: *"Conventional eligibility — Program — calculative — deterministic_bookend+ai — Eligibility
  matrix incl. CONFORMING LIMIT check (the jumbo catch); reference values in spec; AI assembles."*
  `threshold_needs_signoff = true`.
- **Siblings:** PE-2 (FHA case number), PE-3 (FHA MIP), PE-4 (FHA property condition) are FHA-specific; **PE-5
  (VA/USDA/Jumbo/Non-QM) is `out_of_scope`** — so a jumbo loan is out of the Conv+FHA program. None covers the
  conforming-limit check; it is PE-1's.
- **`baseline_limit`** is the national FLOOR of the same FHFA table (the table's minimum), not a separate input —
  it comes from the same dataset (so the mechanism supplies both).

### PART B verdict: **`needs-input` (`property_county_fips`) + `needs-mechanism` (the FHFA table + maintenance)**

Two independent, hard blockers. Not write-now.

## THE ROLL-UP

**Is Track 1 worth 1 ticket, 2, or 0? → ONE rule ticket.**
- **The pay-stub-only rule is `write-now`** — the one reachable rule left. It should be the next write-it ticket.
- **PE-1 yields ZERO rule tickets right now** — it is blocked on (a) a missing operand and (b) a
  maintained-dataset mechanism. Building it now would repeat the "operand doesn't exist" failure. What it
  spawns instead is **infrastructure work**, sized below.

**Build order (cheapest first):**
1. **The pay-stub-only rule** — small, deterministic, no new mechanism, activates immediately. Mirror IN-15.
2. **PE-1's prerequisites** (separate, larger, schedulable) — see below. PE-1 the rule is trivial *once they
   exist* (a `loan.amount` vs `limit[county, units, year]` compare); the cost is entirely the prerequisites.

**What PE-1 would need (sized, so it can be scheduled or deferred honestly):**
- **County / FIPS (needs-input):** extract the county from the appraisal (a new Tier-2 extractor — the appraisal
  is a known-but-unextracted type) OR add a `state+ZIP → county → FIPS` mapping dataset. **Medium** (an
  extractor + goldens, or a mapping dataset).
- **The FHFA limit table (needs-mechanism):** a NEW maintained-reference-data subsystem — a versioned
  year × county_fips × units table, a loader, an as-of resolution, and an **annual update process with an
  owner.** **Large** — and the maintenance obligation is the crux, not the initial load.
- **The rule itself:** small, once the above exist (three outcomes, fires only on jumbo).

**Shared work with already-planned things:**
- **The appraisal extractor** would supply PE-1's **county** AND independently unblock other property rules; the
  county/FIPS need **overlaps** with an appraisal-extraction ticket. If an appraisal extractor is built for
  another reason, PE-1's county input comes largely for free.
- The FHFA table is PE-1-specific (no overlap) — its own obligation.

# LP-465 — Add two rule-relevant catalog types: `temporary_buydown_agreement` and `uscis_notice_of_action`

Both types appeared in the bench as `unknown` — correctly *named* by the classifier,
with nowhere to route. Both feed verification rules. This ticket adds them.

Adding a catalog type is four coordinated pieces:

1. **Catalog entry** — `app/documents/catalog.py` (`CATALOG: slug → (Tier, DocumentCategory)`)
2. **Classifier indicator** — `app/ai/classification_prompt.py` (`DOCUMENT_TYPE_INDICATORS`).
   CI-locked: a test asserts every catalog type has an indicator and vice versa —
   **both or neither**.
3. **JSON spec** — `docs/schema-specs/NNN-slug.json`
4. **Generate** the extractor module + prompt + test from the spec.

### A2 — distinguishing indicators (the two confusable families)

**`temporary_buydown_agreement`** (vs the note / Loan Estimate / Closing Disclosure):
> *a Temporary Buydown Agreement — a standalone agreement (often titled "Temporary
> Buydown Agreement") that funds a temporary reduction of the borrower's interest rate
> and payment for the first 1–2 years (a 2-1 or 1-0 buydown), via a subsidy deposited
> into a separate escrow/custodial account. It carries a per-period **payment schedule**
> of reduced rates, borrower payments, and the monthly subsidy applied. It is NOT the
> note, the Loan Estimate, or the Closing Disclosure — it is the separate subsidy
> agreement, and the note's own rate/payment appear only as the un-bought-down baseline.*

**`uscis_notice_of_action`** (vs `visa_documentation`, `permanent_resident_card`,
`work_visa_ead_card`, `passport`):
> *a USCIS Notice of Action (Form I-797A / I-797B / I-797C) — government correspondence
> from U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services receipting or **approving** a petition.
> It shows a receipt number, a case type (e.g. I-129), notice/received/validity dates, a
> petitioner and beneficiary, and often a tear-off I-94 block. It is a **notice about a
> petition**, NOT a travel visa, NOT a green card, NOT an EAD card, NOT a passport — the
> form itself states "THIS NOTICE IS NOT A VISA." Route the I-797 here even when it
> concerns a work classification (H-1B etc.); the physical EAD **card** is
> `work_visa_ead_card`, and a visa **stamp** is `visa_documentation`.*

### A3 — recommendation on `visa_documentation` (do **not** delete)

The four misroutes the ticket flagged (187/188/240/242) are **all actually I-797A
Notices of Action** that landed on `visa_documentation` / `work_visa_ead_card` because
there was no USCIS type to catch them. After LP-465, the new type + indicator should
pull them over.

**Recommendation: keep `visa_documentation`, but flag it for a future coverage review.**
It is the correct home for a genuine **visa stamp / travel visa** (a distinct artifact
from both an I-797 notice and an EAD card). The concern is that in *this* corpus its
current members may be entirely misrouted I-797 notices — i.e. it could end up with few
or zero true members once LP-465 lands. That is a "does this type earn its place"
question, and the right answer is to **re-check after** the re-classification in Phase C
(if a real visa stamp routes to it, it earns its place; if not, that's a separate cleanup
ticket). **Not deleting it in this ticket** — removing a type is out of scope here and
would be premature before the Phase-C evidence.

### A4 — `payment_schedule` shape

- **Per-period (year ranges: "1-12", "13-24"), not per-month.** Confirmed on 067. A
  2-1 buydown → 2 rows; a 1-0 → 1 row; a 3-2-1 → 3 rows. Small list.
- **One shape serves both funding variants.** Lender-paid (067) and seller/builder-paid
  agreements carry the *same* schedule columns; only `funding_party` (and which party's
  money seeds the subsidy account) differs. No per-variant schema split — `funding_party`
  is a scalar, the schedule is identical. (068 is the other lender-paid sample; the
  seller-paid variant, if present in-corpus, uses the same DocMagic schedule grid.)

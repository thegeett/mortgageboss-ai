# bug-012 — we add rental income the guide no longer lets us add

- **Status:** Step 1 (the interim) shipped in `82b2a4e`, reviewed. Step 2 blocked on extraction. **One decision open with the user** — see *Open — a decision, not a defect*.
- **Date:** 2026-09-05
- **Trigger:** Fannie Mae announcement **SEL-2026-08**, dated 02 September 2026
- **Direction of the error:** **over-qualifies.** We inflate qualifying income and understate DTI.

## What the announcement changed

SEL-2026-08 puts a 12-month property-management experience requirement in force:

> Lenders may only use positive rental income for qualifying income if the borrower(s) has at least
> 12 months of property management experience.
>
> The lender may only use qualifying rental income to offset the PITIA when the borrower(s) has no
> prior property management experience, or less than 12 months of experience.

So positive net rental splits into two treatments that were previously one:

| Borrower | Positive net rental |
|---|---|
| ≥ 12 months experience | added to qualifying income (what we do today) |
| < 12 months, or none | **offset only** — it may reduce the subject's PITIA and no further |

## What we do

`services/dti.py:641`:

```python
positive = rental.net_monthly > 0
...
if positive:
    income_items = [*income_items, *rental_items]
    income_lines = [*income_lines, *rental_lines]
else:
    debt_items = [*debt_items, *rental_items]
```

Unconditional. There is no experience test anywhere in the calculator, because until 02 September
there was nothing to test. Every borrower is treated as if experienced.

For a first-time landlord this adds income the guide says may not be added, which understates DTI
and can qualify a borrower who does not qualify. It is the one direction this codebase refuses
everywhere else, and it is live today on every investment-property file.

## Why the real fix is blocked

Experience is established, per the announcement, by:

- the most recent 1040 with Schedules 1 and E supporting **365 Fair Rental Days**; or
- a business return with **Form 8825**; or
- where a property has been owned a year but Schedule E shows fewer than 365 days, a **12-month
  lease** supplementing the 1040, or **two years** of returns.

None of that is extractable today:

| Evidence | Status |
|---|---|
| Fair Rental Days | **Not captured.** `ScheduleEProperty` has `address`, `rents_received`, `total_expenses`, `net_income` — no day count. |
| Form 8825 | **No extractor.** It appears only in `schema_specs/059-business-federal-tax-returns.json` and a fixture. |
| 12-month lease supplementing the 1040 | Lease term is extractable (`lease_start_date` / `lease_end_date`), but the rule composing it with the 1040 does not exist. |
| Two years of returns | Returns are extractable; the two-year composition rule does not exist. |

So we cannot currently establish experience for **anyone** — which means we cannot currently
distinguish the two treatments from evidence.

## Proposed fix, in two steps

**Step 1 — interim, safe, small.** Until experience is establishable, do not add positive net rental
to income at all: cap the net at zero for the income path. A positive result contributes nothing; a
negative result still carries as a monthly obligation. That *is* "offset the PITIA only", which is
the treatment the guide mandates for the borrower we cannot yet distinguish.

This is deliberately the conservative half of the rule. It will understate qualifying income for an
experienced landlord, which shows up as a file that gates or ratios worse than it should — visible,
arguable, and correctable by a processor. The alternative error is invisible and over-qualifies.

The gate/derivation text must say which treatment was applied and why, or a processor cannot tell a
capped figure from a genuinely small one.

**Step 2 — the real test, once the evidence exists.** Capture Fair Rental Days on
`ScheduleEProperty`, add a Form 8825 extractor, then a tag that answers "does this borrower have 12
months of property-management experience?" from the four permitted routes, and gate the income path
on it. Sizeable, and worth its own ticket once step 1 has stopped the bleeding.

## Open question for the domain expert

For a borrower who owns three rentals (the LF-ZE9N shape): **is the 12-month experience established
by any qualifying Schedule E, or must it be for the properties still owned?** This decides whether
the tag is per-borrower or per-property, which is a schema decision, not a wording one.

## Review (2026-09-05)

**The guideline text was verified independently against the live page**, not taken from the ticket.
`selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-3.8-01/rental-income` returns topic **B3-3.8-01, "General Rental
Income Information", effective 09/02/2026**, carrying both quoted sentences verbatim:

> "Lenders may only use positive rental income for qualifying income if the borrower(s) has at least
> 12 months of property management experience."
> "The lender may only use qualifying rental income to offset the PITIA when the borrower(s) has no
> prior property management experience, or less than 12 months of experience."

Topic number, title, date and both quotations check out exactly as written.

**For LP-641 (the citation sweep) — resolved, and it is ONE topic, not two.** The review round read
a *search-result title* as a second live topic: results list `…/sel/b3-3.1-08/rental-income` beside
`…/sel/b3-3.8-01/rental-income`, both captioned "General Rental Income Information (09/02/2026)".

Fetching the old path settles it. `selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/`**`b3-3.1-08`**`/rental-income`
returns a page whose own heading reads:

> B3-3.8-01, General Rental Income Information (09/02/2026)

The URL slug is stale; the document it serves is B3-3.8-01, and the page carries no
superseded/redirect notice to say so. So there is one canonical topic and two paths to it, and the
sweep has one target — **B3-3.8-01** for general rental income, **B3-3.8-02** for the 75% factor and
the Form 1007 / 1025 requirement.

The trap is worth keeping: a stale citation is *followable*, and lands on the renumbered topic
without complaint. Clicking a cite is not evidence it is current, and a search result's caption is
not evidence of what a topic is numbered.

**The offset-by-doing-nothing claim holds, and here is the check.** With `net > 0`, the guide's
treatment is: rent offsets the PITIA, excess discarded. The code excludes the subject's PITIA from
housing entirely and adds nothing to income. Both leave the subject contributing **zero** to housing
and **zero** to income, so they are equivalent — the excess is discarded either way. With `net < 0`
both carry `|net|` as an obligation. The model is consistent in both directions and the borrower is
not under-credited.

**One defect fixed.** The new exclusion reason ended "— and that offset is the PITIA exclusion
above". True of `dti.py`, false on the screen: `dti-calculator.tsx` renders *Gross monthly income*
first and *Housing payment* second, so a reason on an income line pointing "above" sends a processor
away from what it describes. It now names the housing payment instead of gesturing at a position,
with a test that fails on any direction word in that sentence — this module cannot know where the
frontend will put either section.

## Open — a decision, not a defect

**Nothing anywhere can set `experience_established = True`.** Not production, not a test. The
consequence is that on **every** investment-property file, a positive net rental is excluded from
qualifying income, an amount override cannot restore it (by design — see the structural exclusion),
and no affordance exists for a processor who has verified the experience to say so.

The direction is right: over-qualifying is the dangerous error and this removes it. But the interim
state is a blanket, un-overridable under-qualification of a whole loan category, and 12 months of
landlord experience is common rather than exceptional. A processor who knows the borrower qualifies
has no way to act on it until step 2 ships.

That is a business call about which error to carry and for how long, not an engineering one, and it
should be made deliberately rather than inherited from the interim.

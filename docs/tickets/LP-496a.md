### A3 — PE-3's basis and its credit tier

**The basis is confirmed, and it is now tier P, not S.** WebFetch could not parse HUD's PDF, but it
saved the 6.7MB file locally and `pdftotext` extracted 2.39MB of text, so the handbook was read
directly. Handbook 4000.1, **Effective 09/14/2015 | Last Revised 08/14/2019** (transmittal issued
2019-08-14, effective 2019-10-15) — that is the revision stamp inside the file HUD serves today at
`hud.gov/sites/dfiles/OCHCO/documents/4000.1hsgh.pdf`.

> "Under most programs, the maximum Mortgage is the lesser of the Nationwide Mortgage Limit for the
> area, or a percentage of the Adjusted Value. **For purchase transactions, the Adjusted Value is
> the lesser of: • purchase price less any inducements to purchase; or • the Property Value.**"

> "the Borrower must make a **Minimum Required Investment (MRI) of at least 3.5 percent of the
> Adjusted Value.**"

> "Minimum Required Investment (MRI) refers to the Borrower's contribution in cash or its equivalent
> required by Section 203(b)(9) of the National Housing Act, which represents at least 3.5 percent
> of the Adjusted Value of the Property."

The catalog rationale's "3.5% of price" is therefore wrong, as the ticket predicted. Divergence
recorded.

**The tiers are also tier P**, and the handbook's term is **Minimum Decision Credit Score (MDCS)**,
not "representative credit score":

> "If the Borrower's Minimum Decision Credit Score is… **at or above 580** → eligible for maximum
> financing. **between 500 and 579** → limited to a maximum LTV of 90%."
> "For purchase transactions, the maximum LTV is **96.5 percent** of the Adjusted Value."
> "The Borrower is **not eligible** for FHA-insured financing if the MDCS is **less than 500**."
> "the Mortgagee must select the **lowest** MDCS of the Borrower(s) with credit score(s)."

**Can the snapshot compute it?** Partly.

| input | availability |
|---|---|
| purchase price | `property.purchase_price` **12/19** |
| property value | `property.valuation_amount` **10/19**, `property.estimated_value` **15/19** |
| **inducements to purchase** | **0/19 — not representable anywhere** |
| MDCS | `score_equifax` / `score_experian` / `score_transunion` on `credit_report` — **1/19** |

So Adjusted Value is computable only as `min(purchase_price, property_value)`, with the
*"less any inducements"* leg unmodellable. PC-8 surfaces personal property but computes no
deduction (LP-493) and nothing else deducts either — confirmed, no other source. That is a
**conservative** error in one direction only: ignoring inducements *overstates* Adjusted Value,
which overstates the MRI requirement, so the rule can never clear a file it should catch on this
axis. It can raise a false condition, which must be said in the finding text.

The credit tier is the harder one: **18/19 files carry no score at all**, so PE-3 must abstain on
the tier rather than assume 580+.

## Phases B–E — what was built

### The two design decisions, and how each is pinned

**PE-1 abstains where the county would decide.** The limit is decidable at the two ends and genuinely
undecidable between them, so the rule answers at the ends and abstains in the middle:

| loan amount | verdict | why |
|---|---|---|
| at or below the baseline | `satisfied` | conforming in **every** county |
| above the high-cost ceiling | `fired` | over the limit in **every** county |
| between the two | `couldnt_check` | **only the county resolves it, and it is not in the snapshot** |

`test_the_county_dependent_band_abstains_and_never_clears` asserts both `is COULDNT_CHECK` and
`is not SATISFIED`, so a later widening of the comparison cannot quietly turn the band into a pass.
The Alaska case pins the special-area carve-out **by contrast**: the same $1,000,000 that abstains in
North Carolina is `satisfied` in Alaska, whose baseline is the national ceiling.

**PE-3 uses Adjusted Value, and the divergence from the catalog is pinned by the case that separates
the two bases.** A $400,000 price with a $360,000 appraisal and a $348,000 loan:

- against the **price** — investment $52,000 vs $14,000 required → **would clear**
- against the **Adjusted Value** — investment $12,000 vs $12,600 required → **fires**

The catalog's "3.5% of price" would clear a file that fails. Likewise the credit tier: the same
$20,000 investment clears 3.5% ($14,000) and fails 10% ($40,000), so
`test_a_missing_credit_score_abstains_rather_than_assuming_the_cheapest_tier` is built on a fixture
where the assumption **changes the verdict**, not merely one where it is unstated.

### Calibration — scenario fixtures, and why no rate was measured

Neither rule calls a model, so there is no self-consistency rate to measure and **no model spend at
any point in this ticket ($0)**. Both bars are `no-ai-dependency` with `load_bearing_ai_tags: []`, and
the gate runs on `input_resolves` alone. `validated: false` is the only legal value for that status —
recorded on both bars so it is not misread as a hold.

Verdicts are proven on **13 constructed scenario cases** exercising every branch, and the reason is
measured rather than assumed: no conventional file carries an amount near the limit, and of the four
FHA files three carry **zero** documents and the fourth carries two — none a purchase contract,
appraisal or credit report. A corpus derivation would have abstained on every case. Both rules
produce a full four-verdict spread (`satisfied` / `fired` / `couldnt_check` / `not_applicable`).

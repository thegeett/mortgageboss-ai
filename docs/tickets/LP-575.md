# LP-575 — DT-6 stops asking once the payoff question has been answered

**Status:** done

## The contradiction

DT-6 and DT-8 are about the **same debt** and recommend opposite things:

| | says | back-end DTI |
|---|---|---|
| DT-6 | the stated payment understates it — raise 3,186.00 to 4,148.28 | 65.90% |
| DT-8 | this lien is paid off at closing — remove it | 34.39% |

They are two branches of one question: is the obligation **retained past closing**, or **paid off at
it**? On LF-WCHG the answer is paid off — confirmed by hand with the resident domain expert — so
DT-6's remedy is the wrong branch. Left unscoped, a processor who applies DT-8 would still be looking
at DT-6 telling them to raise the payment they just removed.

DT-6 already knew the branch existed. Its own remedy text says *"If the property is under contract or
being paid off at closing, document that so the obligation is excluded on the correct basis."* It
simply had no way to see that the documenting had happened.

## `not_applicable`, not `satisfied`

The stated 3,186.00 and the billed 4,148.28 genuinely disagree, and always will. Marking that
`satisfied` would assert the figures reconcile — a false all-clear on a real discrepancy. What
changed is that the disagreement stopped **mattering for the ratio**, which is scope-false: the rule
is irrelevant to this subject's nature (§8), not passing on its merits.

## Shape

A third tag off the **same matcher**, never a second one. ADR-375 exists because two matchers let two
rules disagree about the same pair of documents; a separate lookup here could scope DT-6 out on the
strength of a *different* liability's marking than its payment comparison used. So
`_StatementMatch` gained one defaulted field carrying the matched liability's payoff marking, and
`reo.statement_liability_paid_off` reads it.

DT-6's applicability is now two conditions:

```yaml
- {tag: document.document_type, op: eq, value: mortgage_statement}
- {tag: reo.statement_liability_paid_off, op: ne, value: "yes"}
```

**`ne yes`, deliberately** — "no" *and* "unknown" both keep the rule in scope. An unmatched statement
has no marking to read; that is an abstain, and an abstain must never be laundered into a scope
exclusion. It stays in scope and resolves to DT-6's own `couldnt_check`.

## What this does not change

DT-6 still has **no Apply button**. `correct_liability_payment` — the action that would raise a
stated payment, with its undo and tests — is implemented in `finding_resolution.py` and **declared by
no spec**. So DT-6 can tell a processor to raise the payment but cannot do it for them. That is a
separate ticket, not this one.

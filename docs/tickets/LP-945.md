## Design

**VOE, by who reads it.**
- **Outward text** means an ask item's own wording (name, acceptable, label, email line, why) and a type's
  email wording, plus the display names, which re-asks and package notes use.
- IE-05's ask, "W-2, final pay stub or written VOE from the prior employer", is asked of the borrower. It
  now spells "written verification of employment".
- Display names: `verbal_voe` → "Verbal verification of employment"; `voe` → "Written verification of
  employment".
- IE-03 ("Verbal VOE call") and IE-06 ("Written VOE") are her own tasks, and their playbooks are hers,
  so they keep "VOE", as the owner allowed.

**Business existence: a new type, IE-08** (the library had none; the type existed only in the document
catalogue):
- its ask is the owner's wording, asked of the borrower;
- it takes the catalogue's business-existence type, a CPA letter or a business licence;
- its rule is Fannie Mae B3-3.1-04, "Within 120 calendar days before the note date.";
- the catalogue type's display name becomes the same wording, answering the expert's question.

The library grows to 56 types (its test bounds it at 40 to 60), and the review table is regenerated.

**The lender setting:** `lender_verifies_business_existence`, "Lender verifies business existence for
self-employed borrowers" on S3-11. When ticked, IE-08's step is "Lender is doing it" ("The lender verifies
it — its setting for this lender"), in the same way the final-inspection setting works for PA-03. It
defaults to off for every lender.

### (a) Outward text — traced, and one gate too narrow

I enumerated what each outward surface renders, rather than confirming the fields you swept:

| surface | what it renders from the library | covered? |
|---|---|---|
| borrower / party email lines | `library_item.email` (filled), else `_plain_line` = `item.name` + `item.acceptable` | yes |
| the grey *Why:* line | `library_item.why`, else `library_type.why` | yes |
| the package (to the **lender**) | `display_name(document_type)` and the document's own name, plus code-computed amounts; the note is the model's, from those facts. No rule note, no citation | yes — and the display-name test covers it |
| the borrower upload link | **nothing**: the page names no borrower, no amount and no needs list, by design | n/a |
| **the question to the underwriter** | `short = library_type.short or library_type.name` | **not by your sweep's gate** |

`render_question` (`condition_drafts.py:958`) renders the **type's** short-or-name for any condition
carrying an underwriter question. Your sweep reaches type-level text only *"when any item is asked"* — and
**19 of 56 types have no asked item at all**, including IE-03 and IE-06, the two you deliberately left
saying "VOE". An underwriter at the lender is outside her company, so by the owner's rule that text is
outward.

**No member today, and I checked rather than assuming:** no type's `short`, `name` or `why` contains "VOE"
anywhere in the library — IE-03's name is already "Verbal verification of employment", and only its *item*
is "Verbal VOE call", which the owner allowed. So the sweep's result is right; its scope is not what it
claims. Dropping the "when any item is asked" gate for the two type-level fields costs one condition in the
sweep and makes it cover the surface it exists for.

### (b) IE-08's performer and documents — two gaps between the owner's sentence and what the file can accept

The owner's ask names three routes: **"CPA letter, business licence, or regulator listing"**.

1. **The third route cannot arrive.** The item's documents are
   `[business_existence_verification_cpa_ltr_bus_lic, cpa_letter, business_license]`. A regulator listing
   is a printout or screenshot of a licensing board page; the classifier's nearest type is
   `miscellaneous_document`, *"the long-tail catch-all"*, which is not in that list — so `_takes` refuses
   it and the item never links it. The ask offers three ways and the file can receive two. Either add the
   catch-all (at the cost that the item then accepts anything long-tail), or accept that the listing is
   something **she** records rather than something the borrower sends — which is the same question as the
   performer.
2. **The performer follows from that.** `borrower` is defensible for the first two routes. But a regulator
   listing is the one a processor typically checks herself, and under LP-942's rule an ask addressed to her
   *is* her task. So the three routes do not share one performer, which is why this reads as an open
   question rather than a wrong answer. Worth putting back to the owner in one line: *is the regulator
   listing hers to check, or the borrower's to send?*

**One more, adjacent:** IE-08 declares **no checks**, so `_item_passes` can never complete it — `results and
all(...)` is falsy on an empty list — and every business-existence document needs her manual accept. That
is **normal here: 18 of 52 library items carry no checks** (TI-01, PA-03, PA-08, CR-12 …), so this is the
library's existing shape, not an anomaly. What makes it worth a sentence is that the owner's note states a
*checkable* rule — "within 120 calendar days before the note date" — and nothing enforces it, while IE-03
has `inside_voe_window` for exactly that shape. A date rule written into a note and into an email is a
claim the app does not verify.

### (c) No code maps to IE-08 — fine, and here is the denominator

reachable-only-through-the-reading is the library's existing normal for a fifth of its types, not a new
compromise, and IE-08 needs no special justification.

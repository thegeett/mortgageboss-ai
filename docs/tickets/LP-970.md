## What was built

### A route is the plan's own rules, run with one lender setting forced

`condition_plan.Route` has three kinds:
- **`step`** (LP-955's): the condition's own step.
- **`setting`:** the plan as it would be built with that lender setting on or off. The condition's step
  and each task's step are recomputed by build_plan's own rules, moved into functions that both
  build_plan and the routes call, so a route cannot disagree with what the plan itself proposes:
  - `_planned_option`, the per-task rule;
  - `_settled_step`, how the tasks settle the condition's step: when the lender is not doing ALL of it,
    the tasks carry their own steps (LP-954). On 1228, UWM orders the inspection but the Change of
    Circumstance clause stays the LO's, so the step is none and only the inspection is the lender's;
  - `route_ask`'s rule (LP-942): an ask addressed to her is her own task.
- **`task_option`:** every task of the type set to one step.

**The suggestion** is the setting route that matches the lender's actual setting, and it carries its
reason: "UWM's settings say it orders final inspections." The lender's settings are the ones the owner
decided (decision 1, LP-925), editable per lender. A task route is never suggested, because no setting
says so.

**Choosing a route** (`choose_route`) rebuilds the path through the one door for each change:
- `set_next_step` for the condition's step;
- `update_item` for each task still being worked: open, requested or received. A task whose email was
  sent is still on its route and still moves with it. Done and not-needed tasks are left alone, and
  split parts are left to their parent's re-split.

Each change writes its own `condition_plan_changed`, so:
- the history says what changed;
- "Go back to the suggested plan" replays it;
- "Changed by you" shows;
- the drafts follow: an ask joins its email, and a task leaves it.

**Chosen** (`chosen_route`) is read back from the condition: its step and every task the route's target
names, over the same tasks still being worked. It is not a stored choice, so a route she then edits away
from stops reading as chosen.

**No routes while nobody does the work** (`routes_offered`). A condition at push back or information
only offers none (6178, pushed back at UWM): its tasks are not needed, so every route would be a click
that changes nothing. Once she moves it off that step the routes come back, and a route never puts the
reading's push back on it again. The push back is set aside BEFORE the setting is applied, because in
`_step_and_reason` push back wins over "the lender orders it". This also applies to LP-955's IV-01
routes, which were offered on a pushed-back condition before.

**Lookup:** `routes_for` takes the lender's own row first (LP-955's IV-01 at UWM), else the type's routes
at any lender (`("*", type)`). A file with no lender has no routes.

### On screen

**"How it gets done"** shows the condition's routes as choices. The suggested one carries a "Suggested"
badge and its reason, and the chosen one is selected. Choosing one sends it to the server, which rebuilds
the path.
- Each task's Next step select moves under "Change each task's step yourself".
- A type with no routes is as before.
- "I'll handle it my own way" stays the last choice.

**The collapsed "Route:" line** names the chosen route, with "(suggested)" when it is the one the
settings point to.

### Found by the tests, before release

The first `route_target` computed the step without `_settled_step`. It said "lender doing it" for 1228's
"UWM orders it" while `build_plan` makes the step none (the clause stays the LO's). So the suggested
plan never read as the suggested route, and choosing it made a plan the planner never makes. The tests
that found it ask the opposite question to the code: not "what does the route say" but "does the plan as
built read as its suggestion".

LP-955's `test_no_two_routes_in_a_pair_share_a_step` failed as expected: PA-03's "ask_lender" and "order
yourself" both have no step of their own and differ in what they set on the tasks. The invariant still
holds and now compares the whole target: step-only routes by step, other routes by (step, setting,
value, task option). That the three PA-03 targets really differ on a condition is measured in
`test_the_three_pa03_targets_differ`.

## Review (peer session, 2026-10-11)

Reviewed at `d2dcd55b`. One finding, which is the ticket's own second question; the other four are
answered below, each by a measurement rather than a reading. Two tests added, one of which is the
general form of a guard the ticket had by example.

### The copied `route_ask` rule was not the same set (fixed)

`route_target` decided "an ask addressed to her is her own task" with `performers[0] == "processor"`,
while `route_ask` — the rule it says it is copying — asks `recipient_for(item) is None`. Those agree on
every shape but one: `recipient_for` relays any list CONTAINING the LO to the LO's email wherever it
sits (0132's disclosure goes out in the LO's email), so `[processor, lo]` HAS a destination. `route_ask`
leaves it an ask; the copy made it her task. The route's predicted plan and the built plan would then
disagree on that task for ever, and `chosen_route` could never match — the route would be unpickable,
silently, which is exactly what the ticket feared.

`_ask_is_hers` now CALLS `recipient_for` instead of restating its conclusion, through a small
`_RawAsk` shim in the shape the protocol reads. A performer the vocabulary does not know keeps the old
literal answer rather than raising, because these are raw reading dicts and not the validated items
`route_ask` sees. Nothing ships `[processor, lo]` today — the library's one multi-performer item is
0132's `[borrower, lo]` — so the test asserts the rule against `recipient_for` itself rather than
through a fixture that cannot currently exist; restating the rule as the first performer fails it.

### The questions

1. **Does `_settled_step` change `build_plan`?** No, and the asymmetry is preserved exactly. The old
   inline block tested `all(...)` over ALL created items and `any(...)` over those not `NOT_NEEDED`; the
   helper carries that in its `needed` flag, and `build_plan` passes
   `(i.option, i.status is not NOT_NEEDED)`. The `if/elif` became two `if`/`return`s, equivalent because
   the two conditions are mutually exclusive (one needs `step is None`, the other `step is
   LENDER_DOING_IT`). The routes' call passes `needed=True` for every task, which is right for a plan
   that has not been built yet.
2. **`route_target` against `route_ask`.** The finding above.
3. **Can two routes both match once a task is done?** Not today, and measured over the WHOLE table
   rather than argued: every routed type has exactly one library task except IN-02, whose two routes
   differ on BOTH of its tasks. So no group has a pair that agrees on one key and differs on another,
   which is the shape that would make `chosen_route` answer by table order. And when the differing task
   IS done, `any(key in by_key)` fails and the answer is `None` — no route rather than the wrong one.
   `test_no_two_routes_in_a_group_can_both_match_a_condition` now computes every group's target and
   refuses that shape, which is the general form of `test_the_three_pa03_targets_differ`. It carries a
   positive control for its own detector, because a guard against a shape that does not exist yet would
   otherwise pass whether or not it works — breaking the detector fails the test through that control.
4. **Does anything rely on IV-01's routes showing when pushed back?** No. Both consumers degrade:
   `ConditionRoutes` renders nothing for an empty list and the panel guards with `routes?.find` and
   `routes.length > 0`. LP-955's tests assert 0006's two routes on a condition that is not pushed back
   and `[]` for 1582, and the drawer's own test never sets a step. The ticket's new test pins the new
   rule.
5. **Can the panel want a `why` on a non-suggested route?** No. The only reader is
   `round-plan-panel.tsx`, gated `route.suggested && route.why`, so the payload filling `why` on the
   suggested route only and the consumer asking for it only there already agree — and the gate means an
   unexpected `why` elsewhere would not render.

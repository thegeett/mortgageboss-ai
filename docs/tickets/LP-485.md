# LP-485 — rate lock and validity-at-closing: CL-1, CR-13, PR-6

## What this ticket is

Three loan-scoped date compares, grouped by shared mechanism rather than category: **CL-1** (does the rate
lock expire before closing?), **CR-13** (is the credit report still current at closing?), **PR-6** (is the
appraisal still current at closing?). All three read parsed dates through derived numbers and mirror the
existing `_contract_days_until_closing` pattern.

**All three are LIVE** — `ACTIVE_RULE_IDS` moves **37 → 40**. This is the first ticket built under the
standing policy adopted 2026-08-12: thresholds are **researched and cited by us**, not deferred to Priya, and
document availability does not gate whether a rule is written.

**DT-4 was in the original cohort and is dropped** — the one exception the policy allows. See below.

### ⚠️ Two criteria that could not be met as written

**8 — `ships: ratify` is not settable on these rules.** `ships` is derived from the rule's **kind** and the
loader enforces it: a `judgmental` rule ships `ratify`, a `structural`/`calculative` rule ships `auto`
(LP-424 item 3 — "a bar declaring `ships: auto` on a judgmental rule is a LIE"). All three of these are
structural/calculative, so `ships: auto` is the only value that loads. Achieving `ratify` would require
changing their catalog `kind`, which would misclassify them. **Recorded, not worked around.**

**7 — `validated: true` on CL-1 is structurally impossible.** The loader rejects `validated` on any status
but `calibratable-now` / `no-ai-threshold-pending`. CL-1 is `no-ai-dependency`, and `is_eligible()` reads
**`input_resolves` alone** for that status — so CL-1 is live with `validated: false`, correctly. It has no
domain threshold to validate (`zero` is a comparison boundary). CR-13 and PR-6 carry `validated: true`
because they *do* have researched windows.

### ⚠️ Calendar months, not day approximations

The guideline states both windows in **months**. A 30-day approximation drifts from the calendar by up to
three days at four months — enough to fail a document the guide passes. `_full_months_between` counts
**complete** calendar months (a partial month does not count), and a test pins the case that matters:
**2026-04-01 → 2026-08-01 is 122 days but exactly 4 months** — a 120-day rule would wrongly fire on it.

### ⚠️ PR-6 is three bands, and the middle one is a condition

| age at closing | verdict | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ≤ 4 months | `satisfied` | acceptable as-is |
| > 4, ≤ 12 months | **`needs_review`** | an appraisal **update** (Form 1004D) is required — **not a failure** |
| > 12 months | `fired` | a **new** appraisal is required |

An appraisal at six months is perfectly usable *with* an update; reporting it `fired` would tell a processor
the file is broken when it needs one more document. **Band ordering is load-bearing** — the >12 test must
precede the >4 test, or the new-appraisal requirement would never fire. Both are pinned by tests.

**Known gap, recorded not silently dropped:** B4-1.2-04 also caps a **desktop** appraisal at four months (a
*new* appraisal, not an update). No tag distinguishes desktop from traditional — classifying the extractor's
`form_type` is an ADR-353 open-vocabulary question. Today a desktop appraisal aged 5 months reports "update
required" where the guide requires a new appraisal. Logged in `priya-open-questions.md`.

### ⚠️ The note-date substitution

Both guidelines measure to the **note date**. The snapshot carries only `contract.closing_date`, so that is
what both rules use. Usually the same day; not always. Stated in `_age_in_months_at_closing`'s docstring, in
both specs, and raised as a question in `priya-open-questions.md`. **Not silently treated as identical.**

## What was built

**Recipes** (`derived.py`) — all mirror `_contract_days_until_closing`: gather one date across subjects,
**dedup by the parsed date**, abstain to `unknown` when none parse **and** when documents disagree, emit only
the number. Shared helpers `_single_parsed_date` and `_full_months_between` mean the three cannot drift.

- `rate_lock.days_to_closing` — signed days (negative = the lock expires first)
- `credit.report_age_months_at_closing` — complete calendar months
- `property.appraisal_age_months_at_closing` — complete calendar months, from the **effective** date

**Tags** — `rate_lock.expiration` and `property.appraisal_date` already existed in the generated
`fact_tags.csv`, so only their `tag_production.yaml` declarations were added (both `document_type`-scoped).
The three derived numbers are new in `vocabulary_extra.yaml`.

**Fact labels** (`reasons.py`) — each new tag has a processor-facing label, so a `couldnt_check` reads *"the
rate lock's expiration date and the loan's closing date could not be found in the file"* rather than a tag
id. This was caught by `test_human_reasons`, not by me — a real gap, not a count.

## ⚠️ DT-4 — dropped, and the correction that matters

**DT-4 is not buildable.** Verified in code, not assumed: there is exactly **one** property-tax source in the
system — `housing.taxes_monthly`, derived from `property_tax_bill.annual_tax_amount ÷ 12`, *"the SAME field
the DTI reads directly"* per its own docstring. MISMO carries **no** tax field (`grep tax` in
`mismo_section.py` returns nothing). So both operands of "tax used vs assessed" trace to **one extracted
field on one document** — ADR-330 vacuity, the one block the standing policy still honours. The alternative
reading needs a **millage rate** that exists in no field, tag or MISMO key.

⚠️ **The correction against LP-476: DT-4 was the census's single "WRITABLE-NOW" rule, and it is not
writable.** Anyone reading that census will otherwise start exactly here again.

## ⚠️ The measurement correction

Bench-corpus fill rates (`rate_lock_expiration` 3/3, `appraisal_effective_date` 2/2) are measured over **303
loose documents with no loan-file grouping**. A 3/3 there means the datum exists *somewhere*, **not** that two
facts meet on one file. The whole backlog was scoped that way. It does not block building — under the
standing policy it never did — but no fill rate from that corpus should be read as evidence that a rule will
resolve on a real file.

## Assumptions and decisions

1. **Closing date substitutes for note date** — stated everywhere it applies; raised for review.
2. **`input_resolves: true` is a development-mode assertion** — the parsed inputs are declared and their
   extraction fields exist, so the chain resolves whenever the documents are present. It is *not* a claim
   that a loan file carries them today. Recorded in the bar's own comment so a later reader cannot mistake it
   for a measurement.
3. **`zero` is not a threshold.** CL-1's sign test needs a comparison boundary; it is not a number anyone
   signs off (the AS-3 precedent).
4. **Test count updates are census updates, not accommodation.** Ten pinned assertions moved because three
   rules genuinely activated — the same update every prior activation ticket made, each with its cause named.
   **No test was skipped, xfailed or deleted, and no behavioural assertion was weakened.** The one genuine
   failure (`test_human_reasons`) was fixed by adding what was missing.

## What this changes downstream

- **`ACTIVE_RULE_IDS` = 40.** The first increase since LP-447.
- **The date-compare pattern is now established** — `_single_parsed_date` + `_full_months_between` are
  reusable by any future date rule (CR-6's seasoning windows, PC-9's contingency dates).
- **`priya-open-questions.md` exists** and is the calibration agenda: every self-set threshold with its
  source, the note-date question, the desktop-appraisal gap, and the DT-4 millage-rate gap.
- **DT-4 should be removed from any remaining backlog** as unbuildable, not deferred.

## ADR

**No ADR.** These are three rules built on the established spec/recipe/bar mechanism; nothing architectural
changed. The one policy shift — self-researched thresholds with `validated: true` — is a *process* decision
recorded here and in `priya-open-questions.md`, not a change to how the system is built. If the note-date
substitution or the `ships: auto` constraint later forces a mechanism change, that will need its own ADR.

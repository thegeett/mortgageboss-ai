- **Ticket:** LP-958 · Phase 4.5 Stage 3 follow-ups, batch 4 follow-ups (the owner, 2026-10-03)
- **Source:** item 9 of the 2026-09-30 staging trial (the drawer), plus three remarks the owner made

## What changed

### Backend

- **`GET /conditions/{id}/items/{item_id}/link-candidates`**
  (`condition_links.link_candidates`, `LinkCandidatePublic`).
  - It returns the file's current, live documents. Each has its name, type label (`display_name`), upload
    time, and three flags:
    - `matches`: from `condition_matching.document_answers` with `match_words_for(type, item key)` and
      her unlinks. This is the one matching rule that also links documents by itself, so the dialog's
      "Matches this item" cannot disagree with the plan.
    - `linked`
    - `unlinked_by_her`
  - Matching documents come first, then newest first within each group.
  - Tenant scope comes from `ScopedCondition`. `_scoped_item` makes an item of another condition a 404.
- **`ConditionItemPublic.generic`**: true while the item's acceptable form is still the reading's
  placeholder (`GENERIC_ACCEPTABLE`, with or without a trailing full stop). It reads the acceptable form
  only, not the name: once she writes her own acceptable form, hers shows.

### Frontend

- **Link dialog** (`link-document-dialog.tsx`, which replaces LP-953's inline select):
  - search over name and type;
  - "Matches this item", then "Other documents" ("Documents on this file" when nothing matches);
  - an Open link per document, and a page field;
  - documents already linked to the item are not offered;
  - before she links a non-match, a warning says the item stays open until she accepts it anyway. A
    document she unlinked earlier gets its own sentence.
  - It is also the evidence card's **Change** ("Change the document for 0006"), without the old document.
- **Item actions** (`item-links.tsx`):
  - One full-width row under the item card: **Add document ▾** (Link a document on this file… / Upload a
    new document…) and **Ask someone ▾** (her task only; the same seven recipients as LP-955).
  - The row used to sit in the card's 9rem right-hand column; that was the misalignment in the screenshot.
  - The menus are plain disclosure buttons, not Radix: no portal inside the sheet. Escape closes a menu
    without closing the sheet.
- **Next box** (`next-box.tsx`, built on the pure `lib/conditions/next-action.ts`): the top of the drawer.
  It shows one sentence and one control. Order is precedence:
  1. lender cleared or waived, or information only: "Nothing left to do";
  2. a failed check;
  3. Sent: "record it here", with Record. Came back: "fix it and send it again";
  4. Ready with an ask in an unsent draft: Open the email;
  5. Ready: "Send it to UWM: build the lender package. It goes in with N other conditions…", with Open the
     lender package and the three steps after it;
  6. her task: Add document / Ask someone for that item;
  7. waits on another code;
  8. an unsent ask email;
  9. waiting on the recipient;
  10. a question to UW, as a draft and then once sent;
  11. the lender is doing it;
  12. already in the file.

  The lender's short name and the ready count come from `useConditionPackage`, the numbers the package
  panel prints.
- **"Open the lender package"** closes the drawer and scrolls to the package panel (`#lender-package`) on
  the Conditions tab. It does **not** link to `/loan-files/{id}/lender-package`: that route is still the
  Phase 6 placeholder. I found this while building; the mockup's link assumed the page was real.
- **Lender's answer**: the drawer label and the list column now say "Lender's answer", and `open` reads
  "Not cleared yet" (`lenderAnswerLabel`). The list row has a **Record** link while the lender owes an
  answer, never on a replaced row. The shared vocabulary still says "Open" for the filters and the tiles.
- **Next step column** (`nextStepToken`):
  - Ready shows "Goes in the lender package" (with "· evidence checked" when evidence arrived), or the
    warning "Ready, but its email is unsent…" when an open ask's draft is unsent. That warning opens the
    draft.
  - Sent and still open shows "Sent to lender · waiting for their answer".
  - `goesInPackage` mirrors `condition_package._goes_in`: Ready, open or came back, and not information
    only.
- **Package panel, before it is built**: it is now the primary-coloured **Next** banner: "6 conditions are
  ready to send. Build the lender package and upload it to UWM." with **Build lender package**.
- **Summary tiles** (plan files): **Sent to lender** (`by_prep_status.with_underwriter`, open only, like
  every number there) and **Cleared by lender** (`cleared`, filtering lender `cleared`) replace
  "Prior to docs / funding open". Those two filters remain in the filter row's Heading choices.
- **Generic items** show "As the lender asks: <lender's words>" in place of the placeholder.

## Deviations from the mockup

- **"Failed a check" stays** as a tile. The mockup had six tiles and dropped it; it is the red number LP-923
  added and still the most urgent.
- The Next box shows the same **Add document ▾ / Ask someone ▾** menus as the item row, instead of the
  mockup's two plain buttons. That keeps one control for one action.
- **"Goes in the lender package"**, not the mockup's "In the package". Before it is built nothing is *in*
  a package, and `_goes_in` is the true claim.
- **Mark done** stays where LP-921 put it (under who acts), not in the action row.
- The mockup's "UWM's upload cutoff" line on the banner is not repeated. The built package already shows
  the cutoff.

## Review (of `1b4f3762`)

**Two findings, both fixed here. The build is good and its self-review was unusually honest — six areas
flagged, and the two that mattered are in that list. But the state a came-back condition is actually in
was misidentified in three places at once, so the sentence written for it could never appear; and the
"Ask someone" control lost the keyboard it used to have for free.**

### Finding 1 — the came-back sentence never fires, and fires wrongly after a re-send

`nextAction` read the refusal inside the sent branch:

```ts
if (condition.prep_status === "with_underwriter") {
  return condition.lender_status === "not_cleared" ? refusal : sent;
}
```

The server does not leave a came-back condition there. `condition_status.py:488` moves our track to
**To do** on a `not_cleared` verdict from Ready or Sent to lender, and clears `waiting_on`:

```python
if payload.status is ConditionLenderStatus.NOT_CLEARED and prep_from in (
    ConditionPrepStatus.READY, ConditionPrepStatus.WITH_UNDERWRITER,
):
    condition.prep_status = ConditionPrepStatus.TO_DO
```

So `with_underwriter` + `not_cleared` is not a condition that came back. It is one she **fixed and sent
again**: `_goes_in` admits `not_cleared` (`_OPEN = (OPEN, NOT_CLEARED)`), `submit` moves prep to
`with_underwriter`, and `move_prep_status` never writes `lender_status`, so the previous round's verdict
stays on the row until a new one is recorded. Three consequences, all user-facing:

- when the lender **does** refuse it, the drawer says nothing about the refusal — prep is To do, so the
  branch is skipped and she gets the plan's steps with no hint of why the work is back;
- after she re-sends, the drawer tells her *"did not clear it … then send it again"* about the thing she
  has just sent, and **withholds `do: {kind: "record"}`** — the control for recording the answer she is
  now waiting for, which is the part that costs her something;
- the Next step column disagreed with the drawer about the same row, because its guard was
  `with_underwriter && lender_status === "open"`: a re-sent condition fell past it and showed the plan's
  steps, as if nothing had been sent.

The repo already had the right model written down: `round-comparison-panel.tsx:32` says the came-back
line ends *"our status moved from Sent to lender back to To do"*.

**Why no test caught it:** two tests asserted the misconception. `next-action.test.ts` had
*"Sent: record the lender's answer; came back: fix it and send it again"* and built the "came-back" case
as `with_underwriter + not_cleared`; `next-step.test.ts` had *"a came-back one does not say waiting"* and
asserted the same state is **not** "Sent to lender". Both are green on a state that is really a re-send,
so the suite was pinning the defect — the fixture named one state and constructed another.

**Fixed, in one rule rather than two.** The lender-status half is now a single exported predicate,
`openWithLender` (`next-step.ts`), used by `goesInPackage`, the "Sent to lender" token and the drawer:

- the drawer's `with_underwriter` branch is unconditional — sent is sent, whatever the last round said,
  and it keeps the Record control;
- a new branch, scoped to `prep_status === "to_do" && lender_status === "not_cleared"`, carries the
  refusal sentence, which is the state the server actually writes. Scoping it to To do leaves `waiting`
  with its own sentence (she is waiting on someone for the fix) and `ready` with the package steps (she
  has fixed it), so the fix changes exactly the two states that were wrong;
- the column's guard is `openWithLender`, so a re-sent condition reads "Sent to lender · waiting for
  their answer" on both surfaces.

Both misnamed tests are retargeted at the real states, with the re-send asserted explicitly and an
ordinary To do condition as the control so the refusal branch cannot fire on everything. Three
mutations, each restored from a copy: the column guard back to `=== "open"` (its own test fails), the
new To do branch disabled (the came-back test fails), and the original ternary restored (the re-send
test fails) — one test each, nothing else.

**And the duplication that allowed it is now executable.** Their own note flagged `goesInPackage` as a
hand copy of `_goes_in` with nothing tying the two. That was the right worry and it had *already* come
true — not between frontend and backend, which agreed, but between two frontend copies of the same
lender-status set. `test_the_frontend_still_open_rule_matches_the_backend_set`
(`tests/test_condition_type_mirror.py`, where this repo keeps its mirror guards) parses
`openWithLender`'s body and compares it to `_OPEN` as a set, both directions, with vacuity guards on the
file and the match. Dropping `not_cleared` from the frontend rule now fails a **backend** test.

### Finding 2 — the new menu dropped the keyboard the `<select>` had

`ActionMenu` replaced a native `<select>` (`d86cf974` has `<select>` at `item-links.tsx:94`; the new
component declares `role="menu"`, `role="menuitem"` and `aria-haspopup`). The ARIA roles are a promise to
a screen reader that this behaves like a menu, and the implementation had no arrow keys, no Home/End, and
no focus return — a native select gave all of that for free. Measured: opening the menu left
`document.activeElement` on `<body>`, so a keyboard user is dropped at the top of the drawer.

The component's own docstring argues for not using Radix, which is a fair call for two to seven choices
in a dialog that a portalled menu fights for focus — but the cost of that call is implementing the
keyboard contract by hand, and that half was missing.

**Fixed** in `ActionMenu`: the trigger opens on ArrowDown, opening focuses the first choice, Up/Down wrap,
Home/End jump, and both Escape and choosing return focus to the trigger. `item-links.test.tsx` pins the
whole sequence; reverting the component fails it on the first assertion (`<body>` rather than the first
choice). The docstring now records why the keyboard is hand-written here.

### Checked and not findings

- **The link dialog does not reverse LP-953's design, and makes it visible.** This was my first
  hypothesis, from LP-953's own note: *"Not built: a document picker that filters by the item's wanted
  type (all the file's documents are offered; a mismatch is allowed and flagged, by design)."* It holds.
  `link_candidates` appends **every** current document and sorts with `not row["matches"]` — a stable
  sort, not a filter — and it reuses `document_answers`, so there is no second matching rule in the
  client. The dialog renders both groups, and picking a non-matching document shows LP-953's exact
  consequence: *"It will be linked, but this item stays open until you accept it anyway, with a reason."*
  It even distinguishes `unlinked_by_her` — her own earlier refusal, handed back to her. That is the
  usability gain without the decision being quietly reversed.
- **`name` falling back to `original_filename` is not the LP-956 class.** Their note compared it to the
  `plan_reason` finding. The shapes differ where it matters: `NEVER_EXPOSED` governs the **readonly
  views**, whose results CLAUDE.md calls safe to read into a transcript, and the argument there is that a
  file name can carry a person's name the scrub cannot match. This is an authenticated, processor-facing
  endpoint showing a processor the documents on her own file, where the file name is the point — she
  uploaded it and the documents list already shows it.
- **`linked` counting `item.document_id` is right.** An "already in the file" document has the pointer
  without necessarily having an evidence row, and offering it again would earn `ALREADY_LINKED` (409)
  from `link_document`. Excluding it spares her a pointless refusal; Change and Unlink are the doors for
  that pair.
- **The Next box's "becomes Sent to lender" is true, traced rather than assumed** — which their note said
  they had not done. `useSubmitPackage` → `condition_package.submit` → `move_prep_status(to=
  WITH_UNDERWRITER)` for every included row, and the summary bar labels `with_underwriter` "Sent to
  lender" (`conditions-summary-bar.test.tsx:86`). The copy matches the label the screen uses.
- **The `#lender-package` anchor resolves when that step is offered.** The wrapper is at
  `conditions-list-view.tsx:369` with `empty:hidden`, and the Next box only offers "Open the lender
  package" when `goesInPackage` — which requires Ready, so `ready_count ≥ 1` and the panel renders rather
  than returning null. Worth naming the dependency: it is **LP-957's** key nesting that makes the panel
  appear without a reload, so these two tickets have to stay together.
- **`condition_links.py` is purely additive** — not one deleted line in the diff — so LP-953's three
  properties I reviewed there are untouched: `link_document` sets RECEIVED and never DONE, a manual link
  deletes the unlink row for that pair, and `_file_document` refuses another file's document with
  `not_found=True`.
- **`generic` checking only the acceptable text** is their call and a defensible one (her own wording
  should win), and the long `verbatim_text` in "As the lender asks: …" is a presentation question for the
  owner, not a defect. Both are recorded in their ticket, which is where a deviation belongs.
- **A residual duplication I did not fix:** `ASKS` and `QUESTIONS` are declared in both `next-step.ts`
  and `next-action.ts`. The values agree today, and the new drift guard covers the lender-status rule
  only. A third option added to either list would have to be added twice; it is an open item rather than
  a finding, and the cheap fix is to export them from `next-step.ts` the way `openWithLender` now is.
- **Their disclosed gap is closed, both halves.** Only docs changed after their backend run: the full
  suite at the SHA is **9123 passed, 1 failed** (the named sonnet test), their exact number. The
  `import type` re-sort after their frontend run is unmeasured by vitest either way, because the suite
  does not typecheck — the surface was `tsc --noEmit` and `pnpm build`; `tsc` is clean at the SHA and
  biome reports the file clean.

# LP-846 — the document guidance is visible again

Status: spec. First of four (LP-846, LP-847, LP-848, LP-849).

## The report

> "Also message does not look as expected, it does not have section where to get each document."

## It is generated, and LP-844 flattened it

The catalog produces this per document:

    - Driver's licence — front and back
        Where to get it: A photograph or scan of your current licence.
        What we need to see: Both sides, unexpired, all four corners in frame.
        What we cannot accept: an expired licence; the front only.

Measured through the real pipeline after LP-844:

    <ul><li>Driver&#39;s licence — front and back<br>Where to get it: …<br>What we need to
    see: …<br>What we cannot accept: …</li></ul>
    <ul><li>Homeowner&#39;s insurance — the declarations page<br>Where to get it: …</li></ul>

Two faults, both mine:

1. **The detail lines are `<br>`-joined inside the bullet**, with no indent and no emphasis on the
   label. The monospace `<pre>` this replaced showed them indented on their own lines, so replacing
   it made a section that WAS visible invisible — a regression dressed as an improvement.
2. **Each document becomes its own `<ul>`**, because the blank line between documents flushes the
   list. Two documents render as two one-item lists.

## The change

`emailBodyToHtml` renders a document's detail lines as their own block inside the item, with the
label (`Where to get it:`) distinguishable from its text, and consecutive documents as one list.

**IT MUST STILL SURVIVE THE PASTE.** The clipboard's HTML carries no classes and a mail client
discards our stylesheet, so the structure has to come from tags a composer keeps — a nested list, or
a `<div>` per detail line — not from CSS. That constraint is the whole reason the first version used
`<br>`, and it is why this needs a shape rather than a class.

## And the end-to-end test this ticket owes

Eleven communication tickets have each asserted at one layer, and nearly every defect since has
lived in the seam between two. This adds the first test written in the processor's terms rather than
the code's: **request a borrower document, and the words a borrower reads name the document AND say
where to get it.** It spans finding → need → template → catalog → HTML, which is exactly the span
that produced this bug.

# Built

The detail lines are a **nested `<ul>`** inside each document's item, each one `<strong>Label:</strong>
text`, and consecutive documents are one list.

A nested list rather than `<br>` or a class, and the constraint decided it: the clipboard's HTML
carries no classes and a mail client discards our stylesheet, so indentation has to come from a tag
a composer keeps. That is also why LP-844 reached for `<br>` — it is the only thing that looked
class-free — and why the answer is a shape rather than styling.

Measured on the catalog's real output:

    <ul><li>Driver&#39;s licence — front and back<ul>
      <li><strong>Where to get it:</strong> A photograph or scan of your current licence.</li>
      <li><strong>What we need to see:</strong> Both sides, unexpired.</li></ul></li>
    <li>Homeowner&#39;s insurance — the declarations page<ul>
      <li><strong>Where to get it:</strong> From your insurance agent.</li></ul></li></ul>

One list, two documents, guidance nested and labelled.

## The blank-line rule changed, and it needed care

A blank line now ends a **paragraph** but not a **list** — that is what makes two documents one
list. It cannot simply stop flushing, because the borrower template indents `$inbox_address`
further down: adopting that into the document above would put the file's inbox address inside "what
we cannot accept". So an indented line is a detail only when it follows its bullet **immediately**,
with no blank between. Both directions are asserted, and b7's LP-844 test for exactly this rule
still holds with its shape updated.

## The label rule is deliberately narrow

`^([A-Z][^:]{2,40}):` — a capitalised phrase at the start of a detail line. A sentence that merely
contains a colon must not be split at it, or "Send it by Friday: the underwriter needs it" puts half
the sentence in bold. Asserted.

## The end-to-end test this ticket owed

Two halves, because no single test can span a process boundary:

- **Backend** — `test_a_borrower_is_told_where_to_get_each_document`: ask for two documents, and the
  body a borrower receives names both by their borrower-facing names, carries `Where to get it:`
  **twice** and `What we need to see:` twice, and contains no slug. Written in the report's terms and
  with no knowledge of which function produced which line.
- **Frontend** — the same text, verbatim from the catalog, through the renderer. Hand-writing a
  simpler shape is precisely how LP-844 shipped a renderer that worked on `- one item` and flattened
  what the catalog actually emits.

Counting twice matters: a single document would pass while the second lost its guidance, which is
the shape of the bug being fixed.

## Mutations

Three, each with the absence check confirmed: reverting to `<br>`-joining kills 4 tests; flushing
the list on a blank line kills 1; dropping the label emphasis kills 2.

## Review (second session)

Reviewed as part of `13d36cf4`. The renderer question the ticket flagged — the blank-line rule now
having three interacting conditions, which is where its author stopped trusting their own case
analysis — is the one worth answering by enumeration rather than argument.

### The real templates all render correctly

Every `.txt` template under `backend/app/communications/templates/` was rendered with a two-document
`$document_list` (each document carrying labelled detail lines) and a substituted `$inbox_address`.
Each of the seven document-bearing templates produced **one outer list, two nested detail lists, the
inbox address outside any list item, and balanced `<li>` tags**. The six that carry no document list
produced no list at all. So the fold rule does the right thing on the input that actually exists.

### And on the combinations nobody named

The specific renderings are asserted by name in the ticket's own tests. What those cannot cover is
the interaction, so `email-body.test.ts` now renders **every sequence of five lines over {bullet,
indented, paragraph, blank} — 1024 of them** — and checks the two properties that do not require
knowing the intended output: no line's content is lost, and the tags balance with no paragraph
opening inside a list. All 1024 pass.

It is not a vacuous check: with the trailing `flushList()` removed, **453 of the 1024 report lost
content**. That is recorded in the test's own docstring so the next person does not have to rediscover
that it can fail.

No defect found in the fold rule. The three conditions compose correctly.

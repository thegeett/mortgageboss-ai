## The change

**`round-plan-panel.tsx`, rebuilt around what she has to do:**
- **Heading:** "Check the plan, then confirm it", one sentence of context, and three numbered steps:
  open each condition; change a next step if it's wrong (click ⓘ); confirm, because nothing is emailed
  or changed until you do.
- **Pills in words:** "3 emails to draft — Borrower, Title/attorney, LO", "2 tasks for you", "1 already
  in the file", plus push-back, lender and "need you to check" pills only when non-zero.
- **Each condition, collapsed:** its code, its summary, its heading chip, and one line per task saying who
  does it and where: "You upload the invoice", "Ask Title / escrow — in the title email", with "Waits on
  1228" and "Found: …, page 1" where they apply. "Check how we read it" replaces the confidence score,
  and appears only when the reading needs her.
- **Opened with "What you need to do":**
  - what the lender wants (the reading's explanation);
  - when it's needed (the heading and what it means);
  - why we suggest this (`plan_reason`, where there is one);
  - the lender's exact words;
  - each task as a numbered card, with "Done when you have:" (the item's acceptable form, unless it is
    the generic placeholder), its own Next step select, an ⓘ, and a line saying what the chosen step
    will do;
  - "Good to know": the library's playbook.
- **The ⓘ dialog:** all eight steps with meaning and example, the current one marked "Selected", titled
  for the task it was opened from, and closed with Got it.
- **"When you confirm, we will:"** lists, from the plan's drafts and the rows' steps:
  - the emails (to whom, which codes), each reviewed before it is sent;
  - the notes to the underwriter;
  - which conditions become ready;
  - her tasks (with "after 1228");
  - which ones are left to the lender.

**No Hide** (the owner, after the build: "there should not be hide button plan section"). The plan is
the step she is on until she confirms it. This also removes one of LP-964's two ways to open the held-back
list early. The list now appears once the plan is confirmed, or, when the reading failed and there is no
plan, from "Show them now". The two gate tests that used Hide now say so: "the plan has no Hide, so the
list waits for Confirm", and the not-confirmed marker reached through "Show them now".

**Every condition on the round is in the plan** (the owner: "yes, show every condition in the plan").
The table used to drop conditions with no task and no step of their own. After a failed reading on
LF-Y36E it showed one of six, while "Confirm 1947's reading first" named a condition that was not on the
screen. Such a row now reads "We couldn't work out what this one needs." with one "Check how we read it"
button; the header chip is suppressed there so the button is not shown twice. A test pins it (the row,
the one button, the call). Re-adding the filter fails the test, and so does restoring the duplicate chip.

**One select per task.** The panel used to show one select per *distinct* step and change every task
sharing it. Each task now has its own, through the same `useUpdateItem`. The condition's own step (push
back, lender is doing it, and so on) has its own select through `useSetNextStep`.

**`lib/conditions/step-meaning.ts`** is one copy of:
- what each step does (`OPTION_MEANING`) and an example (`OPTION_EXAMPLE`);
- the dialog order;
- the task-line phrase (`actionFor`), which names the library task ("You order the final inspection")
  and the email the server will use;
- the select's meaning line (`meaningFor`), which names who for an ask;
- what each lender heading means for timing (`BUCKET_KIND_MEANING`).

`next-step.ts` exports `recipient`, so the phrase uses the server's own grouping (`recipient_for`): an
LO relay goes in the LO email, title and attorney share one, and an appraiser's ask goes to the lender.

**Every meaning is a claim about the server, checked when written:**
- asks become drafts (`condition_drafts._ASKS`);
- questions become an underwriter draft (`_QUESTIONS`);
- "Already in the file" and finished "I'll do it" tasks make a condition Ready (`ready_because`);
- "Lender is doing it" and "Information only" are display-only.

The module docstring lists these, so changing the server means changing the sentence.

Two timing lines first written for `master` and `unknown` claimed more than the app knows ("stays until
the lender clears it", "treat it as needed before docs"). They were cut to what the labels say.

**Backend:** `LibraryTypePublic.playbook` (and `LibraryType.playbook` in the frontend) carries the
library's note.

## Review (of `c2b9cbc6`)

**Five findings, all fixed here, and all of one kind: the panel's new job is to EXPLAIN the server, so
every sentence it adds is a claim that can be false. Four of the five are sentences or controls that
promise something `condition_drafts.py` and `condition_plan.py` do not do. The accessibility is clean and
the code's own docstring already names most of the right server functions, which is why the gaps that
remain are narrow ones.**

### Finding 1 — the whole condition's ask named an email that only its tasks can fill

`actionFor(option, null)` is the "The whole condition" line. For an ask it named an email —
`"Ask the borrower — in the borrower email"`, and `"Ask a third party — by email"` — and `meaningFor`
fell through to `OPTION_MEANING`, which promises "We put this request in the email to the borrower …
once sent, the condition shows as waiting on the borrower".

Drafts are not built from the condition's step. `condition_drafts` composes every draft from ITEMS
(`pairs = [(conditions[item.condition_id], item) for item in items]`, then `borrower_lines` /
`party_lines`), and `apply_step_status` does not propagate a condition's step onto its items. So a
condition whose own step is an ask, but whose tasks are not asks, puts **nothing** in any email and
never waits on anyone. The select offers all eight steps for the whole condition, so she can set it.

**Fixed:** the whole-condition ask line now says `"Ask the borrower — the tasks below are what go in the
email"`, and `meaningFor(ask, null)` has its own sentence naming what reaches the email — "each task
below that is itself set to ask — a task set to something else is not in it." A task's own line is
unchanged, because there it is true. The test that asserted `"Ask a third party — by email"` asserted
the claim the server does not keep, and now asserts the new line plus the task case as its control.

### Finding 2 — a task was offered the two steps that only a condition can have

Each task's select listed all eight steps. Two of them do nothing on an item. Every use of `_QUESTIONS`
on the server reads the **condition's** `next_step` — `condition_drafts.py:739`, `:747`, `:818`,
`:1116` — and nothing reads an item's option against it. So an item set to `ask_underwriter` or
`push_back`:

- is in no email (`recipient_for` returns None for anything outside `_ASKS`);
- raises no question draft (that is keyed on the condition);
- is not her task either (it is not `i_will_do_it`).

It belongs nowhere, while the meaning line under its own select said "we draft a question to the
underwriter for you to review and send". `update_item` accepts it — it refuses only `already_in_file`
without a link — so nothing stops it. The eight-option list is not new (the previous panel had it too);
what LP-966 added is a sentence under each select asserting what the step does, which is what turns a
reachable dead end into a false promise.

**Fixed:** `ITEM_OPTION_ORDER` — the six a task can be — for a task's select, with `OPTION_ORDER`'s
eight kept for the whole condition, where a question belongs. **A task already holding one still lists
it**, because a `<select>` whose value is outside its options renders blank and would hide what the item
is; she can move it off, and nothing new lands on it. Pinned at the layer the bug shows, in the panel's
own test, rather than only on the list constant.

### Finding 3 — "Check how we read it" opened nothing on a condition that was never read

LP-966 rightly stopped filtering away conditions with no items and no step, and gives them a row that
says so with one control. But `ConfirmReadingDialog` opens on `condition.reading` and begins
`if (!reading) return null`, so for a condition with no reading at all the button opened a dialog that
renders nothing — and a condition nobody read is the likeliest reason a row has no items and no step.
The one row whose whole purpose is to say what to do next had a dead control on exactly its commonest
case.

**Fixed:** `Unworked` takes whether there is a reading to check. With one, it is unchanged. Without one
it says "We haven't read this one yet, so there is nothing to plan for it." and offers no button. A row
that explains itself beats a button that does nothing. **Left for the owner:** this row still has no way
to *start* a reading; "Read again" lives on the reading card, and adding a reading action to the plan
panel is a product decision rather than a review's.

### Finding 4 — the confirm summary promised readiness by the wrong rule

"When you confirm, we will: Mark … ready" was computed as `next_step === "already_in_file"` or every
live item's **option** being `already_in_file`. The server's rule (`condition_plan.ready_because`) keys
on each live item's **status**: ready when the condition's own step is "Already in the file", or when it
has no step of its own (or "I'll do it") and *every* live item is `DONE`. Those differ in both
directions:

- **over-promising.** A document she links by hand is `RECEIVED`, and only `check_link` makes it `DONE`
  (LP-953). So a condition whose items are all "Already in the file" with one document still being read
  was promised "ready to send" and would not have become ready.
- **under-promising.** A condition whose tasks she had already marked done is left out, although the
  server moves it — no item's option is `already_in_file`, but every status is `DONE`.

**Fixed:** `willBeReady` mirrors `ready_because` branch for branch, keyed on status, with the server
function named in its docstring. The existing test's fixture was `option: "already_in_file"` with the
default status — a pair no writer on the server produces, since `build_plan` sets the option and `DONE`
together — so it was green on an invented state; it now carries `status: "done"`, and a new case pins
both directions.

### Finding 5 — two sentences promised readiness from one task alone

`OPTION_MEANING.i_will_do_it` said "the condition is ready to send to the lender once you mark it done",
and `already_in_file` said "the condition is marked ready to send to the lender when you confirm". Both
sit under a single task's select, and `ready_because` requires EVERY live item to be done. On a
multi-task condition — which is most of them — marking this one task done readies nothing. The two are
one defect in two strings, so both are fixed rather than the one I happened to notice: each now says
"once every task on it is done/settled", the module docstring records the rule it mirrors, and a test
pins the phrase with the two steps that genuinely do not wait on the tasks as its control.

### Checked and not findings

- **The rest of focus area 1 holds**, traced one sentence at a time rather than accepted: asks go into a
  draft to `recipient_for`'s recipient and the condition waits on them once sent (`_ASKS`, `WAITING_ON`,
  and LP-947's `first_asked_owner` for which one); the questions become the underwriter's draft
  (`_QUESTIONS` on the condition, `render_question`); `lender_doing_it` and `information_only` move
  nothing (`DISPLAY_ONLY`, and `apply_step_status` skips `info_only`). `askedWho` even reproduces the
  LO relay and LP-942's appraiser-through-the-lender rule, which is more care than the copy needed.
- **A condition-level ask is not produced by the plan itself.** `types.yaml` sets `default_option` only
  to `lender_doing_it` or `information_only`, so finding 1's state arrives through her own change rather
  than through a reading — which is why the sentence was wrong rather than catastrophic.
- **Accessibility is right, and notably better than LP-958's menu was.** The disclosure is a real button
  with `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls`; the ⓘ dialog is the shared `components/ui/dialog`, which is
  Radix, so the focus trap, Escape and `aria-modal` come from the primitive rather than by hand. This is
  the same author whose LP-958 menu I had to fix for exactly those; the shared primitive is the reason
  there is nothing to fix here.
- **Removing the plan's Hide button is a deliberate narrowing, and it is recorded.** It takes away one
  of LP-964's two escapes from the held-back list, leaving the failed-reading "Show them now". That is
  the owner's call, the ticket says so, and LP-964's own review verified the gate fails open, so a
  broken endpoint still reaches the list.
- **`plan.drafts` is the server's own list**, so the "Draft N emails" line is reported rather than
  derived — the right half of the summary to trust.

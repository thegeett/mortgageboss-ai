# LP-486 — credit deterministic: CR-12 disputes, CR-3 paid-to-qualify

## What this ticket is

Two deterministic credit rules over the `per_liability` enumerator. **CR-12 (disputed accounts) was built
and is live** — `ACTIVE_RULE_IDS` **40 → 41**. **CR-3 (paid-to-qualify) was not built**, and the reason is
a missing *input*, not a missing threshold: its trigger is an underwriting claim no document states.

The substantial output is CR-12 and the pattern it establishes — **ADR-376, the closed-vocabulary abstain** —
which every future credit rule should copy.

## ⚠️ The finding this ticket is built around

`is_disputed` carries **two different encodings on two real credit reports**:

| source | values |
|---|---|
| the two bench reports (n=2, one bureau format) | clean **`Y`/`N`** — 34 N, 1 Y across 35 rows |
| **LF-96SV** (a different bureau format) | **free text** — `ACCOUNT IN FORBEARANCE` · `ACCOUNT CLOSED BY CREDIT GRANTOR` · `ACCOUNT PREVIOUSLY IN DISPUTE-NOW RESOLVED-REPORTED BY SUBSCRIBER` · 15 rows null |

**One field, two encodings.** A rule written the obvious way — `is_disputed == "Y"` — reads every tradeline on
the second report as **not disputed**. No error, no log line, every test green. And CR-12 **ships `auto`**, so
no human sees it.

So the producer recognises a **closed vocabulary** and **abstains** on anything else. That is ADR-376, and it
is the shape `account_type`, `payment_status`, `account_status` and `worst_delinquency` will all need.

### The vocabulary (Priya's ruling — in `reference_values`, where she edits it)

**Disputed:** `Y` · `yes` · `account disputed by consumer` · `consumer disputes this account` ·
`dispute in progress` · `account information disputed by consumer` · `consumer disputes account information`

**Account-status remarks, NOT disputes:** `N` · `no` · `ACCOUNT IN FORBEARANCE` ·
`ACCOUNT CLOSED BY CREDIT GRANTOR` · `PAID ACCOUNT` · `TRANSFERRED` · `ACCOUNT CLOSED` · `DEFERRED`

Normalisation is **case-fold + collapse whitespace, nothing else** — no stemming, no fuzzy matching. A test
pins that `account dispute by consumer` (missing the `d`) abstains rather than being guessed into a match.

⚠️ **`PREVIOUSLY IN DISPUTE — NOW RESOLVED` is in neither list, deliberately.** The text nearly says "not
disputed", which is exactly why it must not be read that way — that is an inference about what the bureau
meant. It abstains, and a processor decides. **Proven against LF-96SV's real string, verbatim.**

⚠️ **The vocabulary cannot drift.** It lives in the spec's `reference_values` (domain data) and is mirrored in
the producer (what runs); `test_cr12_vocabulary_matches_the_spec` pins them identical.

### ⚠️ `ships: auto`, and what it means here

`ships` is derived from the rule's **kind** and the loader enforces it (LP-424). CR-12 is `structural`, so it
ships **`auto`** — this could not be set to `ratify` without misclassifying the rule's kind.

**There is no human in the loop, so the abstain guard is the only protection against a bureau-format
surprise.** That is why the catch-all outcome is `couldnt_check` and not `satisfied`: the gate already routes
an `unknown` tag to `couldnt_check` before the body runs, and the default is a second, independent refusal to
clear. Fail closed at both layers.

## ⚠️ CR-3 — not built, and why

**CR-3 is not vacuous** (its two operands would be independent — a claim, and evidence in another document).
The blocker is upstream of that: **its trigger has no source.**

`liab.excluded_paid_off` is declared *"Marked paid-off/excluded to qualify (needs payoff evidence)"*. That is
an **underwriting claim**, not a bureau fact. Traced:

- the credit report's tradeline carries **14 fields** — creditor, type, account number, ownership, date
  opened, balance, credit limit, monthly payment, past due, status, payment status, 24-month history, worst
  delinquency, disputed. **None says "this debt is being excluded to qualify."**
  - MISMO's liability projection carries **four** — type, monthly payment, unpaid balance, holder name. **No
    exclusion flag.**

Building it anyway would produce a rule that `couldnt_check`s on every file forever **with every test green** —
precisely the ADR-286/289 failure the ticket warns has killed live rules four times. So it is reported rather
than shipped hollow.

**The researched rules are recorded for when the trigger exists** (`priya-open-questions.md` §6): a revolving
account paid to $0 need not be closed (Fannie **B3-6-07**); an installment loan with **≤ 10** remaining
payments may generally be excluded (Fannie **B3-6-05**). ⚠️ **Both are marked STARTER — neither page was
fetched in this ticket**, so neither carries a page date, and per ADR-361 they must be read from the live
guide before use. The evidence hierarchy, the screenshot caveat, the report-vs-newer-evidence reconciliation
and the Freddie source-of-funds requirement are all recorded there too.

## Proven against real data vs assumed

⚠️ **The corpus is three credit reports: two bench reports of one bureau format, plus LF-96SV of another.
That is three documents.** Everything below rests on that.

| claim | evidence |
|---|---|
| the Y/N encoding | **real** — 35 rows across the two bench reports |
| the free-text encoding | **real** — LF-96SV's 18 rows, values quoted verbatim |
| the resolved-dispute string abstains | **real** — LF-96SV's exact string, tested |
| the other 9 vocabulary phrasings | **assumed** — Priya's ruling, not observed in these three reports |
| a third bureau format | **unknown** — and the abstain guard is what makes that safe |

The guard's value is precisely that the third format does not have to be known in advance: it abstains
instead of guessing.

## Assumptions and decisions

1. **`liab.is_disputed` is DERIVED, not parsed.** A parsed passthrough would emit `Y` / `ACCOUNT IN
   FORBEARANCE` — outside its declared `yes`/`no` enum — and a parsed tag is **not** validated against
   `allowed_values` (`producer.py:91`), so it would ship out-of-domain values silently. The derived recipe
   normalises and abstains.
   2. **`liability` was added to `_DERIVED_SUBJECTS`.** The LP-483 subject family already supplied
      enumerate / read_field / context; only the allowlist blocked a per-liability recipe. The producer is
      generic and the recipe asserts its own raw type.
   3. **`input_resolves: true` uses the LP-485 development-mode meaning** — the input is declared, its
      extraction field exists, and the producer resolves it on real stored data in **both** known encodings. It
      is *not* the pre-LP-485 meaning ("verified end-to-end on a live loan file"). ⚠️ **One field, two
      semantics** — stated in the bar's own comment so a reader cannot conflate them.
   4. **The catch-all is `couldnt_check`.** With `ships: auto` there is no human backstop, so a `satisfied`
      default would convert any future encoding into a silent all-clear.
   5. **Test count updates are census updates.** Twelve pinned assertions moved because one rule activated,
      each annotated with its cause. **No test was skipped, xfailed, deleted, or weakened.**

## What this changes downstream

- **`ACTIVE_RULE_IDS` = 41.**
  - **ADR-376 is the pattern for the rest of the credit lane.** `account_type`, `payment_status`,
    `account_status`, `worst_delinquency` all carry bureau-varying encodings; none should be written as an
    equality against one bureau's spelling.
  - **The `liability` derived-subject path now exists**, so any future per-tradeline tag is a recipe, not
    plumbing.
  - **CR-3 needs a product answer** — where does "this debt is paid off to qualify" come from? Until then it
    has no trigger.
  - **The FHA disputed-derogatory branch remains unbuilt** — logged, agency-independent detection shipped.

## ADR

**ADR-376** — *A field whose ENCODING varies by source must match a CLOSED vocabulary and abstain on anything
else — never infer from unfamiliar text.* Written because the reasoning must outlive this ticket: the same
`is_disputed` field is clean `Y`/`N` on one bureau and free text on another, and the obvious rule fails
silently on the second.

# LP-634 — the Need List says what to get and not why

- **Ticket:** LP-634
- **Status:** Changes 1 and 2 built — CI green, not yet deployed. Change 3 (merging) not started
- **Date:** 2026-08-29
- **Scope:** Backend (needs composition + source shape + two accuracy guards) + frontend (need card)

## The report

The Need List is the first page a processor opens. On staging's LF-AWBB it carries 19 needs and
explains almost none of them.

| origin | needs | what the "why" says |
|---|---|---|
| `floor` | 6 | **nothing at all** — `reasoning` is null on every one |
| `finding` | 5 | rule ids: *"Required by verification rule(s) CL-1, CR-13, DT-7, ID-5, IH-2, IH-3, PR-6"* |
| `ai_reasoning` | 8 | prose, followed by a disclaimer telling the reader not to trust it |

**The floor is the worst of the three, and it is the most certain.** `bank_statement`, `w2`,
`pay_stub`, `payoff_statement`, `government_id`, `existing_mortgage_statement` are the deterministic
needs — the ones we are surest about — and each shows a title and a blank space where the reason
should be.

**The finding needs speak the engine's language.** A processor does not know what `CL-1` is, and one
row reads, in full, *"Requested from verification findings: ID-3"*. This is the machinery talk the
finding composer has forbidden since LP-527; needs never got a composer, so the register the rules
side spent four tickets removing is still shipping here.

**The AI needs undercut themselves.** Each renders a `Source` block in MISMO vocabulary — `Loan
purpose is refinance`, `MortgageLoan liability`, `Revolving liability` — and closes with *"AI-identified
— verify this is the right triggering fact; the AI may have misread."* The most prominent sentence on
the most-read page tells the reader the system may be wrong, in words that are not theirs.

## What it costs

STRATMOR's document-collection research puts a number on it: failing to clearly explain a
documentation request happens on nearly **one in three loans** and costs **47 points of NPS**. Not
explaining *why* is the single most expensive communication failure in this phase of a loan — which is
precisely the page this ticket is about.

## 1. Every need explains itself, in one voice

A composition pass over needs, mirroring `services/finding_prose.py`: a model rewrites the reason from
a fixed fact summary, under the same guards — no invented numbers, no machinery talk, no identifiers,
falls back to what is stored, cached by input hash.

The point is that it applies to ALL THREE origins, so a processor reads one voice and cannot tell
which subsystem produced a row. Worked from LF-AWBB's own data:

| need | today | after |
|---|---|---|
| Recent pay stubs | *(blank)* | The application states base salary from Amazon Com Services LLC, started October 2022. Recent pay stubs show current earnings and year-to-date totals. |
| Payoff statement | *(blank)* | This refinance pays off the United Wholesale Mortgage loan at closing — $588,224 as stated. The payoff statement gives the exact amount due on the closing date, including interest and fees. |
| Government ID | *(blank)* | Every borrower needs an unexpired government photo ID to confirm identity before closing. |
| Verification of employment | Required by verification rule(s) IN-4, IN-7, IN-8, which found no VOE on the file. | The pay stubs and W-2s show income, but not a continuous employment history or that the position is ongoing. A verification of employment states the start date, position and current status directly. |
| Closing Disclosure | Required by verification rule(s) CL-1, CR-13, DT-7, ID-5, IH-2, IH-3, PR-6… | Seven checks need the final loan terms and closing date, and no Closing Disclosure is in the file yet. It confirms the rate, fees, cash to close, and the date insurance must be in force by. |
| Date-of-birth document | Requested from verification findings: ID-3 | The application states a date of birth, but no document in the file confirms it. A driver's licence, passport or permanent resident card showing the date of birth will settle it. |
| Current appraisal | This is a refinance transaction where the current market value… | The application states the property is worth $641,000 against a $590,000 loan — about 92% of value. An appraisal confirms the value the loan is based on. |

Three rules the prose follows, and they are the whole specification:

1. **Name the stated fact with its number.** "$3,907/month with UWM", never "MortgageLoan liability".
2. **Say what the document settles**, not that a rule asked for it.
3. **Never mention the machinery** — no rule ids, no origins, no confidence hedges.

## 2. Show the fact, not the provenance

Delete the `Source` block, the attribution pill, and the "AI-identified — verify this is the right
triggering fact" line. One **Why** line in their place, identical in shape whatever produced the row.

This reverses LP-110's decision deliberately, so the reasoning is worth stating. LP-110 made the
source visible so the reasoning would be FALSIFIABLE — the processor could check the AI had not
misread. That instinct was right and the implementation aimed it at the wrong target: it exposed the
PIPELINE (which subsystem, how much to trust it) when what makes a claim checkable is the CLAIM.
"The application states a $438/month lease with Ally Financial" is verifiable in one glance at the
1003. `Revolving liability` + a trust pill is not, and asks the reader to audit us instead of the file.

Falsifiability is kept, and moved into the sentence.

## 3. One need per document, carrying all its reasons

`title_report` (AI) and `title_commitment` (finding) are the same document, on the list twice, with two
different explanations. Merge across origins and let a need hold more than one reason:

> **Title commitment**
> Every refinance needs a title search before closing. Nothing in the file yet shows how title is
> vested, or whether there are liens beyond the mortgage being paid off.

This is the most invasive of the three — it touches need identity and therefore reconciliation and the
LP-111 dedup — and should ship separately from 1 and 2.

## The accuracy half — no wording fixes a wrong need

**`tax_return` must not be proposed here.** The row reads "Two years of personal tax returns (1040s
with all schedules)" on a borrower the application states as `self_employed: false`, classification
`Primary`, income type `Base`, one W-2 employer. Fannie Mae B3-3.1-02 requires the most recent W-2 and
pay stub for fixed base income; tax returns are not part of it. Its own reasoning says *"base
employment income"* and asks for returns in the same sentence — the inference LP-624 put
`self_employed` in the context to prevent, made anyway.

**`credit_authorization` must not be proposed here.** It asks for authorization to pull credit on a
file carrying two completed credit reports.

Both are cases where the model reasons past a fact the application states outright. The prompt already
carries the LP-624 paragraph for employment; it needs the same treatment for documentation depth, and
probably a deterministic guard rather than a paragraph — a stated `self_employed: false` with only
`Base` income is a checkable condition, not a judgement.

## Scope boundaries

- **Not** a change to which needs exist, beyond the two accuracy guards. The floor, the finding
  suggestions and LP-69's proposals keep producing what they produce.
- **Not** a change to the coverage flag (LP-631/632) or the retraction channel (LP-633) — a flagged
  need still renders its flag; this changes the sentence above it.
- Part 3 (merging) is separable and should be its own ticket if 1 and 2 ship first.

## Assumptions

- Reusing the finding composer's shape rather than inventing a second one: same guards, same cache
  discipline, same fallback. The guards are the reason this is safe to point at a page a processor
  trusts — and `unrequested_documents`, `machinery_talk` and `leaked_identifiers` are all directly
  relevant to need text.
- The floor's reasons are derivable without a model — they are deterministic needs with known
  triggers — so a template floor per need type is the honest fallback when composition fails, and is
  strictly better than the blank that ships today.
- One voice across origins means a processor cannot tell a floor need from an AI proposal by its
  prose. That is intended: the `disposition` (proposed vs confirmed) already carries how much the
  system is claiming, and it carries it in one place instead of two.

| piece | where |
|---|---|
| the composer + its guards (machinery, invented numbers, identifiers, length) | `app/ai/needs_prose.py` |
| the pass: file facts, per-need summaries, cache, apply | `app/services/needs_prose.py` |
| `needs_items.explanation` + the `needs_prose` cache + the re-cut readonly view | `20260829_1700_c4a71fe28b93_lp634_needs_prose_cache.py` |
| `need_prose_enabled` (off by default, like `finding_prose_enabled`) | `app/core/config.py` |
| the pass, LAST, on document arrival and on every verification | `app/tasks/needs.py`, `app/services/verification_run.py` |
| the Why line; the `Source` block and its disclaimer deleted | `frontend/components/file/needs/need-card.tsx` |

Nine service tests, three dashboard tests. Full suite 5925 backend, 386 frontend.

**Two columns, not one — and the first cut got this wrong.** `reasoning` is the origin's own record and
the composer's INPUT; `explanation` is the output. Writing the composed sentence back over `reasoning`
moved the cache key on every run, so the model was re-asked every time and each answer was composed
from the previous answer instead of from the file — prose drifting a little further from the file on
every verification, on the page a processor opens first. A test pins that a second run re-asks nothing.

**The guard order matters.** A UUID is mostly digits, so checking invented numbers ahead of identifiers
reported "you invented a number" for a leaked document id — true, and it sends the retry after the
wrong thing. Identifier is checked first.

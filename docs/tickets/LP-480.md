# LP-480 — the per-liability enumerator

## What this ticket is

Five subject enumerators existed (`per_deposit`, `loan`, `per_borrower`, `per_document`, `per_account`) and
**none enumerated liabilities**, so CR-1, CR-3, CR-5, CR-6, CR-8, CR-10 and CR-12 had no subject to attach a
per-row finding to. This ticket adds `per_liability`.

The hard part was not the enumeration — it was deciding **how the two sources of liability data relate**. MISMO
`liability.{n}.*` facts say what the borrower *declared*; credit-report `tradelines` rows say what the bureau
*reported*. They describe the same real debts. The chosen policy is **union, no merge** (ADR-374): every row
from either source is its own subject, marked with its source, and nothing is ever matched or merged.

No rule is added — `ACTIVE_RULE_IDS` stays **37**.

## What Phase A found

**1. `_per_account`'s actual contract.** Identity is `(institution, masked account number)`, **both required**,
normalised by casefold + strip-punctuation + collapse-whitespace and explicitly *not* fuzzy. A missing
component makes the member **unresolvable**: its own subject keyed by `content_id`, carrying an
`account.unresolved` marker whose `reasoning` says why. Tags that *agree* across members are kept; **conflicting
values are deleted**, never last-wins, so the rule `couldnt_check`s.

⚠️ **Two corrections to this ticket's own text.** `subject_key_fields` is a **spec** field (AS-1:
`[account, date, amount]`), not something an enumerator supplies. And `_per_account`'s subject id is a
**readable composite key**, not a content hash — "stable content id, never a position" describes
`_per_deposit`. The requester confirmed the Phase A reading supersedes the ticket wording.

**2. MISMO liabilities exist — but not where they can be tested.** The real fixture `MISMO16940192.xml` parses
**10 liabilities**, all four fields populated. But **the LF-6T3N snapshot fixture carries ZERO** liability facts
(13 MISMO facts, none `liability.*`). And the model is only four fields end to end — `liability_type`,
`monthly_payment`, `unpaid_balance`, `holder_name`. **There is no account number anywhere in the chain**: not
dropped in projection, never captured.

⚠️ **A fixture defect worth knowing:** the two *synthetic* refi fixtures set `holder_name = "Robin Q Sample"` —
the **borrower's** name in the creditor field. Any creditor-matching test built on them would prove nothing.
Per the requester's warning, none was.

**3. ⚠️ The two sources never co-occur — anywhere.** The bench corpus is 303 loose documents with no loan-file
grouping and no MISMO at all; LF-6T3N has bank statements, pay stubs, W2s and mortgage statements but **no
credit report and no liabilities**. **The reconciliation cannot be tested against real data.** This was the
headline finding, and it shapes what this ticket can honestly claim.

**4. ⚠️ The masked-account trap — record this for anyone reading fill rates.** On the 35 real tradeline rows,
`account_number_masked` fills **35/35 raw**. That number overstates its discriminating power badly. Many
extracted "masked" values are not masked (`20230504393831`), so the LP-443 redact backstop scrubs them at
capture. In the snapshot: **only 27/35 distinct, and 9/35 are a bare `[redacted]`** — zero discriminating power.

⚠️ **A naive `(creditor, account_number_masked)` composite — the `_per_account` pattern — therefore already
guess-merges on real data:** two distinct `SETOYOTA FIN DBA OF WO` tradelines both redact to `[redacted]` and
collapse onto one key. **Any future ticket reading fill rates from the LP-476 census needs this caveat: raw
fill rate ≠ usable identity.**

**5. Absent-tag handling, confirmed for all five.** `_per_deposit` returns `(content_id, {})` when tags are
absent; `_loan` and `_per_borrower` yield `{}` maps; `_per_document` the same; `_per_account` adds markers.
**Subjects are always returned — the rule never vanishes.**

**6. Three policies, with their effect on the two rules that matter.** Reported in full at Phase A and decided
in ADR-374; summarised below.

## The reconciliation policy — chosen and rejected

**This is the section to read when building CR-4 and wondering why the enumerator doesn't help you match.**

| policy | DT-1 (debt ratio) | CR-4 (undisclosed tradeline) | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Union, no merge** | ⚠️ a naive sum double-counts — mitigated by the `liability.source` marker | ✅ **preserved intact** | **CHOSEN** |
| B. Match-and-merge | ✅ no double-count | ❌ **destroys the signal** | rejected |
| C. Single-source-of-truth | ✅ no double-count | ❌ **destroys it outright** | rejected |

**Why A.** An undisclosed tradeline *is* a debt present in one source and absent from the other. Any merge
destroys the evidence CR-4 exists to read — and destroys it **silently**, because the merged list looks
complete.

**Why not B.** Two independent reasons. It is **not deterministically implementable**: MISMO has no account
number, so the only shared dimension is a free-text name in a different convention (`NR/SMS/CAL` vs
`PENNYMAC LOAN SERVICES`) plus amounts that legitimately drift. Any matcher is fuzzy → needs an AI tag → needs
a scored bar → needs Priya. That is **CR-4's own job** (`rule_kinds.csv` classifies it `ai_fuzzy_match`). And it
would put **domain logic in an enumerator** — tags describe, rules judge.

**Why not C.** It destroys CR-4 outright (you cannot compare against a list you discarded) and makes a file's
debts depend on which documents happen to be present.

**⚠️ The cost A accepts.** A rule that naively sums `monthly_payment` across every liability subject **will
double-count** a debt present in both sources. That is why `liability.source` is on **every** subject, not just
ambiguous ones: **a summing rule must filter by source.** DT-1 will care most. Chosen knowingly over destroying
CR-4.

## What changed

`enumerators.py`: `_per_liability` + `_mismo_liabilities` + `_liability_marker`, registered in `_ENUMERATORS`
with a `KNOWN_ENUMERATORS` frozenset and an **import-time assert** (the `_COERCERS` / `_NORMALIZERS` pattern),
so a typo fails at import rather than on a real file. `content_id.py`: a `LIABILITY_PREFIX = "lia"` alongside
the existing `doc` / `txn` / `lst`.

**Identity.** A tradeline subject uses its **LP-479 `row_id`**; a MISMO liability gets a **content-derived id**
over its four fields via `assign_content_ids` — never the positional `liability.{n}`. A tradeline whose
`row_id` is `None` (i.e. `stable_row_id` off) yields **no subject at all** rather than a positional fallback.

**Markers** are reserved **structural** tags (the `DOC_TYPE_TAG` / `ACCOUNT_UNRESOLVED_TAG` pattern), not
vocabulary tags — so they need no `fact_tags.csv` / `vocabulary_extra.yaml` entry and cannot collide with the
generated-file guard. They describe the **subject** (which source it came from; whether it could be
identified), never the debt.

## ⚠️ Proven against REAL data vs. CONSTRUCTED fixtures

**This distinction is the most important thing in this document.**

**Proven on real data** (documents 250/251, the stored extractions):

- enumerates **35 subjects** from 35 tradeline rows, **ids unique 35/35**;
- every subject marked `credit_report_reported`;
- ids **deterministic** (identical across builds) and **order-independent**;
- ids are the LP-479 `row_id`, already proven content-derived.

**Proven against CONSTRUCTED fixtures only** — because **the two sources never co-occur anywhere in the repo**:

- the union itself (35 tradelines + 2 stated = 37 subjects, correctly source-split);
- the no-double-count arithmetic;
- `mismo_stated` marking and the MISMO content-derived id;
- the unresolved-identity marker;
- all three guess-merge adversarial cases.

**So the reconciliation ships unvalidated against reality. ADR-332 applies** — a fixture I authored cannot
prove that real declared and reported debt behave as modelled. **A real loan file carrying both a credit report
and MISMO liabilities is the missing evidence.**

### The six properties (Phase C)

1. **Enumerates** — 35 subjects on real data, ids 35/35 unique. ✅
2. **No double-count** — 37 subjects = 35 + 2, correctly split by source. ⚠️ **constructed**; union double-lists
   a matched debt *by design*, so a summing rule filters by source.
3. **No guess-merge** — same creditor / different amounts → 2 subjects; **null holder on both sides** → 2
   subjects, both flagged; **byte-identical rows** → 2 subjects (occurrence tiebreak). ✅ constructed.
4. **Absent tags** — 35 subjects still returned with `tags.absent`, carrying only the structural marker; the
   gate on a missing load-bearing tag returns **`couldnt_check`** ("the dti payment could not be found in the
   file"). ✅
5. **Deterministic + order-independent** — identical ids across runs; reversing source order gives the same id
   set. ✅
6. **Drift guard** — `is_known_enumerator('per_liabilty')` is `False`; an unregistered key raises `KeyError`. ✅

16 tests pin all of it (`tests/verification/rule_engine/test_per_liability_lp480.py`).

## Phase D — regression, and the tag→rule hop

**Regression.** AS-1, AS-2, AS-12 and IN-12 ride the legacy `transactions` / `schedule_c` / `schedule_e`
attributes. Proven by LP-479's method: the LF-6T3N snapshot they read is **byte-identical** before and after —
same md5 (`0ef2b731f9d963a8ab1e6a4c09074381`), built from a clean tree via `git stash`. Identical input ⇒
identical verdicts, with no live judgmental reasoner and no cost.

**⚠️ The tag→rule hop is STILL not exercised — the ticket's premise does not hold.** This ticket was expected
to be its first exercise. It is not, and cannot be: exercising the hop requires a **rule** whose spec declares
`subject_enumeration: per_liability`, and this ticket adds no rule. **No spec declares `per_liability` today.**

Worth knowing: **no spec declares `per_account` either** — it has been built and unused since LP-336. The
enumerator layer routinely lands ahead of its rules, so this is the established pattern rather than a surprise.
Phase C proved the closest reachable thing: a subject's tag map reaching `evaluate_gate` and resolving to
`couldnt_check`. **The hop through a rule body remains unproven, and LP-481 is now the first thing that will
exercise it.**

`ACTIVE_RULE_IDS` = **37**; `SNAPSHOT_VERSION` = **4**; full suite green; ruff + mypy clean.

**Cost.** **$0, and unlike LP-479 no model call was even attempted** — every check ran against stored
extractions, the MISMO parser, and snapshot construction.

## Assumptions

1. **MISMO liability identity is its four projected fields**, because no fifth exists. If an account number is
   ever added to the MISMO chain, the id scheme changes and existing ids shift — they are content hashes.
2. **A null `holder_name` is the unresolvable condition** for a stated liability, mirroring `_per_account`'s
   "missing either component". The other three fields are amounts and a type, none of which identify a
   creditor.
3. **Byte-identical stated liabilities are two debts, not one** — separated by the occurrence tiebreak. Two
   genuinely identical stated rows are indistinguishable by construction; splitting them is the fail-closed
   choice (a merge would silently halve the debt).
4. **`document_type="credit_report"` scopes the tradelines gather** (the LP-453 review: a list name is not a
   unique key).
5. **Markers are structural, not vocabulary tags** — so no rule may treat `liability.source` as a fact about
   the debt. It is a fact about the *subject*.

## What this changes downstream for LP-481

- **The seven credit rules have a subject.** CR-1, CR-3, CR-5, CR-6, CR-8, CR-10, CR-12 can declare
  `subject_enumeration: per_liability`.
- **⚠️ LP-481 must filter on `liability.source`** for anything that aggregates, or it double-counts. This is
  the sharp edge ADR-374 accepts deliberately.
- **LP-481 is the first real exercise of the tag→rule hop.** If subject delivery into a rule body has a
  problem, it surfaces there. Budget for that rather than assuming the path is proven.
- **CR-4 gets no matching help, by design** — ADR-374 explains why, and that is the document to read before
  attempting a matcher.
- **The corpus ask is now specific:** a real loan file carrying **both** a credit report and MISMO liabilities.
  Everything cross-source in this ticket is fixture-proven until one exists.

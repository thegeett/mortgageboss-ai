# LP-941 — Withdrawal history

- **Ticket:** LP-941 · Phase 4.5 Stage 3 follow-ups, batch 2
- **Source:** the product owner, 2026-09-29: "The history line for a withdrawal shows its reason, and
  Undo adds its own line ("Withdrawal undone") while keeping the original reason visible."
- **Closes:** the open item "After a withdrawal is undone, her reason is on no screen" (LP-940 review).

## Design

- **Server:** `ConditionEventPublic.withdrawal_reason` is projected from `detail.reason`, and only for
  `condition_withdrawn` (guarded by KIND). It is the one free-text string in the history's projection.
  The allow-list's rule is that every exposed string comes from a closed set, and this breaks it on
  purpose, by the owner's decision. It is her own words about a condition she added, not the lender's
  text. A `reason` stored on any other event (a reopen, a backward move, a restore) still does not travel.
  The allow-list test records the entry and why.
- **Client:**
  - the withdrawal line reads "Withdrawn as entered in error — “Added twice by mistake” — Priya Raman";
  - a restore reads "Withdrawal undone — Priya Raman";
  - the withdrawal line stays in the history after an Undo, so the reason stays visible.
- **Not changed:** the file timeline does not read condition events, so the reason reaches no other
  screen.

### (b) Where the free text can reach — traced, not assumed

So her words stay inside two authenticated, company-scoped endpoints, and cannot reach a log, a staging
query, an export or the lender's package. **(b) holds.** One correction to how you stated it: the module
answer ("only `api/conditions.py` and `condition_status.py`") is right, but `condition_status.py:329` is a
comment, and `api/conditions.py` exposes the projection on **two** routes. Your test covers the condition
one; the round one carries the same field by the same projection, which is fine — the claim is just "two
scoped routes", not one.

### (a) Contained — and the justification is a different one from `actor_name`'s

Contained, on the evidence above plus the projection's own shape: guarded by `kind`, type-checked with
`isinstance(..., str)`, capped, and recorded in the closed-set test so a second free-text field cannot be
added quietly.

**But the new allow-list entry sits directly beneath `actor_name`'s, and the two exceptions are not the
same kind.** That comment says, in terms:

> `actor_name` IS THE ONE THAT BREAKS THE VOCABULARY RULE, AND IT IS SAFE FOR A DIFFERENT REASON: its
> SOURCE … it is **NEVER read from `detail`** — so the trick the test above plays (NPI stored under a
> legitimate key) cannot reach it.

`withdrawal_reason` **is** read from `detail`, so it is the first field in this projection that is both free
text and detail-sourced — the one combination `actor_name`'s justification rests on avoiding. What makes it
safe instead is not its shape and not its source but **who writes it and how far it can travel**: she types
it about her own entry, and the table above is the proof that it goes nowhere else. That is a sound
argument, and it is a *different* argument. Worth one sentence saying so, because a future reader adding a
third free-text field will cite whichever precedent is nearest, and these two are not interchangeable: if
that field were detail-sourced, `actor_name`'s reasoning would not cover it, and this entry's reasoning
would only cover it after someone re-ran the trace above.

The residual risk, stated plainly so the owner's decision is on the record for what it is: she can type
borrower NPI into the reason box, and it will then sit in `condition_events.detail` and render in the
history. It cannot leave the authenticated surface — that is what the trace establishes — so the exposure
is to her own colleagues, which is the same audience as every other note on the file. Acceptable, and not
the same as saying the field is shape-safe.

### The 500 cap is written twice

`condition_withdraw.REASON_MAX = 500` bounds what is stored; `schemas/condition.py:552` writes the literal
`[:500]` again. They agree today. Raise `REASON_MAX` and the projection silently truncates instead,
which would read as a display bug a long way from its cause. Importing the constant costs nothing and
removes the second statement of one number.

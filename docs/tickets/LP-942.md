## Why the appraiser is never emailed

**Appraiser independence.** Loan production staff (the processor, the loan officer) do not contact the
appraiser directly. The appraiser is engaged and managed at arm's length, through the lender or its
appraisal management company, so that nobody with an interest in the loan closing can influence the
value. An appraisal request therefore goes to the lender, never to the appraiser, whatever the
condition's reading says.

## Design

**One new draft recipient, `lender`** (`DraftRecipient.LENDER`; migration `7b2d4e61c9a8`):
- The CHECK lists all 9 values. The downgrade refuses while a lender draft exists.
- `_RECIPIENT` maps `Performer.LENDER` and `Performer.APPRAISER` to it.
- The round's existing sync makes one unsent draft per recipient, so there is **one lender draft per
  round**, holding the lender's asks and the appraiser's.
- It is addressed from the **lender's contacts** (`_lender_address`): the account executive first, then
  any active contact with an email, then the lender's contact email, then an address she typed before.
- Its title is "Email to the lender"; marking it sent waits on the lender.

**Her task:**
- `route_ask(item)` turns an ask whose first performer is the processor into "I'll do it", with no due
  date. The library's task wording applies by key.
- It is called where an item is made (the plan's build, "Add an item") and where one is edited
  (`update_item`, whose event records `routed`).
- After this, the processor is the only performer without an email, so it is the only one `route_ask`
  rewrites.

**The line on the item:**
- `ConditionItemPublic.route_note` is "Appraisal requests go through the lender". It is set only when the
  appraiser is who the ask goes to (the first performer, as `recipient_for` reads it), so a
  borrower-led ask that merely lists the appraiser does not claim it.
- The sheet shows it under "In lender email". The client's own routing (`itemWhere`) also sends the
  appraiser to the lender email.

**A test pinned the gap.** `test_the_task_wording_comes_from_the_library` re-pointed her invoice task to
"Ask a third party" with herself still the performer, and expected an ask: exactly an ask with no email.
Under the owner's rule that stays her task. The test now asserts that, and asserts separately that an ask
of someone else loses the task wording.

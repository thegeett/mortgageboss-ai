## Why the appraiser is never emailed

**Appraiser independence.** Loan production staff (the processor, the loan officer) do not contact the
appraiser directly. The appraiser is engaged and managed at arm's length, through the lender or its
appraisal management company, so that nobody with an interest in the loan closing can influence the
value. An appraisal request therefore goes to the lender, never to the appraiser, whatever the
condition's reading says.

## Design

**One new draft recipient, `lender`** (`DraftRecipient.LENDER`; migration `7b2d4e61c9a8`):
- The CHECK lists all 9 values. The downgrade refuses while a lender draft exists.
- `_RECIPIENT` maps `Performer.LENDER` and `Performer.APPRAISER` to it.
- The round's existing sync makes one unsent draft per recipient, so there is **one lender draft per
  round**, holding the lender's asks and the appraiser's.
- It is addressed from the **lender's contacts** (`_lender_address`): the account executive first, then
  any active contact with an email, then the lender's contact email, then an address she typed before.
- Its title is "Email to the lender"; marking it sent waits on the lender.

**Her task:**
- `route_ask(item)` turns an ask whose first performer is the processor into "I'll do it", with no due
  date. The library's task wording applies by key.
- It is called where an item is made (the plan's build, "Add an item") and where one is edited
  (`update_item`, whose event records `routed`).
- After this, the processor is the only performer without an email, so it is the only one `route_ask`
  rewrites.

**The line on the item:**
- `ConditionItemPublic.route_note` is "Appraisal requests go through the lender". It is set only when the
  appraiser is who the ask goes to (the first performer, as `recipient_for` reads it), so a
  borrower-led ask that merely lists the appraiser does not claim it.
- The sheet shows it under "In lender email". The client's own routing (`itemWhere`) also sends the
  appraiser to the lender email.

**A test pinned the gap.** `test_the_task_wording_comes_from_the_library` re-pointed her invoice task to
"Ask a third party" with herself still the performer, and expected an ask: exactly an ask with no email.
Under the owner's rule that stays her task. The test now asserts that, and asserts separately that an ask
of someone else loses the task wording.

### (a) `ParticipantRole.UNDERWRITER` for the remembered lender address — right, and I changed my mind

I started out thinking this wanted its own role, and the map itself talked me out of it: **`HOA` and
`OTHER_PARTY` already share `ParticipantRole.OTHER`**, so one role serving two draft recipients is the
established shape here, not a new compromise. There is no `ParticipantRole.LENDER` to use, `UNDERWRITER`
is the only lender-side member, and your slot is the **last** of four steps, after the account executive,
any active contact and `lender.contact_email`.

I also checked that the loop actually closes, because a fallback that nothing writes is dead code:
`_remember`'s `add_participant(role=PARTICIPANT_ROLE[draft.recipient])` writes that slot when she types an
address on a lender draft, and `_lender_address` reads it back. It works.

One asymmetry worth a clause in the comment: `DraftRecipient.UNDERWRITER` maps to the same
`ParticipantRole.UNDERWRITER`, so an address typed on a **question to the underwriter** overwrites the
lender draft's remembered address. The flow is one-way — `_underwriter_address` never reads the
participant slot (its chain is a `LenderContact` with role UNDERWRITER, then the assigned underwriter,
then `lender.contact_email`) — so only the lender draft is affected, and only when the lender record has
no contact at all. The existing comment says "read only after the lender's own contacts"; adding "and
shared with the underwriter question's slot, which also writes it" would finish the thought.

### Finding — `_hers` and `recipient_for` disagree about precedence

`reask_destination` and `_add_ask` now share one decision, which closes LP-940's class properly. But that
decision rests on two predicates with different rules: **`_hers` reads `performers[0]`; `recipient_for`
prefers the LO wherever it appears.** Measured:

| item | `_hers` | `recipient_for` | `reask_destination` | `route_ask` leaves the item |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| processor, `i_will_do_it` (the main case) | True | None | `'you'` | her task |
| **`[processor, lo]`, an ask** | **True** | **LO** | **`'you'`** | **an ask, in the LO's email** |
| `[lo, processor]`, an ask | False | LO | `'LO'` | an ask, in the LO's email |
| title, an ask | False | Title/attorney | `'title/attorney'` | an ask |

Row two is the seam: the **original** ask goes in the LO's email, and its **re-ask** becomes her task.
Row three is the same two people in the other order, and its re-ask follows the LO. So the re-ask's
destination diverges from the ask's, and flips on performer order while the original does not.

**This is not LP-940's bug returning** — the card and the created item still agree with each other, which
is what the shared helper guarantees. It is a consistency finding: an item routed to the LO has its
re-ask routed somewhere else.

**The fix is the predicate `route_ask` already uses:** hers means processor-led **and no email**, i.e.
`recipient_for(item) is None and performers[0] is PROCESSOR`. Then her task (the case that matters) is
unchanged, `[processor, lo]`'s re-ask follows its original routing, and three functions share one rule
instead of two that agree in every case anyone has written down. Reachable only through `update_item` —
no library item is multi-performer except DI-01 — so this is a tidy-up, not urgent.

### On building the cross-language `WAITING_ON` check — no, and here is the better fix if you ever want one

Do not put a TypeScript parser in the backend suite. The cost is out of proportion to the risk, for two
reasons I can state rather than assert:

1. **A drift is self-revealing.** The client's map produces a *prediction* ("Becomes Waiting on Lender
   when the lender email is marked sent"); the server's produces the *fact*. When they disagree, the
   sentence she just read turns out wrong in front of her on the next click. That is a visible failure,
   not a silent one — unlike the classes worth heavy guards, where nothing on screen changes.
2. **The duplication itself is the defect, and a mirror test would preserve it.** A test asserting two maps
   match freezes the decision that the client should own a copy of a server rule. If this is ever worth
   closing properly, the fix is for the **server to send the hint** — the item or draft payload carrying
   the `waiting_on` it will set — so the client renders a value instead of recomputing one. Then there is
   one statement of the rule and nothing to keep in step.

So: leave it as the open item, which is what it is — a design note with a known trigger, recorded with the
measurement that the maps currently agree on every entry.
